667. **Audio skips and the floors in the morning build (AU1, queue #398; the director, 13 Oct, playing the morning build at 8fc43b58).**
"So far, I'm getting audio skips. The audio seems buggy. The performance seems a little skippy. The sound on the floor is
still a little odd." D1's #397 (note 666) traces the slow frames; this is the audio's share.
   - **The skips were the frame feeding the speaker.** The app rendered the mix on the frame loop and kept 60 ms queued at
     the device (`FeedSpeaker`), so a frame longer than that ran it dry: every slow frame FrameWatch writes (note 549:
     100 ms and up) was a gap the length of the frame less 60 ms, and the night's first frames (seconds each) were silence.
     Not voice stealing (64 voices; the bench's peaks are well under) and not loads (short takes decoded inline, a few ms
     each, long ones on workers), though both were on the same frame-bound path.
   - **`AudioPump`** (Ballast.Audio) feeds the device from a thread of its own, above the game's in priority, whenever it
     holds less than mix.json's `deviceLead` (60 ms, the frame-fed speaker's latency). The game changes the mix from its own
     thread as before: what the two share is locked where it's kept (`Mixer.Gate` round the voices and each block; a voice's
     `ParamSet`; voice chat's `StreamBuffer`; the `SampleLibrary`'s tables), and a voice's position and level are read a
     block late at worst. In the app nothing decodes where it's played (`InlineBytes` 0): a first play starts a decode's few
     ms late, never stalling a frame or the device.
   - **What it can't ride out** is being kept from running longer than the lead: a collection that stops every managed
     thread, or a machine with no core free. Once a night is past its first frames (`FrameWatch.Window`), each time the
     device runs dry the lead grows 20 ms, up to `deviceLeadMax` (150 ms): a machine that never stalls keeps 60 ms, one that
     does hears a gap or two. FrameWatch's slow-frame lines say "audio ran dry n times" when it did, and the night's summary
     gives the count, the longest block, the longest stall and the lead: the answer to whether a skip lines up with a frame.
   - **Measured.** The app headless (llvmpipe, 1.4 s frames, `SDL_AUDIO_DRIVER=disk` writing what the device played, gaps
     counted as runs of digital silence inside the sound): main, 94% of the night's sound was gaps (four of 1.3-1.4 s in
     6 s); with the pump, none in 30 s, and none in two 4-minute runs at 320×180 (247-252 frames), the pump's longest stall
     58-75 ms on a 4-core container that llvmpipe keeps busy (where .NET ignores thread priority). `AudioPumpTests`: a
     300 ms frame against a 200 ms lead starves a real-time device not at all; the game playing, stealing, stopping and
     moving voices, setting parameters and writing speech while the pump renders flat out; the lead grows only while
     adapting, and no further than its most.
   - **The floors.** `dt audio render --scenario feet` (new): your own steps and a crewmate's half a stride behind, walking,
     over the train: in the cab its boards, the shovelling plate and the gangway's grate in turn; inside a car its boards,
     a crewmate on the roof overhead; on the ballast beside a stopped train. Each step's attack (its blocks within 10 dB of
     its loudest) over the sum of every other stem, in a body band (150-1000 Hz) and a crack band (1-6 kHz). Every surface
     is 9-15 dB over the moving train's bed; the wood floor (the cars' and the cab's) alone has its crack 13 dB under its
     body (-22.7 against -9.1 dB in the cab): a thud with no board in it, the 300 Hz boots note 419 found and left installed
     because the director liked them at build 1121. Now the director hears it "still a little odd", note 419's planks (a
     hard boot on deal: the heel's crack, the board's knock at 0.5-1.7 kHz, the joists' short thump) are the wood floor's
     first choice (`install.py` `SURFACE_CHOICE`, as the roof's tin). Installed: the boards' centre from 317 to 806 Hz,
     their crack 3.5-5 dB under their body (13 before) and 15-17 dB over the moving train inside a car (9-11 before).
     `CrewAudioTests`: the boards' crack within 8 dB of their body (the old boots fail it, 12-15 under), and in the cab and
     inside a car at speed every step's crack 6 dB and more over the train. The plate, grate, tin and ground already read
     as what they are (crack level with body, 9-15 dB over the bed), and stay.
   - **Built in CI.** The recipes need the packs on Drive, which the cloud proxy refuses. `.github/workflows/audio-build.yml`
     builds and installs what an agent's `tools/audio/build_request.json` names (with the checklist's items for its lines,
     `tools/audio/build_store/`) and pushes the installed sounds back to the agent's branch; `install.py --surface` keeps it
     to one surface.
