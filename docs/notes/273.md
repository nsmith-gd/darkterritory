273. **T128, the director's notes on build 1121: falls, where a grab takes you, a player left behind, and the forts (GDD App. F; queue #7).** Four notes, each reproduced or measured headless before it was changed. Built on #189's pressure director (note 266).
    - **"Far too easy to fall off the train."**
      - *Cause.* Nothing held you on a roof. A step with your centre past the roof's 1.5 m half-width put you in the air, and at speed the ground killed you (spec B.3). Walking at roof-run pace while you looked 30–50° aside for under a second, with the key held, was enough. The wind (note 201) pushed you sideways on top of that. Thrown-on-a-bend (note 260) and the Draggers were already warned or telegraphed, so they weren't the cause.
      - *Measured.* `FallTests`' careless walker: a wandering aim, a glance aside every 5–10 s with the key held, a correction only once the edge has been in view for half a second, half the cars at a run, the gaps jumped, wind 1, 16 m/s on R 500 m bends. Over 4 × 300 s it fell **42 times (14 deaths) before and 0 after**. The before figure is the same walker with `edge.lip` 0, measured in the same test. With glances allowed during gap jumps too, it was 54 before and 13 after, all 13 off short jumps.
      - *Fix* (`PlayerMotor.HoldAtEdge`, `player.json` `edge`). On a roof or a coupler plate, a walking step that would bring your feet within `lip` (0.3 m) of where the footing ends is held. Across the car and along it are held separately, so you slide along the edge.
        - You go over a side only if you mean to: walking at it within `stepOffDegrees` (30°) of straight out. The careless walker's deliberate steps went over 7 out of 7.
        - Off a roof's end only by a jump, a ladder, or a step down onto more of the train within `catchDrop` (3.5 m): a coupler plate, a platform, the tender. A car's door steps, a running board and the chassis don't count as a catch. That keeps the bots' step off the end onto the plate (note 231) working.
        - The wind brings you to the lip and no further (`windOverLip` false; true restores the old wind).
        - Jumps, the Draggers' `PullOff`, thrown-on-a-bend and every creature's knock put you in the air, so the hold never sees them.
      - *Spec reading.* Spec B.2's "roof run: wind and balance penalty" is kept as the wind's stronger push while running, which now ends at the lip. The flag records the other reading.
      - Stateless, from the intent and the car shapes alone, so `PlayerState` and the wire are unchanged and prediction re-simulates it exactly.
    - **"A creature carried the director up a mountainside."**
      - *Cause.* The Whistler is the only creature that carries a player away from the train. The Corrupted drag theirs along the cars, and the Gaunt carries bodies away over the ground already. The Whistler ran `nestDistance` (60 m) straight out from its gap at the gap's height. That is past Line Plan §12.6's 40 m walkable corridor (`train.json` `recovery.corridorM`), and the run went through the land. Where the land rose, the carried player was held underground, and when let go `UpdateSupport`'s "the earth, however far up it is" stood them on the hillside.
      - *Fix* (`Whistler.NestSite`). The nest is straight out across the line from the gap. It tries the side the player was taken from first, at `nestDistance` (now 30 m, inside the corridor), shortening by `nestStep` (4 m) to `nestMinDistance` (8 m). The way there must be runnable:
        - no 2 m of it steeper than `nestMaxSlope` (0.4, about 22°);
        - never more than `nestMaxRise` (6 m) above or below the rail;
        - no water (a generated line's `TerrainField.WaterNear`).
        If neither side has such a spot, the nest is at the formation's edge. The carry runs over the ground (`GroundAt` each tick).
      - The run's length goes in the replicated `Extra2`, so `CreatureArt` puts the nest down when the Whistler arrives.
      - `GrabDestinationTests`: on flat land the nest is 30 m out, on the ground. With a 1.5 rise-over-run mountainside on the taken side it goes to the level side, within ±6 m of the rail's height, and the player stands on the ground there. A gentle bank is fine; in a cutting with walls on both sides the nest is at the formation's edge.
    - **"A player left behind should feel the world close in … They needn't die at once."**
      - *Rule* (`Director.Abandoned`, `enemies.json` `director.abandoned`). Once a second, alongside the night's pressure and whatever holds that (the grace, a ban, the caps), each crewmate gets a pressure of their own when they are:
        - alive and on the ground;
        - more than `behindM` (150 m) along the line from the train's nearest end;
        - outside the forts.
      - *Rate.* It builds by tier × the night's conditions term (note 266's `Terms.Conditions`) × (`perSecond` 0.05 + `rampPerSecond` 0.15 × min(1, seconds left / 60) + `perKm` 0.3 × km behind).
      - *Hunts.* Past `threshold` (6) a Ribbit pack comes for them alone. Ribbits fit because App. A.6 has them hop in on whoever they outnumber on the ground, and they can be outrun. The pack is:
        - `pack` 2 the first time, one more each time to 5, and always larger than the group the player is in;
        - put down `spawnOut` 35 m away the first time, closer each time to 18 m, on the line's side the player is on, on the ground and out of the forts.
        Each hunt takes `relief` (6) off. While a pack is still after them, no other comes.
      - Hunts don't come from the budget or count against the caps, and they use their own dice stream (`0xAB4D0`), so the night's own draws are unchanged. Back aboard, back near the train, or in a fort, the pressure is gone. The roster gate holds: an edition without Ribbits sends nothing.
      - `LeftBehindPressureTests`: 400 m behind a train at 12 m/s, the first hunt comes 20–90 s in. A player who gets away from each pack 8 s after it arrives sees at least 4 hunts in 4 minutes, never smaller and never slower to come, and is still alive. The Ribbits sent target only them. Beside the train or aboard, there are no hunts and no pressure.
    - **"Forts must be safe spaces that monsters never enter (§9)."**
      - *Found.* Note 263's `World.SafeYard` holds only while the run is in `RunPhase.Yard`: the departure yard before the gate. It didn't cover:
        - the departure fortress once the train is through the gate, for a crewmate left behind there or a creature chasing them in;
        - the terminus. Its last 500 m is spawn-free (`noSpawnFinal`, plan `terminus_safe`), but anything already about, or riding in aboard the train, could follow the crew in.
        No other stop is a fort. Facilities are freight stops (§16–18), and the holdouts aren't forts.
      - *Fix* (`run.json` `forts`, `World.InFort` / `TrainInFort`). The forts are safe all night: the main line up to the outer gate (the run's yard), and from the terminus gate on (the plan's `Terminus.GateM`, or the run's terminus zone on a hand-laid line; a silent settlement only if its plan keeps the gate safe), out to `halfWidthM` (80 m) either side of the line. While any of the train is in one:
        - the director holds ("in a fort");
        - the Stoker isn't drawn.
        Any creature found inside a fort after the enemies step is dismissed: it lets go and is gone. This covers spawns put down inside one, creatures riding the train in, and creatures chasing a crewmate in. Hazards (the marsh, the Sleepers) and in-car incidents (car fires) aren't creatures and aren't touched.
      - `LeftBehindPressureTests`: the forts end at the gate, start again at the terminus, and stop 80 m out. A crewmate left behind 100 m inside the gate gets no hunts. A pack hopping in after a player at the gate never has a member inside and never grabs them. A train standing in the yard holds the director.
      - *Not done.* The Choir's meter isn't stilled at the terminus. The fort buildings still have no collision (T124).
