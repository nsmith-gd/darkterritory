     - "It should be impossible to have a night with no creatures."

   - **1. The night's floor** (`director.stops.residents`, `floorKinds`; `Director.Floor`):
     - The problem: the night's chances (`nightChance`, note 635) are rolled kind by kind. Seed 1 rolled 22 of the 28 named kinds out, and every creature that lives at a yard but the Followers. Over 5,000 seeds, 15.4 kinds are out on the mean, and 22 or more on 0.5% of nights.
     - After the roll, kinds rolled out are brought back one at a time, each drawn by its own `nightChance`, so the likelier come back first. It continues until two things hold:
       - as many of the stop residents (`residents`: Ribbits, Gaunt, Moose, Soot Children, Followers, Freight Beetle, Grumbler, Mouse Moose, Tower Jaw) are about as wait at a stop on this tier for this crew (below);
       - `floorKinds` (8) of the 22 kinds the director sends (`Spawns.Rules`) are about. A kind `nightChance` doesn't name is about every night, and a chance of 0 (the director's "never") is never brought back.
     - It draws on the roll's own stream after the roll, so the night's other draws are untouched, and a kind the roll left about stays about. `Director.FloorBrought` lists what came back. Seed 1 on the Frontier for a crew of four (a stop's two waiting) had 5 of the 22 kinds the director sends about, and of the residents only the Followers. It gets the Tower Jaw back (two residents), then the Gannet and the Freight Beetle (eight kinds).
   - **2. How many of a stop's own come** (`director.stops.byTier`, `perCrew`, `max`, `boarding`; `StopsTuning.Count`, `Split`):
     - The count is byTier (local 1, frontier 1.5, deadLines 2, deepTerritory 2.5) + `perCrew` (0.35) for each crewmate over one, rounded half up, from 1 to `max` (6). The tier is the night's difficulty (GDD §11: "You aren't picking a difficulty level. You're travelling farther from civilization").
     - The crew is the players in the night now (`Director.Crew`).
     - Of the count, the `boarding` share (0.3, rounded half up, never the only one) board, and the rest wait.
     - Local, a crew of one: 1, waiting. Frontier, two: 2 (1 + 1, as #1294). Frontier, four: 3 (2 + 1). Dead Lines, four: 3. Deep Territory, four: 4 (3 + 1); eight: 5 (3 + 2).
     - Each is a different kind: most rules let one of a kind come at a time. So a stop gets as many as it has kinds whose rule it meets, which is one more reason for the floor.
   - **3. More abandoned villages in the same night** (setpieces.json `deadSettlement.perNight` 2, `leadM` 120; `LineBuilder.Halt`):
     - **Why there were so few** (traced in `LineBuilder.Fill` on local:1 and frontier:2): a 7 km night's main line is mostly fixed zones:
       - the fortress and the threshold, to 1,580 m;
       - each facility's approach, holding and departure, about 843 m, which its yard's zone fills to within 20 m;
       - the alternates' windows and the terminal spike, which take no settlement.
       - What's left is short stretches: the opening, 450–590 m; between two facilities, 570–1,020 m.
       - The every-so-often settlement (`everyKm` 1.5–3 km, counted from the threshold's end) fell due mid-stretch, after a first connector, and rarely with room left. In `dt linegen generate` over local, frontier and deadLines 1–4, only local:4 had one.
     - **First try (dropped): a halt at a stretch's start**, out of the room its hazards, signatures and quotas were owed (`Owed`). It laid 15 settlements on 10 of 12 nights. But the stretch's difficulty budget lost the room it spends on hazards: across the 12 nights the real hazards fell from 31 to 24 (three river crossings, two causeways, a climb, a tunnel). The director's "no fewer hazards" rules that out.
     - **Now: a halt in a filled stretch's recovery** (`LineBuilder.Halt`), **on since the director's "Yes, turn the abandoned villages on" (19 Oct; `ivy-villages-595`, after #1307 merged with it off)** (`perNight` 2):
       - With `perNight` 2, after a stretch is filled exactly as before, its first run of straight recovery connectors (one after another) at least `haltM` (300 m) long becomes a halt, `leadM` (120 m) into the stretch. That keeps it clear of a yard's zone (`PlanStops` wants 100 m) and of the threshold's easement (deepTerritory:6's halt at its first metre had a bow `PlanStops` wouldn't stand a village on). Where it fits, at `townChance`, it's a dead town.
       - A settlement is straight and level, as a recovery is for. It draws on dice of its own (`script/halts`), so the stretches after draw as they did.
     - **Measured with `perNight` 2** (local, frontier and deadLines 1–4, the census's nights):
