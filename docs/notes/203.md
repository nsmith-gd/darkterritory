203. **Bookmarks, the rest: each player's peak in the film, and the night's stills kept on disk (GDD v1.4 App. D.12, D.13, E.5; WP8, note 176's and 177's "not yet").** E.5: "Each player's peak frame is captured as their auto-bookmark (D.12)"; D.12: "A derailment captures one still per crew member from the cinematic, at the peak of their shot."
    - **The peak still.** The host's derailment bookmarks are as they were (one per crew member, their first-person eye: note 176), so nothing new goes on the wire. What changes is the picture each machine takes.
      - `DerailSequence.PeakOf(film, player)` finds the crew member's own shot in the cut and how far into it the film shows their peak (`WreckFilm.Peaks`). The shot's time eases but only runs forward, so it halves towards the moment `FilmShot.At` reaches the peak, clamped to the stretch the shot covers.
      - `BookmarkStills.Due` hands that out as a `DueStill` with its `FilmPeak`: the shot's camera, and the recorded instant.
      - The app draws it the way the film draws that frame (`DerailSequence.Stage`): the film's cars, the crew as ragdolls and nobody else, O2's cutaway and O12's light rig for that camera, none of the live wreck's dust. It's the frame the player sees in the cut at that moment, without the name card.
    - **Reading: the first person stays as the stand-in.** Each client shoots the film off the frame loop (note 177), and a client that never gets the film (it joined after the derail tick) has nothing to take a peak from.
      - So a derailment's still is still taken `bookmarkSeconds` in from the first-person eye.
      - When the film is in, the peak is due at once and replaces the stand-in (`Keep(..., peak: true)`, `IsPeak`). The film is pre-simulated, so its peak doesn't wait for the cut to reach it.
      - A crew member the film has no shot of keeps the stand-in. The cost is at most one extra offscreen frame per crew member, once a night.
    - **Kept past the run end (D.12, D.13).** `BookmarkAlbum` writes each still on the report as a PNG (320x180, as kept), if this machine has it. They go in the user's app data, beside the crash reports and saves: `DarkTerritory/bookmarks/<yyyy-MM-dd HHmm> <line>/`.
      - Each file is named by its number, its run time, its kind and whom it's of: `01 18m24s grab priya.png`, `02 1h02m05s derailment dave.png`, `03 3m20s bookmark dave.png` (the dead's own, named for whom they followed).
      - `night.txt` beside them has the report's lines, each still's file under the line it belongs to, then the dead's own with who took them.
      - The app saves every frame once the report's in. It writes only what's new or changed, so a derailment's stand-in is rewritten when its peak replaces it.
      - A disk that refuses is said once on the console, and the night goes on.
      - The run-end screen says "n STILLS KEPT IN YOUR BOOKMARKS FOLDER" under ENTER: BACK, and the console prints the folder.
    - **Readings.**
        - *Which stills:* the report's: D.13's capped automatic ones and the dead's own. That's what the night showed. A GRAB cut by the cap was never on screen, so it isn't kept.
        - *"Named by night":* the folder is named for when the night began by the player's clock (the sim's run seconds can't say which night) and for the line. A file's time is the run's, as the report prints it, without colons (Windows won't take them).
        - *Presentation only:* the sim never sees a file. The wall clock is only in the folder's name.
    - **Not done:** D.15 question 5's short clips. It's an open question ("may be worth the tech later"), and clips would mean recording frames, not taking a still. There's no setting to turn the album off.
    - `dt screenshot --film s --stills dir` takes each crewmate's peak still from a hosted night's film as the app does, and keeps them the same way. It prints each one's moment against `WreckFilm.Peaks`. `dt screenshot --hud --report [derailed] --stills dir` now keeps the staged report's stills the same way (it used to write `bookmark-n.png`).
