354. **The town's stones underfoot, and the ground's (AU1, queue #91; the director, 8 Oct 2026: "Theres a super weird squishy footstep sound when I walk on the stones in the town"; App. F.1, build 1121: "Footsteps on the ground sound wrong; on wood and grates they're good").** The ground's footsteps, every set off the train, were a boom or a soft wobble a step.
    - **The stones' own steps were soft.** The `cobbles` set (a stop's streets, a halt's platform) and `concrete` (inside a stop's buildings) were built on Kenney's concrete footsteps, which are soft soles: 99% of each step under 1 kHz, a damped 250 Hz wobble at heel and toe. That reads as a squish, not stone. `tools/audio/recipes/crew_feet.py`'s `stone` builds each contact as a hard boot on rock instead: the heel's crack (a few ms of noise from 1.2 kHz up, and a tick), the stone's dead knock (three broad modes at 0.4–2.3 kHz, choked within 20 ms, so no pitch), a 25 ms thump of weight with no ring, and the boot's own sole click. Outdoors there's sand between the setts and no room; indoors the same step has less sand and the short hard room. Walk, run, jump, land and scuff on both are rebuilt and installed, centred 1.8–2.6 kHz where they were 300–390 Hz. On the audio checklist the cobbles and concrete candidates are replaced, not added beside the old ones, since the old ones were what the director heard.
    - **Pinned:** `CrewAudioTests.BootsOnStoneCrackRatherThanSquish` renders the installed walk, run and land on stone and wants as much over 1 kHz as at 100–500 Hz. The soft ones were 7 to 17 dB under.
    - **The rest of the ground boomed.** `dirt` (packed earth, the forest floor) was Kenney's wooden steps lowpassed at 900 Hz under the boot lowpassed: two slow swings of 40–60 Hz at heel and toe, 89% under 250 Hz. `grass` had the boot lowpassed at 700 Hz under its swish, a sub wobble each step. `ballast`'s crunch had no heel (a 200 ms hiss) and its pad of weight (pink noise lowpassed, mostly sub) a boom under it, as did `mud`'s. Now each step's weight is `tup`, a short dead knock at 150–260 Hz, high-passed at 110 Hz and choked within 30 ms. Dirt is that tup with the packs' real crunch thinned and darkened to dry crumbs and needles, grit, and a twig's tick now and then. Grass is its swish with the wobble high-passed out, a rustle of stalks, and a soft tup. Ballast gets a stone heel (a choked knock at 0.4–1.3 kHz) under the crunch, with everything under 90 Hz cut. Mud keeps its squelch and suck, with everything under 80 Hz cut. The boards, grates, plate, roof and coal are the train's and untouched: the director likes the first two.
    - **Pinned:** `CrewAudioTests.TheGroundUnderfootIsAStepNotABoom` wants what's under 120 Hz at least 8 dB under the step (250 Hz–4 kHz) on dirt, grass and ballast, and 3 dB on mud. The old dirt was 7 dB over, grass 3 under, ballast 6 under, mud level; the car's boards are 10 under.
    - **A walled town's streets were the land under them.** B2's walled towns (queue #74, note 335) draw their streets in beaten stones (the `ballast` texture) and their lanes in mud (`ground_mud`) as strips over the ground (`WorldArt.Streets`), but `GroundTexture` didn't know they were there: underfoot was the land beneath, grass or the shore's red mud, so a street of stones played the grass set's soft steps. That's the squish the director heard in the town. `GroundTexture` now takes the stop's town (`Footing.Ground` passes `world.Town`) and, after what's built at the stop, asks `TownWay`: on a street (along it from `S0` to `S1`, within half its width of `D`) the stones; on a lane's strip of mud (the same strips `Streets` draws, from one `LaneMids`, so what's drawn and what's heard can't drift apart) the mud; off them, the land as before.
    - **Pinned:** `CrewAudioTests.AWalledTownsStreetsAreItsStonesUnderfootAndItsLanesItsMud` stands a town of 3000 at a generated stop and walks its streets and a lane through `Footing.Ground`. Without the change the streets were `grass`.
