187. **The switchyard's standing cars and the wreck yard's derailed train (GDD §18; spec D.1, D.2; level-design I.4; WP15b).**
    - **Built on what was there:** the rakes and couplings of `TrainOnLine` (any number of rakes, each on a path; rakes that meet at a crawl couple), the yards' tracks (note 95, level-design P5: every track of a generated yard is a spur off the main line with its own switch), the crates' stowing and the crate hands, the hand lamps in the guard van, and the set pieces' harm-next-tick (note 185). Two more D.2-style modules join `ModuleKind` on the end, `Rakes` and `Wreck`; `facilities.json` `kinds`: the switchyard is `["rakes", "crates"]`, the wreck yard `["wreck", "winch"]` (they keep their crates and winch). The new code is `Run/Yards.cs`.
    - **Switchyard (§18 "mixed. Cars scattered across six sidings. A live track puzzle solved by shouting"):**
      - **Standing rakes at the start of the night** (level-design I.4's deferred "derelict cars"): `TrainOnLine.Stand` adds a rake of cars to a train already built, growing its vehicle, pose and frame arrays; the cars take the ids after every vehicle there is (`OwnVehicles` is where the train's own end). `Run.StandCars`, called from `World.EnableRun` on the host and every client alike, puts `rakes.cars` (1–2) on every track of the yard but its own (on its own if that's all it has), `rakes.back` short of the buffer stop, handbrakes on, each car part-loaded (`rakes.load`) with one of `rakes.cargoes` ("mixed": each pays at its own cargo rate). They're `Vehicle.YardCar` and unlit (nobody's lit their lamps). Only the route goes into it (route.json's 12 m points, never the session's switch tuning), so every machine stands the same cars with the same ids.
      - **Not the train until coupled:** `TrainOnLine.Standing(rake)` is a rake of yard cars alone, on a spur, without the engine. `TrainRakes` counts the train's own rakes (the engine's and its cuts); the bots' "is the train together" tests, the report's lost cars (`Run.Tally`, the clerk's "cars lost" lines) and the winch's car-finding all leave standing rakes out. Coupled up, the cars are the train's: they're in the engine's consist, delivered and paid like any car; cut off again on the main line they're lost like any car.
      - **They come away ahead of the engine:** every yard track's points face up the line (I.4: no trailing points, no run-round), so the engine runs onto the standing cars front first and they're in front of it from then on: the night runs on propelling them. Nothing about the consist assumed the engine was first except `StopPlan` (below); the rest that reads a rake's first vehicle wants its front one, which is what it gets.
      - **Never more than fits:** in all, no more than the shortest of those sidings takes with the engine and `rakes.spare` (1) car of the train's own behind it, since the crew go into each siding with every car already picked up still in front.
      - **Bots:** a switchyard's own track is a stop as before (its crates; and any cars standing on it, coupled up as the engine runs in); each other siding with cars standing is a stop of its own, a *pick-up* (`StopPlan.PickUp`, keyed by its siding so crew mark each done separately). On a pick-up the shunter cuts behind the first car behind the engine and throws that siding's switch; the driver runs in onto the standing cars at a crawl so they couple (`SpurIn` aims at their rear, as `BackOut` aims at a cut), backs out onto the cut, clears the points, and the switch is set back; nobody else gets down. `StopPlan.Ahead` counts the cars ahead of the engine into what goes in, and skips a stop whose spur can't take the engine. Walkers don't climb a standing car to get aboard (the shunter did, and stood on the wrong car's roof all night).
    - **Wreck yard (§18 "salvage, high value. Pull cargo off derailed trains. Unstable, unlit, already occupied"):**
      - **The wreck:** the last train came off at the end of the yard's own line. `wreck.heaps` (3–4) of its cars lie where `wreck.layout` puts them, metres from the buffer stop (positive: through it and beyond) and out on the site's side; each holds `wreck.salvage` (2–3) pieces, crates of cargo `salvage` carried out by hand and stowed as crates are.
      - **Unlit:** there are no lamps (the powerhouse's lamp isn't drawn at a wreck yard either). A heap's salvage is only found, and comes out onto the ground beside it, once a hand lamp is within `lampReach` (6 m) of it, carried or set down, or it's in the engine's headlamp (within `beamLength` 40 m ahead, from 2 m either side widening `beamSpread` 0.2 m a metre). The layout puts two heaps in the beam of an engine run up to the buffer stop and the rest out wide or beside the cars: those need someone to take a lamp to them.
      - **Unstable:** every piece picked up costs its heap `strainPerPiece` (0.4); at none it groans for `warnSeconds` (3 s: the tell, dust shaken off it, and the HUD's "IT'S GOING") and then shifts: everyone within `crushRadius` (3.5 m) takes `damage` (45), death cause `Wreckage`, attributed to whoever pulled the last piece (`Run.WreckBy`); it settles at `settle` and shifts at most `shifts` (2) times. The pieces come out at the heap's near edge, so someone carrying one off towards the cars is clear of it in a couple of steps; someone lingering by it isn't.
      - **Already occupied:** enemies.json `director.residents.wreckYard`: Draggers ×3 and the Gaunt ×2 while stopped there.
      - **Bots:** the crate hands (and the winch pair, once the sleds are in) carry out whatever's been found, as crates (`StopPlan.Loose` takes a piece lying within 60 m of a heap; `CratesToLoad` counts them), and step clear of a groaning heap. ~~They carry no lamps, so they salvage what the headlamp finds.~~ One takes a hand lamp out to the dark heaps (note 492).
    - **Replication:** the standing cars are rakes and vehicles like any (records keyed by id), and each heap is a record of its own (`RecordKind.Heap`: salvage unfound, found, stability, groan, shifts). Protocol 18.
    - **Headless:** `dt facility drill switchyard|wreckyard` (and `--route tier:seed`) report the cars picked up (id, load, cargo), those still standing at that facility, the engine's rake front to back, the heaps, and every stop the driver made. Both default drills leave with the crew alive and the switch back: the switchyard's crates loaded and its siding's cars brought away ahead of the engine; the wreck yard's sleds in and the headlamp's heaps salvaged. `dt screenshot --route r --site --facility switchyard` shows the engine run up a siding to its standing cars; `--facility wreckYard` the heaps from beside the engine at the buffer stop, all but the last found, the first groaning (`--settled`: quiet).
    - **Readings:**
      - "Cars scattered across six sidings" is the yard's other tracks (its generated layout's 1–5 of them, spec D.1), a rake on each, rather than six whatever the yard has.
      - "Assemble them into the train" with every point facing up the line is cars propelled ahead of the engine for the rest of the night. A run-round or trailing points would let them go behind it; that's I.4's engine change, still deferred.
      - "Mixed" cargo is each car its own, from a list (goods, food, timber, machine parts, medicine, coal), part-loaded, rather than the facility's one cargo; the switchyard's crates stay goods.
      - "Unlit" is a rule about finding, not seeing: the sim decides what a lamp has found, so a bot and a player agree on what's there. The crew's lamps are the hand lamps and the engine's headlamp; the cars' own lamps don't reach out of them.
      - "Unstable" is the heap shifting from what's pulled out of it, with a tell first, never at random.
      - A yard track that isn't one of a generated plan's edges is walked at its own limits (none): `PlanRouteWay.Locate` names it `yard<n>` rather than throwing (it threw as soon as an engine went down one).
