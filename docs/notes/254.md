254. **The crew cap, and a full lobby (notes 24 and 253's "not yet"; GDD §1 "Players: 2–8", §15).** Nothing capped the crew: the lobby took 12 Steam members, the LAN list said n/12, and a full game left a joiner waiting.
    - **The cap is `player.json crew.cap` (8).** GDD §1 says 2–8 and §15's largest crew is 8; ARCHITECTURE §8 question 4's "8+" is the net budget's headroom (to 12), not the design.
      - **The reading of "8+": mods.** A mod raises it with a patch (`{"$patch": true, "crew": {"cap": 12}}`, note 53). The content hash means everyone in a game runs the same cap. `CrewCapTests.AModsHigherCapTakesALargerCrew` takes ten. Past 12 is untested.
    - **What counts (`HostSession.Occupied`).** Every place that's someone's:
      - the crew in the world, living and dead (the dead are still crew: the dead channel, the vote, the Holdout queue);
      - those welcomed and waiting to board at a stop;
      - the places held for the dropped (note 253).
    - **Bots count.** A bot holds a crewmate: an id, a body, a part at the stops. So the menu's bots and `--bots` are clamped to cap − 1 when hosting, and the host's own player always has a place. The codebase has no bots giving way to a joining human (T110 only made "no bots" the menu's default), and none is added here: a bot leaving mid-night would take its part (the driver, the gunner) with it. To make room for a friend, host with fewer bots.
    - **Turned away (protocol 27).** Greeting a joiner (note 253's Hello line), the host checks the token first: a held place's token always gets back in, full or not, because its place is already counted. Otherwise, at the cap, the host sends `Refused` (reliable: `RefusalReason.CrewFull`, the crew, the cap) instead of a Welcome. It keeps the link open for `crew.refuseLingerSeconds` (3) so the refusal arrives, then hangs up (`HostSession.Refusals`, `TurnedAway`).
      - The joiner's game (`NetPlaySession.Connect`) stops waiting and the join screen says **CREW FULL (8/8)** (`Refusal.ToString()`), as it says other refusals.
      - A joiner back too late (its place ran out and was taken) is refused the same way. It stops retrying, and its HUD says **CREW FULL (8/8)** in red mid-screen ("YOUR PLACE WAS HELD TILL IT RAN OUT, AND THE CREW'S FULL: [F5] TRY AGAIN") and top right (`LinkInfo.Refused`). F5 tries again.
    - **A slot frees** when a held place runs out (note 253's reserve), or when a player **leaves on purpose**. A joiner's game says `Leave` (protocol 27) as it's closed (`ClientSession.Leave`, from `NetPlaySession.Dispose`). The host then lets the place go at once instead of holding it 180 s. The body stays (spec E), the token is no good, and `HostSession.Leaves` counts it.
      - If the word is lost, the place is held as for any drop.
      - The host takes it either way round: if the hang-up is heard first (the loopback hears it at once), the place already held is let go.
    - **The listings.**
      - The LAN beacon's aboard is now `Occupied`, and its max is the cap. The browser's row shows `n/8`, and at the cap it's greyed `FULL` (`ListedGame.Full`, unchanged).
      - Before, aboard was the HUD's head count less the bots, which counted a Passenger wearing a crewmate's face (App. A.7). That would have given it away to anyone looking at the join list.
      - **The Steam lobby** (`NetPlaySession.Advertise`): `aboard` and the new `max` and `full` values. The member limit is the cap (`IOnlineBackend.SetMemberLimit`, new: Steam's `SetLobbyMemberLimit`). At the cap it's `SetJoinable(false)`, so Steam's search drops it, and an invite or "Join Game" can't get in. A joiner turned away there is told CREW FULL (n/cap) from the lobby's values where the platform shows them, else "the lobby is closed". When a place frees, it opens again.
      - `Lobby.SetJoinable` and `SetMemberLimit` only call the platform on a change. `FakeOnline` keeps the limit and `Doors(id)` shows it to a test.
      - The browser takes the cap from `max` before the member limit.
      - The host's status line says `crew n/8` (FULL at the cap), and the yard's lobby panel says "THE LOBBY: n ABOARD OF 8" and, at the cap, "CREW FULL (8/8): NOBODY ELSE CAN JOIN".
    - **Silent connections take no place.** The greeting line (note 253: connected, not yet said Hello) isn't counted, and since f436200 a quiet greeter is never welcomed until it speaks. So a silent or abandoned connection can't fill the crew or shut the lobby; the link's timeout takes it.
      - The same phantom from the client's side: a UDP client whose host hung up redialled on its own while under `ConnectSeconds` old (`NetPlayTests.ADroppedJoinerReconnectsToItsOwnCrewmate` counted 3 players for 2 at one run here). A client's link to its host, once gone, now stays gone (`DatagramTransport.Drop`), and a late Accept is ignored. Coming back is a new transport, as note 253's redial already makes.
    - **The harness.** With more bots than the cap, the crew is a crew of the cap (its parts, fireman and all). The rest are spare hands with no stop part, turned away at the door. The report gains `crew` (cap, places taken, refusals, and each refused bot with what it was told), and the refused are out of the crew's figures.
      - `dt harness --bots 10 --seconds 120 --route frontier:7 --enemies` (the rough link): `crew` reads cap 8, 8 places taken, 2 refusals, "bot 9 (roof-walker): CREW FULL (8/8)" and bot 10 the same. The eight played on as before: 2.3 km, 45 kbit/s down a client, the worst correction 0.08 m, no deaths.
    - **Determinism.** The refusal happens on the host before anyone's welcomed, and the refused never enter the crew, so `NetcodeTests` is untouched.
