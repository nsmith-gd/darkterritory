Loot should spawn in all yard lines in early game and mid game").** Two things were wrong, and they were separate.
    - **A bare siding.** A yard's stock (level-design P14, stops.json `stock`) was dealt into shed bays picked at random across every shed until the tier's cars' worth ran out, so a siding could stand with nothing beside it: of 40 yards a tier, 3 local, 3 frontier and 3 dead-lines yards had a bare siding (about one yard in thirteen; deep territory's bigger stock left none bare). Now, where the tier's `everyTrack` is set, every yard track has something to load beside its loading face: a crane bay of its own, or a container in a shed it stands beside, on its side of the shed (nearer it than the shed's other track). A track the dice left bare gets a crate stack in the bay of such a shed nearest the middle of its face, on its side (`StopGenerator.StockEveryTrack`). No dice are drawn and the stack is the last container laid, so the rest of the stop (its track, its buildings, what the economy deals into its other containers, its kits and toys) is what it was; `dt site sweep --seeds 30` is unchanged on every tier, band and attempt count alike (the planner fills the empties from what's nearest, so more stock doesn't move the score).
    - **Reading:** "early game and mid game" is the first three tiers (local, frontier, dead lines; `everyTrack` true), and deep territory is left to its dice (false), though its bigger stock left no siding bare in 40 yards. One flag a tier to move.
    - **Pop-in.** A stop's loot (its crate stacks, strongroom, village finds, kits and toys) came out when the train first stopped there (`StepLoot`: under run.json `stopBelowSpeed`, inside its zone), and a facility's own crates when the train first stopped on its spur (`StepLoading`): in front of whoever was looking. Now both come out once the engine is on the main line within loot.json `stockAhead` (800 m) of the stop's zone, whatever its speed: further out than enemies.json `interestRadius` (520 m, the furthest a client is ever sent a body from), the lamp and the fog, so nobody sees them appear. 0 puts back the old rule. A stop passed at speed keeps its loot lying out; asleep and outside everyone's interest, it costs neither physics nor bandwidth.
