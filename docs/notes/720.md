720. **Voices: the speaking tube, the whistle codes, singing together, lamp signals (K1, queue #454; the director's calls of 14 Oct 2026, `docs/design/friendslop.md`).**
The audit's Friends goal ("It's extremely important that friends exceeds our target meaningfully") leans on the voice being a tool, not only a channel. The director approved the four, and ruled: "Singing does not fight the choir."

   - **What it was:**
     - Voice already acted on the world: the Choir takes the loudest, the Gaunt is talked down, the Passenger never speaks.
     - The cab and the guard van had no way to talk but the radio, which needs a set and dies in tunnels.
     - A crew singing was only loud, and fed the Choir like shouting.
     - A crewmate with no microphone couldn't tell the bot driver anything.
   - **Now:**
     - **The speaking tube** (`Sim.Train.SpeakingTube`, `VoicePath.Tube`; player.json `voices.tube`):
       - A mouthpiece in the cab on its left wall, 1 m behind the coal bunker (a step behind the hatch ladder), and one in the guard van's front right corner. The cab's is measured from the bunker, so it stays by the footplate whatever #450 does to the cab behind it.
       - Said within `mouthReach` (1 m, across the floor, stood in that car) of one, the host also sends it out of the other's horn to whoever's within `heardWithin` (10 m). The crew near the speaker hear them as ever. However long the train is; a van cut off breaks it.
       - The ends are numbered (`Ends`) and joined in pairs (`Tubes`): U1's speaking-tubes upgrade (PR #967) adds ends and pairs, nothing else.
       - The client plays it from the far horn on its own stream, `voice-tube` (new): 600 Hz–2 kHz, a +8 dB ring at 1150 Hz, no static, 1.5–10 m falloff.
       - Art (`Art/VoiceKit.TubeEnd`, `Tag`): a brass horn with a rolled lip and a whistle-plug on a chain, its pipe down the wall into the floor (up, it crossed the cab's window slit), and an enamel tag beside it, TO THE VAN or TO THE CAB (`content/ui/speaking-tube.json`, by end).
     - **The whistle codes** (`Art/VoiceKit.WhistleCard`, `content/ui/whistle-card.json`):
       - An enamel card on the driver's side wall beside the cord, under the window slit: ONE LONG - STOPPING, TWO LONG - GOING, THREE SHORT - BACKING, FOUR SHORT - HELP, TWO SHORT - HEARD YOU.
       - Only a card. The cord doesn't report pull lengths (it blows while it's held), so the bots don't answer codes.
     - **Singing together** (`Sim.Player.Singing`, `World.Singers`; player.json `voices.singing`):
       - On the host, from the voice level every intent carries. Two living crewmates within `earshot` (12 m) are singing when, over `windowSeconds` (4 s), each averages over `floor` (40/255), each moves by at least `swing` (12, its spread: a drone isn't a song), and their envelopes' correlation is at least `inTime` (0.6). Integer sums, compared without a root.
       - `feedsChoir` false: singers' voices don't feed the loudness meter or their Choir shares. Talk out of time feeds it as ever. They still count as a voice raised for the night's first threat's draw (note 287): a song is still a sound.
       - Livestock aboard settle by `calmsLivestock` (0.6): the meter's livestock floor, and the hounds' livestock weight over 1, are eased by that share (`World.LivestockCalm`).
       - The Gaunt: a singer within its listening radius makes talk count `gaunt.singingTalks` times over (enemies.json, 3), anger and calm alike. Sung off, the report says "Sang the Gaunt off ... Singing: Dave, Dunmore." (`IncidentKind.Sang`, ranked with a rescue).
       - Nothing on the HUD. A townsman's line hints at it.
     - **Lamp signals** (`LampSignal`, `SignalEvent`, `World.Signals`, `ConductorBot.Signalled`; player.json `voices.lampSignals`):
       - With a hand lamp in the fist, hold LAMP SIGNAL (U, rebindable) and lean the mouse (`SignalWheel`, shaped like the emote wheel so #456's wheel can host it): GO up, STOP left, BACK UP right. The corner says "SIGNAL : HOLD [U]" with a lamp in hand.
       - Intent like an emote: two bits of the intent's third byte, sent once. The host keeps a `RecordKind.Signal` record for `seconds` (3), one per crewmate, `cooldown` 0.5 s. It moves nothing by itself.
       - Drawn: the lamp hand by IK over the lantern clip (`SceneArt.LampSignalHand`), a 1.2 s beat: swung across at arm's length, raised and lowered, or circled. The lamp and its light go with the hand.
       - The bot driver reads one shown within `seenWithin` (60 m) of the cab or the last car's rear. STOP: brakes to a stand and stands up to `standSeconds` (90), or till GO. GO: the reverser forward at a stand if it's back, then its own driving. BACK UP: to a stand, reverser back, `backMetres` (40) at `backSpeed` (1.5 m/s), then stands (and after `standSeconds`, forward again).
       - A STOP shown to a moving train that stands within `stoppedWithin` (40 s): "Stopped by a lamp signal ... Signalled by {actor}." (`IncidentKind.Signalled`).
     - **Protocol 59**: a voice frame's path is two bytes (the tube's the ninth flag) with the tube's end after it; the lamp signal in the intent's third byte, bits 2–3; `RecordKind.Signal`.
   - **GDD first:** §12 "The cab talks to the rest of the train", §14 "Singing is not noise", §32's corner line, App. C.7 and C.8.
