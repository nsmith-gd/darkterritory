251. **`dt film`: the whole derailment as a video, and what watching it found (GDD v1.4 App. E.1, E.3-E.6; notes 170, 174, 177).** `dt screenshot --film` (note 177) rendered one frame of the cut, a process a frame, and none of the first person or the replay. The demo's derailment is "the clip the demo will be shared for" (§23), so it wants watching end to end.
    - **The command** (`src/DarkTerritory.Cli/FilmCommands.cs`): `dt film [--crew 8] [--fps 24] [--speed 20] [--route frontier:7] [--cars 6] [--width 1280 --height 720] [--track id] [--out out/film]`. A hosted night with the crew aboard (the rest bots) runs `--seconds` (40), held at `--speed` so it's out of the fortress's yard and running when it comes off, and is derailed once. It's then stepped tick by tick through the first person, the replay, the film's cut and the cause card, each frame drawn at `--fps` to `frame_NNNN.png` with the HUD as the app draws it (480x270 canvas, scaled), the cards and the skip prompt included.
      - **One pick, two callers.** The beat, the cars as drawn, the camera and what the scene shows were inline in the app's loop; they're now `DerailSequence.Show` (a `DerailShot`) and `DerailSequence.Dress`, which the app and `dt film` both call. `DerailSequence.Timeline` is the sequence beat by beat (the first person, the replay, each of the cut's shots), which the JSON's shot list and the test read.
      - **The sound** is the game's own mixer offline: a `GameAudio` driven tick by tick as the app drives it (the listener at the camera, the opera at `WreckSeconds`, faded under the cause card), rendered on in step with the sequence, to `derailment.wav` with a spectrogram beside it.
      - **Encoded** to `derailment.mp4` (H.264 CRF 23, AAC 160k) and a 16-frame `contact.png`, with `--ffmpeg`, the PATH's ffmpeg, or the static one `pip install imageio-ffmpeg` bundles (the cloud sessions have no ffmpeg).
      - **The JSON**: frames, seconds, the shot list (beat, subject, card, sequence and recorded times, the camera's distance and elevation), the music (track, in-point, when it starts, when its hit lands, when it fades and when it runs out), and for each crewmate over their own shot: the share of it they're in the central 70% of the frame (O6), their height in it, their clearance off what's under them, the share of it airborne and the most they're tipped.
    - **Fixed, from watching it.**
      - *The replay lost the train.* `ReplayCamera` meant to ride with the train until it came off, then hold; a reference test that could never pass held it from the replay's first frame, so the train ran in from behind the camera and the pile-up ploughed off into the fog. It now rides (`off` false: the replay frame is from before the derail) and, once off, keeps its offset from the middle of the train as it ploughs on (its travel, not the cars' tumbling).
      - *O12's fog.* "Fog pushed out to at least twice the shot distance" wasn't applied to the film: its settle wide was a smudge. `DerailSequence.Fog` applies `Views.CinematicFog` at the shot's distance in the film, and in the replay (its chase view is 80 m off the middle of the train).
      - *Crewmates inside fell through the floor.* The hollow car kept a ragdoll above the box's foot, the rails, so anyone in a car dropped 1.1 m to the track and lay there under the cut-away car. `FilmCar.Floor` (the car's interior floor, from its shape) is now the floor (`World.Derail` passes it to `WreckFilm.StartOf`).
      - *The peak wasn't a flight.* E.5's peak read the pelvis over the ground: anyone stood up was "in the air" (0.95 m against a 0.6 m threshold) and a roof rider was flying from the first frame, so roof riders always got the last, biggest shot for lying on a roof. `WreckFilm.Clearance` is the lowest joint off whatever's under it (the ground, or the floor or roof of a car it's over, in the car's frame); airborne is over `AirborneAbove` (0.3 m).
      - *The cause card ran off the screen.* Its wrap measured a glyph without its spacing pixel, a third too many characters a line; the radio card the same.
    - **Also (E.6's "not done"s in note 174):**
      - *Half speed.* Main's sound package (note 236, `mix.json music.gameRate`) already slows the game under the opera; this branch's own copy of it was dropped in the merge. `dt film` renders through it.
      - *The film's wreck is heard.* `FilmFrame.Impacts` were recorded "for the hits that made a sound" and nothing played them. `GameAudio.Film` plays the hardest hit in each stretch of recording the shot plays through (a shot that revisits a moment hits it again, from its own angle) and the grind of the fastest recorded car; the live wreck goes quiet meanwhile.
    - **Not fixed (findings, in tuning or bigger than a fix):**
      - *The opera runs out.* The five synthesised tracks are 21-24 s; from the replay's start at crew 8 the sequence needs about 28 s more (to 32 s), so the music stops near 26 s and the settle and the cause card play in near silence. The real recordings in PR #150 (45 s windows) would cover it; otherwise `dt audio opera`'s arrangements want to be ~35 s.
      - *The cause card is 2 s* (E.5) for a 130-character line typed over 70% of it: legible on a still, unreadable at speed. A `causeSeconds` of about 5, or the line in two cards, would read.
      - *Five of eight are in cars,* and with E.3's doorway gaps not in yet they tumble inside a car that's cut away whole, which reads as lying on the track. Doorways (or a cutaway that keeps the floor) are what would make them fly.
      - *Small in frame.* Subjects fill 5-40% of the frame's height, mostly under O6's 25%; the director's 7 m at 45° is a long way off a body lying down.
      - *The replay's tail is still.* At 20 m/s the cars are at rest a few seconds after coming off, and the replay holds 9 s.
    - **Tests:** `FilmPlaybackTests.TheWholeSequenceRunsFirstPersonReplayThenTheCutShotByShot` (the timeline, the frame count at 4 fps, each frame's beat and shot) and `ACrewmateInsideStandsOnTheFloorAndARoofRiderIsNotFlyingTillThrown`.
