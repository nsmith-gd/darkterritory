1057. **The towns' and the dykes' leftovers (Uma, nathaniel3; queue #761; level-design T.4, L.3; maritime-rules §4; note 1031's "Not done"; note 1050's rank 11).** *In progress* (branch `uma-dykes-761`).
   - **Before.** A dykeland town's stages were thin plank decks on posts 3.5 m apart, its stone houses sitting flush on them; a round could walk a townsperson straight over a terrace's or a bench's wall (`Town.Clear` walks through the ground's own walls); the dykes had no sluices, the dyke's bank was drawn as a smear between land columns 15-22 m apart, and the line never ran on the dyke itself; a hand-laid line's trees and leavings were only drawn; L.3's finds, bodies and porches as below. The World Building board had no open note or culture (26 Oct).
   - **Built** (each deterministic, from the plan or the night's seed):
     - **Stilts under the dykeland's houses** (Game `WorldArt.FormGround`; `StiltEvery` 2.2 m, `StiltHalf` 0.18 m): round each stage, stout posts down into the mud, a girt along them 0.35 m over the water and every other bay braced corner to corner; only where the stage looks out over open water (where a walk or the next stage meets it it's out of sight), so the town's ground grows by a few thousand triangles, not the 230,000 a post round every stage's whole outline cost. The boardwalks' own piles are as they were. `--town stilts` frames the nearest house over the water from beside it, low.
     - **The rounds kept off a bench's or a terrace's wall** (Sim `TownRounds.OnFoot`): a place a townsperson walks to out of doors, and the last step from a bench's approach onto its seat, is taken only if the ground along the way steps no more than a stride (`TownGround.Stride`, 0.7 m) anywhere; else they go elsewhere. On main, 5 of the 20 town types and sizes `TownRoundsGroundTests` checks had rounds over a wall (the furnace's and the dykeland's both sizes, the salt town's: 2 to 44 ways each); none now.
     - **Street folk facing a turned house** was already built (`TownGenerator`'s lamp-carriers by `TownHouse.Body`/`BodyFacing`, in front of the house as it stands turned); `TownRoundsGroundTests.TheStreetFolkFaceOutFromATurnedHousesFront` pins it in a harbour and a crag town.
     - **Aboiteaux** (Sim `LinesideProps.Aboiteaux`, stream `lineside-aboiteau`; pieces `aboiteauGate`, `aboiteauIntake`, `MaritimeKit.AboiteauGate`/`AboiteauIntake`, 98 and 106 triangles; footprints.json written by `dt art footprints --write`): along a dyke shore, at four places in five every 260 m, the sluice's gate on the dyke's seaward toe (a plank headwall and wings, the box's mouth and its clapper hung a hand open, the wet channel out) and its intake on the landward toe (a lower headwall, the open mouth and the sluice board on its frame with the screw and wheel). The box runs under the bank, so the crest is walked over. The two stand together or not at all (`LinesideProps.Props`: a board's sight line, a leaving or a building that keeps one out keeps both). **The dyke's bank drawn true** (Game `PlanScene.LakeBands`): a dyke's own band of the land's finer columns (6 m) from 6 m short of its landward toe to 6 m past its seaward one, as a lake's is; before, it was a smear from the fields at 45 m to the marsh at 78 m, over the gate.
     - **The Tantramar grade as the dyke** (Sim `LineBuilder.Waterside`, stream `dyke-grade`; tiers.json `dykes.onGrade` 0.35; `PlanShore.OnGrade`; `TerrainField.Shore`): a share of dyke shores have no dyke of their own: the line's own bank holds the tide, the salt marsh right under it on the water's side and the fields on the other. Their aboiteaux run under the track, the gate 9 m out at the bank's seaward foot and the intake 9 m out on the fields' side. Frontier:44's dyke (5230-5880 m) is on the grade now.
