262. **The demo's creatures, higher poly and textured denser, and their clips checked for parts through parts (the Look Review: "some things are coming out looking weird... continue working on better models"; "go ahead with the creatures animation pass and updated higher poly model & texture pass").** The Track Doll, Car Hugger, Whistler, Tippy Toesie, Ribbits and the Choir.
    - **More triangles alone didn't show.** `rig.densify` (a Catmull-Clark level with the hard edges creased, collapsed back to the part's share of `kit.target_tris`) took the Ribbit from 3.8k to 7.4k triangles, and close up its before and after were the same picture. What read as low quality was how the kit builds a body: limbs are tubes pushed into a trunk, with a seam and often a gap at every hip and shoulder, and a head of stacked discs.
    - **`rig.fuse`: one skin from several parts.** This is the crew body's method (`crewbody.py`, note 145), now for any creature:
      - `kit.fuse(name, parts, voxel, faces, cut)` welds the parts and fills their holes (a tube's UV seam is a column of doubled vertices and its ends are open, so without this the remesh wraps both sides of every surface). It voxel-remeshes them into one surface, keeps only the outward shells, relaxes it, and retopologises it to the game mesh.
      - Each new face takes the material of the nearest part face. Each vertex takes the bone weights of the nearest point on the parts, interpolated over that face, then relaxed over the skin's edges, at most four bones a vertex.
      - `cut(point)` opens the skin again where it was meant to be open (the Car Hugger's mouth). It cuts the dense union and then the game mesh, and QuadriFlow is told to keep the edge.
      - Fine parts stay out of the fused skin: hands, fingers, eyes, teeth, lips, insect legs. A voxel union melts them, and QuadriFlow drops them.
    - **QuadriFlow, checked.** It drops limbs thinner than its quads (Tippy's shin went), refuses a mesh with any edge under 0.1 mm a side, and isn't the same run to run (one Whistler union gave 1,576 good quads one run and 683 missing a third of the body the next). So `fuse` tries it with seeds 0 to 3, symmetric across x. It measures each result against the union and keeps the first that leaves at most 1.5% of the union further than 4 voxels (8 mm at least) from it. Otherwise it collapses the union to the same count, which never loses a form but spends its triangles less evenly. The build log says which it took. **Not deterministic:** a rebuild can give a slightly different mesh. The budgets, the clearance check and the renders are what's pinned.
    - **What changed, per creature (triangles; GDD §27):**
      - **Ribbit 3.8k → 7.6k.** Body and legs are one skin, so the haunches and the legs grow out of the flanks. The head is remodelled as a toad's flat wedge (`SKULL` sections) with a rolled lip over the gums. The eye mounds, the paratoid glands, the nostrils and the lids are one fused skull skin. The eyes are cloudy grey-green with a toad's flat-bar pupil. The ears are pinched at the root and cupped along their length. The jaw is a deeper scoop, and the paws stay a separate piece.
      - **Tippy Toesie 4.4k → 8.5k.** The neck, torso, arms and legs are one skin, with knees, calves and thighs under it. The hands are a separate piece, given knuckles and fingers that curl and spread.
      - **Car Hugger 13.5k (unchanged).** The hide and arms are one skin with the mouth cut open again, and the hands are a separate piece. The gap between the lip ring and the body's rim is gone.
      - **Whistler 8.5k.** The head's wedge and the siphon are fused with the segmented body. Its legs have a coxa knuckle, a swollen femur with a ridge and a spine (two on the forelegs), and banded tibias.
      - **Track Doll 9.2k and Choir 2.9k** are densified only. The doll is jointed porcelain, so its seams are meant.
    - **Textures.**
      - `overbake.Atlas.unwrap` now uses smart-project at 75 degrees with tight margins, which fills 54–56% of each atlas instead of 38–55%.
      - The Car Hugger bakes to 2048. It wraps most of a car (38 m² of skin), and at 1024 it came out about 120 px/m.
      - **The renderer has a 2048 hero tier** for layers authored above 1024 (`look.json` `bigHeroLayerSize`). The scene set gains bindings 11–13, and a hero slot from 64 up samples the big arrays (`scene.frag`).
      - Texel density, against GDD §27's hero target of 256–512 px/m: Ribbit 434, Tippy 476, Choir 499, Track Doll 312, Whistler 282, Car Hugger 247. The Car Hugger is about 4% short; its belly and the inside of its funnel take a share of the atlas they don't need.
    - **A distance copy for each, so the frame stays in budget.** At their full budgets the six creatures put the headset's trackside view at 1.57M triangles a frame, over `perf.json`'s 1.5M, because each is drawn for two eyes and the shadows (`PerfBudgetTests`). The precedent (note 102) was to trim the models to fit. Instead:
      - `overbake.finish(lod=0.4)` also exports `<name>.lod1.glb` (`rig.export_lod`): every part of 400 triangles or more collapsed to two fifths, symmetric, on the same rig, clips, atlas and materials.
      - `CreatureArt` loads it with its joints renumbered by name to the full model's. Past `look.json`'s `creatureLodMetres` (14 m) from the eye it draws the copy, posed with the full model's palette. The scene is built about the eye, so a creature's distance is its matrix's origin.
      - Up close, as near as one comes to anyone, it's the whole model. `dt art clip` and the reel draw at the origin, so they always show the full model.
      - `CreatureArtTests.TheDemoCreaturesHaveADistanceCopy` checks each copy: a quarter to three fifths of the triangles, the same materials, and every joint one of the full model's.
    - **`Clearance.Mesh`: any model's clips checked for parts through parts, from its own mesh.**
      - Each bone with 24 or more vertices carried mostly by it becomes a capsule fitted to them: along their longest spread, from the 10th to the 90th percentile of it, its radius their median distance off that line, so the capsule sits inside the skin.
      - Every pair of bones three or more joints apart is measured at 30 fps. The finding is how much deeper the pair overlaps than it does at rest.
      - Bones a clip scales are left out (the throat sac, the Car Hugger's rings): they're meant to swell into their neighbours.
      - `dt art clearance --only <model>` uses it for anything but the crew's figures (`--mesh` forces it). `CreatureArtTests.TheDemoCreaturesStayOutOfThemselves` fails any of the six deeper than `Clearance.Touching` (6 cm).
    - **What it found, all fixed:**
      - The Car Hugger's left upper arm, folded in lurk, went 0.29 m into its body and across the mouth. The mouth sits over to that side, so that hand's fold target was inside the lip; it now folds down beside it.
      - The Track Doll's hand "at its chest" in admire was at its cheek, and went 0.11 m into the head as it snapped round. The hand is now at the chest.
      - The Whistler's coil went through itself (0.14 m) in fold. It's wound a little looser and its front rides up over the tail's turn, still under the plate (`WhistlerTests`, 1.05 m).
      - The Choir's rim curled through its curtain on a hit.
      - The Ribbit's jaw went into its chest in devour, and an ear swung into its flank on a hit.
    - **Verified:** CreatureArtTests (budgets, loops, the crew's and the creatures' clearance), WhistlerTests, RibbitTests and TippyToesieTests. Each creature was looked at before and after, close up and in its clips (`dt art clip`), and in the Look Review's reel.
