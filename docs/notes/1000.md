## The gaps

1. **Aboard at speed, inside the cars.** Most of what acts on a moving train acts on the *outside*: the rear, the gaps,
   the edges, the roofs, the couplings, the trucks. A crew inside a lit, shut car (note 633 keeps the Climbers out) is
   safe from everything but the Fire Flies, the Stoker's car and the Passenger. The director felt exactly this (line 28).
2. **A chaser that outpaces a walk.** Nothing afoot is faster than a walk except the Moose's charge (in straight lines)
   and the Lodger's lunge (near its house). Ribbits are outrun at a walk (line 25; their pace is #702's).
3. **A halt in the wilderness.** A stand outside any stop (a red bend, a Hotbox pulled, a Knotter killed) brings only
   what boards at a stand (Whistler, Climbers). Nothing comes *out of the woods* for a standing train (line 28).
4. **Pressure at every stop, by number.** Each stop has its own creatures (yards the Pickers, villages the houses), each
   one at a time; nothing is a crowd that rises the longer you stand (line 27; `n1-C2-dread-at-the-stops`'s timer would
   bring one more of the same).
5. **A counter to turtling in a car.** See 1: the safe car has no answer.
6. **A counter to sprinting off alone.** Tippy Toesie (standing still) and the Ribbits (outnumbered) are the only ones,
   and both are beaten by keeping moving.
7. **A counter to one player hogging the cab.** The Track Doll and the Stoker press an *empty* cab or a hot fire; a
   driver who never leaves the regulator is never pressed for it.
8. **Looting alone** (Rook's table): the house creatures are the houses' own; nothing the director sends finds a
   looter inside a house.
9. **Composition.** Every creature acts on its own rule; nothing ties several together at one player (line 35, "target
   players specifically").

Each has its silhouette, its tell (spec A.3/A.4, a band checked against the allocation table), its rule as an App. A.4
tree, aboard or afoot, what it counters, its compositions, early versus late, the social test and a build cost. All nine
pass at least five of §20's six with 1 and 3; the Bellman's 5 ("someone gets blamed") is partial.

**Compositions the set makes** (the bind each pair puts the crew in):
- **Spread out vs. bunch up:** the Close (aboard, not all in one car) against the Lurcher and Ribbits (afoot, never alone)
  and the Climbers (outnumber them at the gaps).
- **High vs. low:** the Rodrider (off the floor) against the Drops (off the roof) and the Gannet.
- **Lit vs. dark:** the Pollards (keep a lamp on them) against the Fire Flies (lamps off).
- **Stand vs. go:** the Stragglers and the Pollards (go) against the Hotbox and the Knotter (stop to fix).
- **Who goes out:** the Blinder (someone else, on the running board) against the Track Doll (the empty cab) and the
  Gannet (the running board in open country).
- **In or out:** the Nailer (get out of the house) against the Lurcher (don't cross the open alone) and the house
  creatures.
- **The conductor:** the Bellman turns whatever's about onto one crewmate.

**Early versus late.** Early nights (Local): the Lurcher, a small Stragglers wave, the Pollards after 40 s. Frontier adds
the Rodrider, the Blinder, the Close, the Drops and the Nailer. Dead Lines on: the Bellman, and every one at its late numbers (six
Stragglers at a time, both sides of the Pollards), which is the director's "a few monsters per POI" early and "six or seven
at a time" later (line 29).
