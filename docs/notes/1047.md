1047. **Decoded texture pixels let go once they're on the GPU (Odile, nathaniel3's Token Budget Manager's worker; queue #747; note 1043's memory finding).** *In review: branch `odile-pixels-747`.* The look and the renderer each kept every decoded texture for the run after it was uploaded: about 1.6 GB of live heap in any process that dressed a renderer (the app, `dt screenshot`, the tests, the tell audit), sitting on the RSS when CI's Linux runner was OOM-killed (note 1043; #1846 put the tests at two modules at a time).
   - **Who held the pixels.** Three places, all holding the same `Image`s:
     - `Look`'s static decode cache (`DecodedFiles`, note 367), so every look in a process shared one decoding. It held strong references for the whole run.
     - `Look._assets`, the `RenderAssets` it dressed renderers from.
     - `GreyboxRenderer._assets`, and the key of its shared material arrays (`MaterialKey`, note 804), which holds the layer list the arrays were made from.
   - **Who reads them after the upload.** Only the renderer, and only to upload again:
     - a `Load` that can't share arrays already on its device: another `GpuContext` (VR's eyes share theirs; the tell audit's budgeted context and a test's second device don't), or the app's renderer remade at a new resolution (the old one released them first);
     - the backdrop, uploaded on every `Load` and on `LoadBackdrop` (`DressSky`).
     Nothing else reads the layers' pixels: `HeroLayers`, `LayerOf` and the frame's layer count use names and widths, and the art (`CreatureArt`, `PropArt`, the lookbook) reads models, not the look's textures. The look doesn't hot-reload its textures (note 687's hot reload is the data files and audio). A new `Look.Load` reads them again, as before.
   - **What changed:**
     - **Outlines** (`Ballast.Render`, `RenderAssets.cs`): `Image.Outline(w, h)` is an image's size with no pixels (`HasPixels` false). `MaterialLayer.Outlined()` makes one of a layer. `RenderAssets.Outlined(reread)` gives the layers as outlines with no backdrop, plus `Reread`, which reads the pixels again in the layers' order.
     - **The renderer** keeps only outlines in `_assets`, and no backdrop (`LoadBackdrop` uploads the sky it's given and drops it). On a miss in the shared arrays it calls `Reread` (`Pixels`) for the upload alone. A hit (a second renderer on the device, or the app's per-night `Dress` with a new sky) reads nothing. Assets given as pixels, with no `Reread`, still upload as they are; the renderer keeps their outlines, not them.
     - **The look** makes its outlines on the first `Dress` (`Assets()` decoded once, held until the upload is done), with `Layers()` as their `Reread`. It reads its own backdrop (the sky texture) when it dresses (`Backdrop()`), for `Dress` and `DressSky` alike. The decode cache holds weak references: shared while something uses the images (an upload, two looks dressing at once, as the tests do), collected once they're all on the GPU. A file changed on disk is still read again. `Assets()` (the decoded pixels) is still there for whoever wants them, and it's theirs to hold.

     - **What it means.** About 1.6 GB of live heap is gone from every process that dressed a renderer, for its whole life after the upload. The two peaks that don't move:
       - **The upload.** For a moment the pixels, every array's mip chains and lavapipe's copy of the arrays are all in memory together. That's each `dt screenshot`'s peak, and the probe's.
       - **The tell audit's peak with the heap uncapped.** On a roomy box the GC lets garbage pile up to about the same height whatever's live. Capped, where the GC has to collect, the same run peaks 0.7-1.2 GB lower. The trough between tells is 1.65 GB lower.
     - Under a 2 GiB cap this branch fails at the upload (the decoded textures alone are 1.6 GB). That's the floor now, not the run.
   - **CI's `--max-parallel-test-modules 2`.** Every GPU test process now sits about 1.6 GB lower for its whole run, and the GC collects harder under the runner's memory pressure, which is what the capped runs show. But the upload's peak (about 5.5 GB at full arrays) is unchanged, so two GPU modules dressing at the same moment still reach what they did. On these numbers I'd keep 2 for now. **3 is worth one trial run** (Game.Tests, Dev.Tests and a non-GPU module at once fit easily now, apart from coinciding uploads). Going higher would want the upload's peak cut too (below). The workflow is unchanged here.
