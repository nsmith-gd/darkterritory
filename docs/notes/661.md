661. **The buffer stop strand: a buffer stop run into dents the engine, it doesn't break it down (D1.4, queue #392; the director, 13 Oct, playing the morning build: "it looks like maybe I hit the stop at the end of the spur too hard or too fast").** At Voss Green elevator on frontier:7 the director ran up the facility's spur into its buffer stop; the engine broke down there and the night was stranded.
    - **Why it broke down.** A branch's buffer stop (one that doesn't rejoin: a facility's spur, a dead line, a yard track) took the front vehicle's shell at a collision's rate, `couplings`: (speed − 1 m/s)² × 0.06. The engine's shell is 1. At 4 m/s that's 0.54 (failing, `failing.below` 0.5), at 5 m/s 0.96, at 5.3 m/s all of it: broken down, and at a stand the night ends stranded (note 576). A facility's spur has no warning ahead of its end (the cab's only for a dead line's, note 286), and the stop drivers set back at up to 6 m/s (`StopDriver.SetBackTop`).
    - **The buffer stop's own knock** (train.json `bufferStop`, `TrainOnLine.HitBufferStop`): over `safeSpeed` 2.5 m/s (the yard's crawl) the front vehicle takes (speed − 2.5)² × `damagePerSpeedSquared` 0.02, and the cargo `couplings.cargoDamageShare` of that, as before. 5 m/s is 0.125; 6 m/s 0.245; a dead line's 25 km/h (`overspeed.deadEndDerailAbove`, 6.9 m/s, past which the train goes through the buffers and off the end) 0.39: a dent the wrench mends, short of failing. 9 m/s is 0.85. Not in the spec (GDD App. A.7 only says "a hit short of the lead is the buffer stop's damage alone"): a first pass, a tuning value for the director.
