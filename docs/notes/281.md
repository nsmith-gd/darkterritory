281. **Fortress towns, first pass: a custom each, people who talk, papers to read, a square that's solid (the director's direction of 6 Oct 2026, GDD App. F.1 T133; GDD §3.1).** The direction: "fortress towns are where the world is built ... procedural, with people in them. Each town has its own odd culture, different from the last. Learn by inference, as in Lethal Company. Little notes, and text-only lines from the townspeople ... Much more in the towns is interactable." Before this, the town was art (note 107): houses you walked through, idle drab folk, nothing to read; the plan's fortress `Identity` was unused.
    - **The writing** (`content/world/towns.json`, `Sim/Towns/TownContent.cs`). Fourteen customs, each the human answer to one creature's rule (its `creature` by enemies.json `costs` names, "choir" always about), with a law, a hall, a centrepiece, six lines and three notes; eleven habits (`quirks`); three trades (`industries`: pit, growing, foundry, the linegen fortress identities) with their hands' lines and stalls; twelve jobs (`roles`) and an `anyone` pool; six threads of mystery (the fall, the lost trains, the dead lines, the penal cars, Dispatch, the fees); generic notices; knocks at the three doors; the gatekeeper's `welcome` with `{law}`. Placeholders `{town}`, `{other}`, `{hall}`, `{law}`, `{n}`, `{name}`, `{name2}` (a person in the same town, so papers and lines point at each other). Written in sentence case, drawn in the HUD's capitals.
    - **The numbers** (`content/tuning/towns.json`): the square (left of the way out, the platform's other side; 100 m to 28 m inside the outer gate, its far wall 30 m out, inside the yard's 70 m pad), how many stand round the centrepiece (3–5), one house in four with somebody out front, 2–3 lines a person, a scrap for 35%, 1–2 habits, 3–4 notices, 1–2 loose notes, reach (talk 2.6 m, read 2.0 m, 28°, closing at 4 m), typing 45 characters a second and 6 s left on screen. None is in the spec; each a first reading, fitted to the yard linegen lays.
    - **Generation** (`TownGenerator`). Deterministic from the route's seed and the fortress's name, each part on its own `Streams` stream (custom, habits, ring, names, roles, lines, papers...). The custom is drawn from those whose creature the edition fields (enemies.json `director.roster`; the demo gets six of the fourteen), never the last town's (`TownSite.Last`). People: the gatekeeper and a wall guard inside the gate, the clerk, the keeper and the storekeeper at their doors, a reader at the board, the fitter by the engine, the lampman by the cars, a porter on the platform, the ring round the centrepiece, and the street's folk down the yard (16–20 in all). Lines are dealt from shuffled decks without repeats in a town; the custom's six go first to the keeper and the ring, then the board's reader and the gate (`Precedence`), the street gets the habits and the trade, and anyone short is topped up from the trade, their job, a thread, and last `anyone`. The gatekeeper's first line is always the law. The board posts the custom's first note, then the trade's, the habits', the generic and the threads'; loose notes lie on a bench, by the hall's door, on a crate by the engine.
    - **In the world** (`Town`, `World.EnableTown`). The plan in world space, its walls (the square's far and end walls, the three buildings, the solid fixtures, a body-sized box for each person) added to the stops' `StopWalls` (now `Add`able), so `PlayerMotor` stops at them exactly as a client predicts. `Target` look-picks the person, board, paper, fixture or door within reach and the cone, for someone on the ground (up on a car, nobody in the town is in reach). Enabled after the run, on the host and every client (`SessionSetup.Build`, `PrototypeSession`), from the route and content alone.
    - **Not replicated, no protocol change.** Talking and reading change nothing in the night. The card is the app's (`Game/TownTalk.cs`): a Use press at the town's prompt (`Hud.TownTarget`: the prompt shown is the town's) opens it, Use again hears the next line or turns the next notice, walking 4 m off or a line said and left 6 s closes it, and the app keeps that press from the host while the key's held (so a lamp at somebody's feet isn't picked up). `Hud.Prompt` asks the town after the train's own controls and before what's lying in reach. Whoever you're talking to turns to you (`GreyboxScene.TownFacing`). What's needed alike is content: `world/*.json` is hashed into the Welcome as `world`, and a joiner who differs is told `world/*.json`. The last town's custom crosses in `SessionSetup.LastTown`, which the campaign keeps (`CampaignState.LastTown`, set when a night settles).
    - **Art** (`Art/SquareKit.cs`, `Art/WorldArt.Towns.cs`): the fortress's walls on the square's side run up to it and on from it on their ten-metre grid, the square's own walls step out to 30 m with a tower at each far corner, the lineside keeps its rocks and fences out of it (`WorldArt.TownSquare`), and no house of note 107's stands in it. The hall (brick, two storeys) and the office and stores (weatherboard), each with a name board, a door lamp and lit windows; a centrepiece for each kind (bell, post, tally, lamps, horn, mirror, shelf, lectern, brazier, line, pegs, bench, carriage, cannon); the board with a paper per notice; the plaque, stalls, benches, fire barrels, a crate; papers lying about; five lamp posts. The people are the crew's model in a lighter drab than note 107's (0.72). L1: kit boxes, readable at night; the director's review decides what's next.
    - **Headless:** `dt town [--route r] [--last culture]` (the town as made: custom, people and their lines, papers, fixtures, doors), `dt town sweep [--seeds n]` (customs' spread, repeats of the last, the longest line and paper), `dt screenshot --route r --town square|board|hall|gate|street`, `dt screenshot --hud --route r --talk person|board|paper|fixture|door [--who i] [--again n]` (the card open as Use opens it).

    - **Second pass: Maritime towns of 20 to 350, their houses and households (the director's notes of 7 Oct 2026, App. F.1 T133).** "These are maritime Canada towns, the buildings must look like Maritimes buildings. They must be fully interior modeled and explorable for some of them with residents. Lore is through NPC dialogue ... creepy things, sometimes hints of how to deal with monsters they've heard about but never the full rule. Cultures should all be traumatized somewhat ... between 20 to 350 people." The pieces:
        - **The writing** was rewritten for it. Maritime names, Acadian places among them (the card's font draws the accents as their plain letters), and every custom marked by loss. A plaque says how many live here and how many did. Households (`households`: the empty chair, the sleepwalker, the window, the one who came back, the letters, the cradle and more) have lines by part (elder, parent, child, lodger) and a thing of their own. A knock at a shut door is answered from behind it (`houseKnocks`); the lost houses (`emptyHouses`) have what's left to see.
        - **Anyone can hint at another town's creature, never the whole rule.** "In {other} they won't sing", "Go out four, come back four", "Never ride the last car". The hints are the customs' own, so nothing invented reaches a player as a rule.
        - **Enough lines for a 350-person town.** Every job has five lines, "anyone" has 47 and every thread three. A town of fifty speakers then never repeats a line and never leaves anyone mute.
        - **Population and the houses** (`TownGenerator.Houses`, `Sim/Towns/TownHouses.cs`; towns.json `population`, `household`, `former`, `houses`, `explorable`). The population (20–350), and how many lived here before (1.3–3.5 times as many), are drawn from the night. The households (2–6 each) take the lots down the yard's street on both sides, nearest the square first, every 15 m at 10.9 m out (note 107's village spacing, which the fortress's walls already allow for), off the platform. The first few, one per 70 people (2–5), stand open (`HouseKind.Open`), the rest shut (`Lived`). The houses of the people lost (the former population's households less the present's) follow: boarded, burnt or left open (`Boarded`, `Burnt`, `Empty`).
        - **An open house's ground floor is the Sim's** (`HouseLayout`): kitchen and parlour either side of a partition, the front door into the kitchen, a doorway through, the boxed stair, the range, table, dresser, cabinet, armchair and the parlour's lamp stand, each with a size and whether it's solid. `Town.Walls` stands the house's walls from it with the door and the doorway left open, plus the solid furniture, so a crewmate walks in, round the table and through to the parlour exactly as the art has it. A shut house is one box, a burnt one knee-high.
        - **The household** stands where the layout says somebody would be: at the range crouched, at the table or in the chair seated, at the window, by the stair. The parent always, then whoever else the household's story has a part for, up to four. Their own lines come first, then the custom.
        - **Nobody talks through a wall.** `Town.Seen` tests the line of sight against the town's walls (skipping the box the target stands in), so a resident can't be picked from outside the back wall.
        - **With T124's solid fortress** (#199, note 274): `Fort.Square` steps the fortress's walls and towers back round the square, and a town's fort stands no village houses (`Fortresses.Solids`). The town's houses are its own.
