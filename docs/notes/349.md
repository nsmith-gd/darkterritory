349. **CAPTIONS: the sounds worth hearing, written as they're heard (F1, UI/UX 3; queue #86; GDD §32 "Accessibility"; the director, 8 Oct 2026).** A player who can't hear the tiptoes on the roof can't learn that they mean something's there. With **CAPTIONS** on (off by default; `Settings.Captions`), what's heard is written in fine print at the foot of the frame on the left, where nothing else is: `[TIPTOEING, ABOVE]`, `[A CHILD CALLING, LEFT]`, the newest lowest.
    - **What a sound is, never what makes it** (content/ui/captions.json): a sound's name (or the name it's a variant of, a '.' at a time: `cs-ribbits.hop-land.ground` is `cs-ribbits.hop-land`'s) and the words someone hearing it would use. Neither a creature's name nor what to do: a reader learns what tiptoeing means as a listener does. The silent-by-design creatures (the Gaunt, the Passenger, the Followers) have nothing to caption. Covered: every creature's tell and sign that has a sound, the hot box, the boiler's strain and safety valve, the whistle (the Whistler's is the train's own) and the toys.
    - **Heard as the ear heard it:** fed each frame from the mixer's voices (not finished, not virtualised) at `SoundInstance.AudibleGain`, the gain the mixer last rendered each at, after distance and walls; under `threshold` (0.03) it isn't heard. Two of a kind are one caption, from the louder; a caption stays up `holdSeconds` (2.5) after its sound was last heard; at most `lines` (3) up, the oldest going first. Where is from the listener's head: AHEAD, BEHIND, LEFT, RIGHT, ABOVE, BELOW.
    - **Verified:** `CaptionsTests`: every caption is a sound that plays; none names a creature; variants, directions, threshold, hold and line count; end to end through the real mixer, a tiptoe 4 m behind is `[TIPTOEING, BEHIND]` and a call 2 km off isn't heard; the HUD writes them at the foot of the frame on the left at every TEXT SIZE; the setting starts off and saves. `dt screenshot --hud --captions` (with `--text-size`), looked at.
