   - **How each is staged** (`src/DarkTerritory.Dev/Tells/TellAudit.cs`: `Stage`, `Prime`, `Ready`, `Toward`; the numbers in `content/tuning/tells.json`, whose new `moving` is the speed the train is held at from the call to the shot):

     - **Freight Beetle.** The eye stands where it's heading, the freight coming at you.
     - **Hotbox.** Its heat comes only with its wheels turning; at a stand it comes half out of its truck after 2 s instead. That's why note 1037 saw nothing glow 120 s in: what it measured was the unfolded body. The audit gives it its `knockSeconds`' heat (90 s fed at its 18 m/s reference speed) and holds the train at 10 m/s, so it glows. Driving the 1.6 km instead tipped a car on the way. The eye is on the side of the car it's in. The glow matches `dt screenshot --view hotboxbug`'s: the axle box lit orange, its light on the ballast.
     - **Tippy Toesie** (it was staged in note 1037's run only by luck). Hidden, it waits at the line's start and comes out behind whoever's stood still and alone its `idleSeconds` (5 s). Note 1037's run got it only because the tells before had kept the caller standing long enough; alone (`--only tippyToesie`) it never came, on main too. In the cab, "behind" is clamped into the cab's few feet; toward the engine is the gym's safe ring, which drives it off. So the caller stands still for 5 s on the ground beside the cab, facing the train, before the call.

   - **Memory** (the manager, 26 Oct: three Linux CI runs died in the tests, one by the OOM killer, and `TellAuditTests` alone took a 15 GB, 4-core box from about 1.2 GB to 8.3–8.9 GB):
     - **What it does now.** Each tell's night is disposed and collected (a compacting GC) before the next is hosted, and the frames' throwaway scenes go with it. One renderer, the same as before. Its material textures are on a budget of their own (tells.json `textureBudgetMiB`, 1536 MiB, passed to its `GpuContext` alone through a new `limits` parameter, so the rest of the process isn't held to it). Under that budget the renderer halves its texture arrays to 256/512/1024 px: 636 MiB instead of about 2.5 GB. At 640x360 they measure the same: the fast four within 8 px and 0.3 L*, and the full run's pass and fail identical.
     - **Measured** (peak RSS of the test process, sampled each second; `TheFastTellsAreSeenAtTheirWarningDistanceInEveryNight` alone, three runs each): main 6.8–7.1 GB, this branch 5.4–5.7 GB. That's about 1.4 GB, a fifth, less. The full `dt tells` peaks at +5.3 GB.
     - **Not under the ~3 GB asked.** What's left after a forced collect, before the first tell: about 1.4 GB live managed (the look's art, the decoded textures, the scene's kits) and about 1.5 GB native (the lavapipe driver, its pipelines and meshes). Each tell adds about 0.45 GB of live heap the first time, then stays flat. Tried and not kept:
       - collecting after every frame (0.2 GB less);
       - a quarter budget (only 0.1 GB less than half);
       - reading the textures from copies downscaled to 1024 px in a temp folder (0.45 GB less, but it took an API on the shipping `Look` and a cache folder written by the tests).
     - **What would take it further** (a row for whoever holds the renderer or the test setup): the look and the renderer letting go of decoded texture pixels once they're uploaded (both keep them for the run), or CI running the GPU tests (Dev.Tests' audit, Game.Tests' renders) one project at a time rather than beside each other.
