257. **The derailment film, bigger and closer: cars hit bodies, the gunner thrown from the seat, landings thud (the director, 5 Oct 2026; GDD v1.4 App. E.3, E.4, E.10; note 251's "not epic enough").** Note 251's video showed small, upright bodies lying in cut-away cars. The director's calls, all in `tuning/wreck.json` "film" and `Train/WreckFilm.cs`:
    - **A bigger fling** (inside E.10's ranges): ×1.6 (was 1.3), a kick of 3–5 m/s (2–4) and 3.5–7 rad/s of spin (up to 4; E.10's top is 8; `spinMin`, so a body in the air goes over rather than drawing next to no spin). E.3's text and E.10's defaults say so.
    - **The cars hit the bodies.** The ragdolls stepped against the cars where the wreck had them at the end of each 30 Hz frame, so a car jumped up to 0.7 m through a body between steps and the hit was a push-out. Now each ragdoll substep sees the cars between where they were a frame ago and now (`Stage.At`), and every contact carries its surface's own motion (`Ballast.Physics.Contact.Motion`, new, zero for the still world): `PbdBody` takes friction and bounce relative to it, and a moving surface bounces what it meets at the speed they closed at. A car ploughing into a body throws it ahead; one coming down on it presses it into the ground (crushed, note 258). Nothing pushes a body to somewhere safe: it can be pinned or run under. Bodies piling up (Pile) and the rest are as they were. No limb separates (E.1): the joints are D.9's bones.
    - **Doorways are gaps (E.3, note 177's "not yet").** `FilmCar.Doorways` (the car's `DoorList`, end doors and a cargo car's side doors): a body inside is held by the walls except in a doorway, floor to lintel; out through one (its pelvis out of the box), it's outside and the car is a solid to it.
    - **The gunner is thrown out of the seat (T112).** `FilmCar` carries its mounted gun (pivot and laid yaw) and the film makes it solid: the barrel and breech (`GunBarrel`, the cannon model's 0.44 m behind the pivot to 1.5 m ahead) on its pedestal (`GunPedestal`). `FilmPlayer.Seated`: the gunner's ragdoll starts `seatClear` (0.25 m) further back from the gun than the seat, with the seat's own velocity (the car's, not flung on into the breech) and `seatKick` (6 m/s, E.10's top kick) up, head over heels at the full spin about the seat's axis; the car slows under them in the air, so they go up and over it. `dt film --gunner` sits the last of the crew in the first gun's seat as it comes off.
    - **Landings are recorded and heard.** Each frame carries `FilmLanding`s (which body, where, how hard, off a car or the ground): a hit of `thudSpeed` (2 m/s, of velocity taken off the head, chest or pelvis inside a frame by the ground or a car) or more, `thudGap` (0.25 s) apart (a hit at the killing speed always thuds). `GameAudio.Film` plays `crew-carry.body-land.ground` or `.wood` (the checklist's thrown-body landing, already installed) at each, the hardest three in a stretch, its volume by the speed. No new sound, so `dt audio render` is unchanged.
    - **Each shot is the throw into their death.** E.5's peak was the highest apex, longest airtime or hardest landing, and the scorer's was mostly the top of the hop off a car's floor 0.3 s in: the shot ended before anything hit anyone, and they were drawn stood upright. A player's shot is now built round their death (note 258): its stretch of recording is 70% before the hit and 30% after, slowest just before it, at `baseSlow` 0.4× easing to `peakSlow` 0.15× (was 0.25× and 0.1×, inside E.10's ranges; at the old rates a 2 s shot showed 0.35 s of the throw). The peak's score still orders them, biggest last; the bookmark (D.12) and the opera's hit (E.6) land on the deaths. E.5 says so.
    - **Framed closer (E.4 O6).** The director's shell scoring now favours the 4 m ring (−0.12 a metre out, was a pull toward 7 m), a player's shot follows the subject's middle (`WreckFilm.Middle`: their bounds' centre, steadied over ±0.1 s; the camera pans, it doesn't move, O13), and its field of view is fitted (`Fit`) to hold them at `shotFill` (45%) of the frame's height through the middle of the shot, never over 60% or 70% of the width, between `fovMin` and `fovMax` (22–60°). O1–O5 are checked as before over the whole shot.     - **A cutaway keeps its floor** (note 251's other answer): a car cut away for a shot is drawn as its deck and sills (`GreyboxScene.CutFloor`), so someone inside reads as lying in a car with its shell lifted off.
