291. **The Look Review's open creature notes (the director, 6 Oct 2026; queue #29, E1).** Six notes on the creatures had no reply. Each was looked at in the reel and the turntables, and is answered in the model or its clips.
    - **The Ribbit's geometry "that doesn't seem quite attached."** Seen from four sides, the hind feet were flat planks laid by the legs. The ears were boards stood up off the skull. The hands were a disc on a stick with a brush of fingers. The jaw was a thin tray under rings of lip, and there was a crease all round the neck. Now the skull is fused into the body's skin (`kit.fuse("body", ["body", "legs", "skull"])`). The hind feet are a hare's, with a heel, a thick sole and four toes grown into the leg. The forearm runs on into the hand's heel, and the fingers have knuckles and a thumb. The jaw is a heavy, rolled scoop of its own fused skin. The ears are lop ears, rooted deep in the skull, hanging down past the neck.
    - **"The swell needs to be much bigger, the tongue has no actual tongue."** The sac's pivot is now its top, under the chin. Swelling, it balloons to a pale, veined ball bigger than the head, out in front of the chest. The head stays level for it, as a calling toad's does; before, the swell pose tipped the head back 56°. The tongue is modelled: broad, flat and wet, with a groove, lying in the mouth's floor, and a sticky club at its end. `tongue_01` scaled along itself shoots it out; `tongue_02`, the club, is scaled back so it stays round. In the `tongue` clip it's 7× its length. In the game `CreatureArt.Lash` aims it at the catch's chest and stretches it until the club is on them. That replaces the cylinder the engine drew. With nobody to reach, it's drawn back to the length it lies in the mouth. `RibbitTests.OnItsCatchItsTongueIsOutToThem` holds it to the chest, as before.
    - **The Cinder Hounds' animations, "a full overhaul with much better quality."** The paws are placed by IK on footfall paths, not swung by angle tables. In each gait a paw is down for its duty, sliding back under the body, then picked up, folded and swung through. The pitch of the foot and toes (hind) or the hand and fingers (fore) is set through the stride, and the stifle is held forward of the hip-to-hock line, the elbow behind (`paw`, `stride`). On that, the gallop is a rotary one, hind left, hind right, fore right, fore left, in 14 frames. It has a gathered suspension (all four off the ground, the spine bowed) and an extended one, the body pitching over the forelegs. The head is steadied against all of it, and the tail streams out, each bone a beat behind. The prowl is a lateral walk: the shoulder blades roll over each step and the back sways. Its stop now holds a forepaw up off the ground while the head snaps round. The crouch, lunge, bite and hit are posed with planted paws. The lunge sinks before it goes, flies with the forelegs out past the head, and lands within 0.45 m of the sim's place, so the cut to the bite doesn't jump. The feet planter is gone. Every clip's paws now rest on the ground (lowest −0.002 m) and leave it only in a stride's flight. The checks are the stick figures of every frame and `CreatureArtTests`.
    - **The Switchman's "little shuffle flee: a bit more fluid, slow, and intentioned."** The flee was 20 frames on four linear keys. It is now two long strides over 2.1 s, keyed every four frames and eased. The weight rolls over each planted foot before the other is dragged through. The hips and shoulders turn against each other, the lantern swings behind the walk, and once a cycle the head turns round over its shoulder, unhurried, to look back at the train. (In the game the sim goes from BreakOff to Gone at once, so the flee is the reel's and the Look Review's.)
    - **The Tippy-toesie, "a general more creepiness."** Its hunch is curled tighter, the neck craned out and the head cocked over too far. The stalk is the checklist's exaggerated slow tiptoe: the knee drawn up to the chest and held, the head popping over to the other side, the foot let down slow. Waiting, its head lolls over much too far and goes further. Then it's upright all at once, turned full on you, and goes slowly back over while its fingers ripple, one after another. Fleeing, it scuttles low with its arms folded up like a mantis's and its head turned round, still on you. The shush finger is put to the face after the head is cocked, and the folded hands are placed by IK before their own shoulders, so `dt art clearance` stays clean (CreatureArtTests). The hunch is eased just enough that it still stands over a door's height on its points, so `TippyToesieTests` (its heights against the door and the roof) passes unchanged.
    - **The Choir, "more ethereal and ghostly."** The bell and the tendrils are ghost parts. A part named `ghost…` (`MeshPart.Ghost`) carries `Skinner.GhostWear` (−1, which no real wear is), and the scene shader draws it screen-door transparent by the view, as the era's games drew a ghost (`bayer4`). It is thin and see-through face on, dense at the rim where the eye looks through more membrane, and the rim gives off a faint cold of its own. The child's mouth and its teeth stay solid, hanging in a bell you can barely see. The drift's tendrils move in slower, wider waves, and the bell turns a little as it goes. The flag is on the part because the bake (tools/models overbake) merges a model's materials into one atlas, so a material name doesn't survive it.
    - **rig.fuse, two fixes.** Edges left with no face (a fold the dissolve closed up) are deleted first; on their own they made QuadriFlow call the whole skin not manifold, and the skin fell back to the collapse's uneven triangles. The Ribbit's body and jaw are QuadriFlow's now. `kit.fuse(..., lose=)` says how much of the union QuadriFlow may smooth over before the collapse is used instead; the default 1.5% is unchanged, and the Ribbit's body takes 3% for the groove under its lip.
    - **Found on the way:** a shader with more than one non-ASCII character in it fails to compile ("unexpected end of file"). The shader text seems to be handed to the compiler sized in characters, not UTF-8 bytes, so each multi-byte character drops a byte off the end; scene.frag's one ü fits in its trailing newline. Keep shader comments ASCII.
