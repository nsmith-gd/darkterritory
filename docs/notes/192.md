192. **The mix and voice lines at L1: long takes off the mixing thread, the work's drone on its tier, spaces, tunnel voice.**
    - **Decoding without a stall.** A take whose file is over `SampleLibrary.InlineBytes` (10 KB, about a second at 64 kbit/s) decodes on a worker, two at a time. Its layer waits in silence, then plays the take from its first sample, its own clock that much behind the sound's (`LayerState._late`). Which take plays is the seed's alone (`SoundInstance.Takes` names it before it's decoded), so only *when* depends on the machine. A short take, or one a worker already has, is decoded or waited for at once. Offline renders set `InlineBytes` to `long.MaxValue`, so they're the same every run. `SoundBank.Prefetch` starts a sound's takes on a worker; GameAudio prefetches the music at startup. `SampleTests` step the worker by hand.
    - **Music's tier (T7, decided 1 Oct).** `Mixer.Tiers` is 7. In mix.json every other tier ducks it: −12 dB under a tell, −9 under voice, −6 and −3 under train state and actions, −2 under the bed and the world (which are nearly always there). It has no rule of its own, so it ducks nothing, voice least of all. `tierDb` is each tier's fader (music −6; its takes are levelled to −30 LUFS). GameAudio plays `ui-music.drone`, if installed, flat, from when the run leaves the yard while the work goes on: it stops as the train comes off the rails or the night's over (GDD v1.4 E.1, opera means the night is over; E.9's outro has no music). The opera's bus (tier 0, note 174) is apart from it: nothing ducks the opera, everything ducks the drone.
    - **Spaces (spec A.6).** `GameAudio.SpaceOf` decides from the listener's ear. Inside a car's walls is "car", even in a tunnel. In a tunnel's bore (within 6 m of the main line) is "tunnel", the open-backed cab included. Then "cab", then "facility" along a facility's yard, else "outside", which is dry. Each space's response is synthesised from spaces.json (`ImpulseResponse.Synthesize`): early reflections where the walls are, then noise decaying in Decay (its highs in Decay × HighDecay), a lowcut, unit energy at WetDb, and the last 30% eased out. `Convolver` is uniformly partitioned overlap-save: 512-point FFTs shared by both ears, and a vectorised multiply-add over the partitions. Every positioned voice goes in at its tier's send, post-fader; flat sounds (UI, music, the radio) stay dry. A new space's reverb starts empty while the last one rings out. **Cost**, Release on AVX2: the tunnel's 1.8 s (338 partitions) is 18 ms per second of audio, 0.2 ms of a 90 fps frame; the car's is 0.08 ms. Responses are built at startup (the tunnel's takes 12 ms). `dt audio render [--sound s] --space tunnel`.
    - **Tunnel voice (GDD §22).** On top of note 183's band-pass on voice under a tunnel (voice.json's "tunnel"), the space's compressor and reverb. "Compressed proximity voice" is read as the sound of it, not its range: the 26 m cutoff stays. The tunnel's `voice` compressor works on a positioned tier-2 voice's level at the ear (−26 dB threshold, 3:1, +8 dB makeup). 4 m against 16.5 m goes from 8.2 dB apart to 4.0, and the far voice comes up 5 dB. Its `muted` list takes the outside (wind, bed-wind, world-night, world-wind, rain outside, thunder) to nothing over 0.3 s and keeps the tunnel's own sounds. The tunnel's reverb takes voice at a send of 0.9. `dt voice bench --space tunnel`.
    - **The tell audit** now meters the tells' own share of the reverb apart (`Mixer.TellReverbStem`, convolved separately only while a tap is on). A tell's room is the tell's; the bed's room still counts against it. With that split, the cab's reverb costs the cab's tells under half a dB. Tells go in at lower sends than voice (0.25 to 0.35) so their rhythm and direction stay crisp (spec A.4 rules 2 and 4).
