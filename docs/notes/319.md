319. **An engine short of steam holds the train back (queue #58, D1; the director's test build, 7 Oct 2026: "both heat and pressure are going down, something is wrong with the boiler, and I'm maintaining top speed").** Not a merge fault: main did it too. With steam driving (T97) the pressure sets the speed the engine can make, and the engine pulls only up to it; nothing held the train back above it. With nobody firing at full regulator (a 4-car train on the flat), the fire was out by 90 s and the pressure gone by 240 s, and the train was still at 17 m/s then, 10.6 m/s at 15 minutes: train.json's resistance (not in the spec) is "about 0.019 m/s² at max speed, so a dead boiler coasts for kilometres". T97's own reading ("pressure sets the speed ... the vent (or the brake) for less") wants it both ways.
    - **The rule.** Going faster than its steam can make, the engine holds the train back at `boiler.json` `starvedDecel` (0.12 m/s²) × (1 − the boiler's power factor): none from `workingBandMin` (60) up, all of it at `powerFloor` (20). A ruptured engine keeps its own `ruptureDecel`. 0 puts the old coast back. In the working band nothing changes, so the spec's acceleration and braking tables, the bots' driving and every steady night are as they were.
    - **After:** the same run holds 19 m/s while the pressure's in the band, then falls with it: 17.6 m/s at 120 s (pressure 40), 11.1 at 180 s, stopped by 270 s. GDD §23's "Fire dies: coasting on grade and momentum, brakes only" still holds on a grade; on the flat the coast is now a few minutes, not a quarter of an hour.
    - **Verified:** `BoilerTests.AnEngineWhoseFireDiesSlowsWithItsSteam` (unchanged in the band; under 6 m/s at 240 s where the old coast was still over 16; stopped by 300 s). A 4-bot frontier:7 night with enemies runs exactly as before (4,031 m in 240 s, nobody lost). The Sim suite.
