855. **The host's voice reaches a joiner outdoors (the director's playtest of 16 Oct evening, `docs/playtest/2026-10-16-evening.md` line 1: "she can't hear me when I'm talking in-game, but I can hear her ... she can hear me when she's in the cab. So maybe it's the outdoor ambience that is interfering with the vocals, but my mic is way too low for her") (Theo, queue #583).**
   - Claimed 2026-10-18 18:55 UTC by the Token Budget Manager (nathaniel3) for Theo; the note and its Handoff block are Theo's to write as the work goes (rule 9).

   - **What it was: both, and neither is host-only.** The host's own player is an ordinary `ClientSession` over UDP loopback
     (`NetPlaySession`: "the host's own player comes aboard before anyone else can: first aboard takes the cab"), so its voice
     goes through the same `VoiceChat.Capture` → Opus → `HostSession.ForwardVoice` → `VoiceRouting` as a joiner's; nothing on
     the send differs. What differed:
     1. **The mics.** Nothing levelled a microphone: `AudioIn` hands SDL's samples straight to the voice activity and Opus, and the
        settings' MIC LEVEL defaults to 1. The mix and every voice test were made against `SyntheticSpeech`, about -15 dBFS RMS:
        a hot mic, a Linux desktop's PulseAudio at 100%. A Windows headset without boost speaks near -35 dBFS: 20 dB under, heard
        20 dB quieter by everyone. Her mic carried; his was "way too low for her".
     2. **Where they stood.** The host drove: in the cab. She was outside it, so his voice reached her through the cab's wall
        (spec A.3's -12 dB and 900 Hz low-pass), and hers him the same way: symmetric, but at speed it left no room for a quiet
        mic. Side by side in the cab (where she could hear him) a voice is 10 dB over the cab's bed even from a quiet mic.
     3. **The outdoor bed.** On a roof at 22 m/s the loudest thing in the voice's band (300 Hz-3 kHz) is the wind past you
        (`bed-wind.wind-fast`, tier 6, -32.7 dB), over the wheels (tier 5, -33.4). Voice ducked tier 5 only (spec A.3), never
        the wind. Measured (`dt voice bench`, the train held at 22 m/s, the new `restDb`/`overRestDb`/`maskedBy`):

        | Speaker → listener (mic) | before | after |
        |---|---|---|
        | cab → engine roof, 7.5 m, through the wall (quiet, -20) | -14.8 dB under the rest | +3.1 over |
        | engine roof → cab, 7.5 m (quiet) | -14.5 under | +3 over |
        | roof → roof, 4 m in the open (quiet) | -0.9 under | +17 over |
        | roof → roof, 8 m (quiet) | -0.5 under | +16 over |
        | roof → roof, 4 m, hot mic (0) | +18 over | +17 over (the leveller cuts a hot mic ~2 dB, the wind ducked gives some back) |

   - **The fix.** (1) `Ballast.Voice.VoiceLeveller`: each client levels its own mic before it's encoded, to `targetDb` -14 dBFS
     of speech, lifting at most 24 dB and cutting at most 12, learning the talker's level only from frames the voice activity
     calls talk (fast up, slow down, pauses ignored), the gain gliding across each frame and a frame's peak held under 0.95.
     MIC LEVEL trims after it, and the voice activity still hears the mic as it comes (trimmed), so the lift never opens the mic
     on the room and MIC LEVEL 0 still sends nothing. Tuning: player.json `voices.leveller` (`PlayerTuning.Voices.Leveller`,
     hot-reloaded). (2) mix.json: tier 2 ducks tiers 5 **and 6** by 6 dB; systems-spec A.3's tier 2 row says so.
     No wire change (rule 10 not needed): the leveller is client side, before Opus.

   - **Held by:** `AudioTests.TheBedAtSpeedNeverDrownsACrewmateTalkingAtSpeakingDistance` (a quiet mic at top speed: 4 m and 8 m
     in the open at least 10 dB over everything else in the band, through the cab's wall either way at least 2 dB; fails with
     the leveller off and fails with the wind unducked); `VoiceTests.TheHostsVoiceReachesAJoinerAsLoudAsTheJoinersReachesTheHost`
     (a host's mic 20 dB quieter than the joiner's, each heard within 1.5 dB of the other through the wall, and in the cab);
     `VoiceLevellerTests` (Ballast.Voice.Tests: mics 10 and 20 dB apart sent within 1 dB, the lift's limit, the ceiling on a
     shout, no pumping on the hiss between words).

   - **Reproduce / look:** `dt voice bench --car 0 --listener-car 0 --speed 22 --mic -20 --seconds 3 --out out/audio/v.wav`
     (the driver to a crewmate on the engine; `--listener-cab` beside him; `--car 3 --z 4` on a roof): a WAV and its
     spectrogram. Through the wall only the voice's harmonics under 900 Hz stand over the bed; in the cab the formants to 3 kHz.

   - **For the director (not changed here):** through the cab's wall at speed a voice is now 3 dB over the bed: heard, not
     comfortably. The cab is a closed space for sound whatever its doors (`PlayerMotor.Space`; a car with a door open is
     outside, the cab isn't). If it should still be hard to talk to the driver from outside at speed, that's as designed (the
     radio's for that, T); if not, the cab's doors open could count as outside, as a car's do (Juno's #584 is on the cab doors).
