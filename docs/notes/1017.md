   - **The bug:** a Linux player (the director's second player, 16 Oct) saw large opaque black rectangles across the title
     screen's horizon, over the trees and the train; still there in tonight-58. Note 850 part 3 put the GPU and driver in
     the problem report and found nothing wrong on lavapipe under the Khronos validation layer; the player's driver line
     never came. This fixes what the code can without it.
   - **Not reproduced here.** The container runs one Vulkan driver, lavapipe (Mesa 25.2.8); the other ICDs it carries
     (intel, intel_hasvk, nouveau, radeon, asahi, virtio, gfxstream) need their hardware. Held to a small GPU's limits
     (`--gpu-limits small`, below) the title screen draws the same picture with smaller textures, and no squares. So each
     suspect is made safe on its own, and the player gets a switch to try (`--gpu-safe`).
   - **The likeliest mechanism, and its fix: a NaN or infinity in the scene's half-float target, spread by the bloom.** Every
     post pass reads the RGBA16F scene target; the bloom's bright pass takes it at half resolution and its two separable
     9-tap blurs (and the wide bloom's, at quarter resolution, twice as far) spread one bad texel into a square block, which
     the composite adds to every pixel under it and the tone curve draws black (`filmic(NaN)`). Black **squares** over the
     brightest parts of the frame (the lit horizon band, the lamp, the smoke) is what that looks like. Where lavapipe and a
     real driver part: a colour past half float's 65504 may store as infinity on hardware where lavapipe saturates; GLSL
     leaves `smoothstep` with its edges the wrong way round undefined (three were: the sky's ground haze at the horizon,
     `sky.frag`; the reflections' ground, `scene.frag`; the smoke's soft blob, `fx.frag`); an implicit-LOD `texture()` inside
     a branch that splits a 2x2 quad has undefined derivatives (the backdrop band's, at its top and bottom edges); and fresh
     GPU memory holds garbage (half-float garbage is often NaN) where lavapipe's is zeroed. Now:
     - `finite()` (`Shaders/view.glsl`) turns NaN into nought and holds a colour inside ±65000, tested by its bits so no
       driver's fast maths folds it away; on everything written to the scene target (`scene.frag`, `sky.frag`, both of
       `fx.frag`'s outputs) and everything the post stack reads back from it (`bright.frag`, the composite's scene and bloom).
     - The three reversed `smoothstep`s written low to high (`1.0 - smoothstep(lo, hi, x)`, the same curve).
     - The backdrop band sampled before its branch, at `v` clamped to the band.
     - Every target `BeginRendering` opens is **cleared** (to black, alpha 1), not `DontCare`
       (`GreyboxRenderer.BeginRendering`): no pass leaves a pixel today, but one that ever did would show the tile's garbage.
   - **The limits the renderer never checked, now checked** (`GpuContext.Limits`, a `GpuLimits`): `maxImageArrayLayers`,
     `maxImageDimension2D`, the RGBA8 image's `maxResourceSize` with `maxMemoryAllocationSize`, and the largest device-local
     heap. The material arrays are big: 333 layers at 512 px (466 MiB each of diffuse, spec and normal), 48 heroes at 1024
     (268 MiB each) and 7 big heroes at 2048 (157 MiB each), **2.7 GB** in all. `GreyboxRenderer.FitShift` halves all of
     them (each layer by its authored width, so nothing moves between the arrays) until the widest fits the image size, the
     largest fits one image's bytes, and the lot fits the heap less a gigabyte (at least half the heap left): none on a
     desktop GPU with 4 GB or more, one halving on a 2 GB card (666 MiB), two on a 1 GB heap (139 MiB). Past the array
     layers (never on a desktop driver: 2048 there; the spec's floor is 256) the last layers are left out and draw as the last
     one kept. Both are said in the log (`gpu: material textures at ...`, `gpu: 333 material layers, past the device's ...`),
     which the crash and problem reports carry.
   - **`--gpu-safe`** (every build) **and SETTINGS, SAFE GRAPHICS** (`Settings.GpuSafe`, from the next start; the screen
     section, after VSYNC): `GpuContext.Safe`. The composite reads neither the bloom nor the occlusion (both at nought, and
     `composite.frag` skips the reads entirely, so a bad texel in either can't reach the frame), the material textures load
     at half size, and the samplers take no anisotropy. The report's `gpu` line ends `; safe graphics` when it's on.
   - **Developer only** (`Ballast.Dev/GpuFlags.cs`, reached by `dt` and under `#if DEVTOOLS` in the app; note 514):
     `--gpu-limits [small | layers=N,image=PX,bytes=MIB,heap=MIB]` holds every device made after to a small GPU's limits
     (`small`: 256 layers, 4096 px, 512 MiB an image, a 1 GiB heap); `dt ... --gpu-safe` takes the safe path.
