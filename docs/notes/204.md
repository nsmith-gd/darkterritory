204. **The combination sweep over a grid of routes and crew sizes (note 186's "not yet"; GDD §34, App. B.10; WP17).** `dt balance --pairs|--triples` ran every combination on frontier:7 with four bots and one seed, so an unwinnable verdict was one lost night on one line with one crew.
    - **The grid** (`balance.json combinations`, `CombinationGrid`): `routes` × `crews`, `seeds` nights a (route, crew) *cell*. Each combination is run in every cell (`Combinations.Nights`: combination by combination, then route, crew and seed, a fixed order). The hazard sets are met as before: one each, in turn down the combinations, or all with `--every-hazard`. Each route has its own staging (`Combinations.Stage` per route and combination).
      - The default grid is the old one (frontier:7, crew 4, one seed), so a by-hand run still takes minutes. `wide` is the nightly's: frontier:7 and deadLines:2, crews 2 and 8 (B.10's smallest and largest), two seeds. That's 8 nights a combination: `--pairs --wide` is 1,088 nights, about 13 CPU-seconds each here.
      - `--wide` picks that grid; `--routes a,b`, `--crews 2,4,8` and `--seeds n` override either one (`--route` and `--crew` still name a single one).
    - **Verdicts, aggregated** (`Combinations.Judge`, `Combinations.Cells`): a row is still one combination under one hazard set, now over every cell.
      - *Unwinnable* means it lost every night in some cell. The detail names the cells and how many of the others it won a night in. A combination lost once in every cell but never every night in one cell isn't unwinnable.
      - *Trivial* means everything came on and nothing landed in any night in any cell.
      - *Unplaced* and *dormant* look at every night of every cell, as they did at every seed.
      - **Reading:** a cell is the unit because B.10 asks for fairness "at every crew size" and a line is what the crew meets. A combination the bots can beat at eight but never at two is the finding, and averaging over the cells would hide it.
    - **Reported per cell:** each row has `cells` (route, crew, nights, lost, unwinnable, grabs, punishes, deaths, the kinds that came on), and each run says its `route` and `crew`. The report's `byCell` gives each cell over the whole sweep: its nights, its losses, and the combinations unwinnable there. The JSON's header says `routes`, `crews` and `seeds` where it said `route` and `crew`.
    - **First run** (Dragger, Climber, Cinder Hounds, Stoker; frontier:7 and deadLines:2 × crews 2, 8; one seed; 24 nights, 80 s on 4 cores): three trivial, two dormant (the Draggers, as note 186 says), and Dragger+Climber unwinnable in one cell, deadLines:2 with a crew of two, where the one death was half the crew. It won a night in the other three cells.
    - **Nightly:** `soak.yml` adds `balance --pairs --wide` beside the default grid's `--every-hazard` run, and prints `byCell`. The job's timeout is 240 min (note 186's 180 min no longer leaves room).
    - **Not done here:** bringing the dormant kinds on (the Gaunt, the Draggers, Ribbits). That's bot behaviour, not the sweep's: done in note 212 (the look-out's errand).
    - Harness-side only; protocol unchanged. Tests: `AuditTests.TheGridRunsEveryCombinationOnEveryRouteWithEveryCrew` (the nights' count and order, the hazards in turn or all of them, the tuning's two grids) and `UnwinnableIsLostEveryNightInSomeCell` (a cell lost every night is unwinnable and named; a loss in every cell that never takes a whole cell isn't; trivial is nothing landed anywhere; `byCell`'s nights, losses and unwinnable list).
