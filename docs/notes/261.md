261. **Cargo stops for the crew that's there (T114 "cargo stops"; GDD §17–18, spec D.2).** `StopDriver` made only the stops its bots could work alone, and only as an eight would (`CrewCalls.CanWorkWinch`: a shunter and both winch hands; crates wanted a hand besides the shunter), and people playing weren't counted at all. A crew of two (the driver and the gunner, who is the shunter) or one bot and someone playing, the default way to play, loaded nothing all night.
    - **The reading (ambiguous, so `facilities.json` "crew").** Spec D.2's "Crew" column counts people at a module, not their jobs. Read so its own table stays true for a small crew: the shunter is free once the train's in and is one of a module's crew; the driver is too, with too few others (`driverWorks`, on); people playing are hands (`peopleAreHands`, on). The "2 mandatory" of the winch and the crane, the ramp's 2 and the crates' "heavy items need two" are unchanged: the pair is still two at two handles.
    - **Who covers what** (`CrewCalls`): `PairBots` is the winch pair, then the shunter, then the driver, up to two. A pair module is planned for when the bots and the people between them make two (`PairWithPeople`); the bots take their places, the first missing handle the shunter's, the next the driver's (`PairPart`, `Lend`). Crates: the shunter carries where fewer than two bots do (one or two carry), the driver only where nobody else can (`Carries`). The hose wants one, its own hand or a spare. The spout's lever stays the shunter's (the driver's walking the cars under it).
    - **The shunter** (`StopHand`) takes its lent part at the end of the spur (the pair parts by `Pair`, not its job), puts anything in its arms down when there's none left, and goes back to the cab (`Done`, not `Aboard`): the switch is still its to set back.
    - **The driver** gets down as it does for a Holdout (note 259's pattern, now `ConductorBot.ReadyToLeave`): brake held, the firebox filled (to 0.95 for this: a crate run is minutes, and at a stand a full box lasts it a minute), the door shut, vented to its standing pressure. `StopDriver.DriverPart` says what for; `ConductorBot.WorkOut` works it through a `StopHand` (`Lend`) and climbs back up when it's done, the loading's over, or the fire's down to 1.25× the low fire (crates in its arms put down first; App. A.5's Stoker comes for a low fire). With no shunter it throws the spur's switch itself (`StopDriver.ThrowAlone`, after `AloneAfter`) and sets it back as before; it can't get between the cars to cut, so it plans only stops the train goes into whole, no pick-ups, and if the shunter dies before the cut it goes on at once instead of standing out the 240 s.
    - **People who don't come.** The driver watches the loading (`StopDriver.Watch`: what's in the cars, the sled along its haul, the castings set down). With nothing moving for `peopleWait` (45 s) it calls it idle (`CrewCalls.PeopleIdle`), and what only people could have done (the other handle, crates nobody bot carries) is left: `PairNow`, `Loaded`. The people playing are told nothing new; the bots just stop waiting.
    - **A cut that runs away is lost, not chased.** The traces of these nights showed the Passenger taking a hand in a car at deepTerritory:2's mine head and letting the cut's brakes off: note 188's backing-out closes on a rolling cut over its own speed, without a ceiling, so a crew of three's engine went after it at 22 m/s for 7 km and a crew of eight's (on main's code as well) to km 0.16.
