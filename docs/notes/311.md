      - One lofted shell of armour (`Kit.Loft`, new), raked back from a plough at the rail up to the cab's window sills. It's curved across in plan, so its ends sweep back into the cab's sides.

    - **The cab** (`CabShell`).
      - Its sides under the windows belly out like a hull's. Their foot rises towards the nose into the prow's sweep.
      - Two strakes run along them, round the prow at the same heights and on along the boiler, the lines the director asked for.
      - Over the waist, armour plate leaves slits for side windows rather than an open frame of posts. Welded patches sit over old damage, and a shutter is propped out over the slits.
      - The windscreen's posts rake forward from the sill into the brow, as a ship's bridge leans out over its bow.
      - The driver's front windows are the same openings as before.
    - **The roof** (`Roof`).
      - A helmet: arched across, its front narrowing and drooping into a brow out over the windscreen, with spikes welded along its edge.
      - Its eaves come down over the sides, more towards the back (`EaveAt`), so the roof's line falls back along the cab.
      - Two shoulders carry it on down onto the boiler's flanks, head-high over the way beside the boiler.
      - Over the top it keeps the arch the coaling hatch sits on (Bunker's `RoofY`).
    - **The boiler.** A rounded casing (`Rounded`, a superellipse; it was an octagon) under the strakes and straps. The skirt over the wheels is bellied too, its foot swept up at both ends like a fender's.
    - **The way in** (the director, 7 Oct: "there actually has to be a way to get into the cab from the train"). The new shell keeps it all clear (the shoulders are head-high over it; the side armour stops at the doorway). Each doorway is framed in rolled iron, with a caged lamp on a bracket at the cab's back corner, lit back along the board.
