We want to be able to populate the town with interesting characters that look cool. So get started on modeling these characters from this lookbook as best you can in keeping with our current style targets"; then, on the first shots: "These could be better quality on the blending and shapes").**

The ten, from the lookbook (left to right, top row then bottom), each a dressed figure (`Redress` on survivor_prisoner,
as notes 570-572: Dave, Nicki, Jacob) with what makes them who they are in `Art/LookbookKit.cs`, worn on the figure's
bones or held in its fists, and given to a town's job (towns.json `roles`):
- the trapper (porter): yellow oilskin, a bucket hat, a gas mask, a gaff, a lobster pot;
- the watchman (guard): a padded coat, a fur ushanka, binoculars on their strap, a red-and-white flag;
- the market woman (storekeeper): a red headscarf, a plaid shawl round the shoulders crossed on the chest, an apron
  over a red skirt, keys at her hip, a crate of greens;
- the lamplighter (lampman): a hooded brown coat, a lamp burning before his mask, two bottles on his back in a
  harness, a lamp on a pole (both lit);
- the linesman (fitter): a flat cap with goggles, a wrap over the face, a tool belt, a coil of cable on a strap;
- the preacher (keeper): a black cassock, a wide hat, a black gauze veil, a gold stole, a pectoral cross, a cross in hand;
- the butcher (cook): a knitted cap, a gas mask, a stained bib apron, a knife;
- the harbour master (gatekeeper): a navy greatcoat, double-breasted in brass, a peaked cap, a white beard, an eyeglass,
  keys, a spyglass;
- the drifter (driver): a long brown coat, a flat cap, a red scarf over the face, a flask;
- the schoolteacher (clerk): a green coat over a long skirt, her hair up in a bun, spectacles, books.

How they're made (the director, on the second pass: "PS2 era people looked better than this"):
- Modelled and baked as the crew and the survivors are (tools/models/crewfigure.py over tools/blender/crew.py): each
  character a recipe, `tools/models/recipes/townsfolk_<id>.py`, its clothes modelled over the figure in
  `tools/models/townfolk.py` (made to the frame's dense copy and the head scan by ray-casting: `crewfigure.Fit`), in kit
  materials `town.*` dressed in the library's layers, sculpted on their high copies (stitching, a can's ribs, quilting),
  skinned from the frame or on a bone (`crewfigure.Garment`), joined to the body and baked into the character's own
  1024 atlas with the rest: their folds, seams, buttons, laced boots and dirt painted in. `crewfigure.Style` grew
  `garments`, `coat` (the skirt shells left off), `scarf`, `body_off` (the bandolier, satchel, belt, chest lamp left
  off the frame: crewbody's blocking, crew.py's lamp, through `DT_CREW_OFF`). The women's face is the scan softened
  (`townfolk.softer`, `soften_face`). The market woman's plaid is woven in code (`townfolk.plaid`); the butcher's blood
  is a baked mask.
- No Blender here: `pip install bpy` and `BLENDER=tools/models/bpy-blender.sh` (Blender 5 as a Python module; rig.py
  reads its layered actions). A character bakes in about seven minutes on four cores; `TOWN_PREVIEW=1` renders the high
  figure to out/review in one.
- In the game, `CreatureArt.FigureFor(c)`: `townsfolk_<id>` (loaded beside the survivors, the crew's clips merged in)
  when it's built, else the dressed fallback (`Redress` on survivor_prisoner, in the character's colours, with
  `LookbookKit`'s code-made pieces; `Art/FigureSurface.cs` measures the figure for them). On a modelled figure only
  what isn't baked is still worn from the kit (`LookbookKit.Baked`): what they carry, the preacher's gauze veil, the
  lamplighter's lit lamp and hose, the teacher's spectacles, the harbour master's eyeglass, the market woman's keys.
- In a town (`GreyboxScene.Folk`, role from the plan's `Townsperson.Role`): the job's character in place of the drab
  figure; the town's other people ("anyone") one in two by who they are, among the seven who go about the town. What
  they carry is put down when they sit, crouch or work; a hand lamp takes the right fist (the lamplighter keeps his
  pole's). Their distance copy is clustered as the survivors' is (note 479). Ten more hero atlases: 48 of the
  renderer's 64.
- The renderer's people (the director: "I feel like our renderer for the game can do better than this for NPCs"): a
  figure's materials (`MaterialLook.Figure`, `CreatureArt.Person`: the townsfolk, the survivors and those dressed from
  them; not the crew, nor the creatures) carry -1 in the vertex's terrain blend, and scene.frag lights them as the
  look's figures (look.json post `figureWrap`, `figureRim`, `figureGrime`, through `frame.figure`): the light wrapped
  round them past the terminator (a face and a coat's folds turn from it softly instead of going one flat tone in the
  fill), a rim of the night's air round their silhouettes, tinted by their cloth (they stand out of the fog), and the
  shader's own grime kept off their baked atlases (the bake's dirt is theirs; doubled, it blotched the coats). The
  defaults are the old look, so nothing else changes.
- Shots: `dt screenshot --lookbook all|top|bottom|id,id [--closeup] [--turn deg] --survey`; in a town,
  `dt screenshot --route frontier:7 --town folk[n]` (the nth dressed townsperson). Tests: `LookbookArtTests`.
