194. **The opera, for real: CC0 recordings of the public-domain works, cut to their climaxes (GDD v1.4 App. E.6; WP25).** The user's call: replace note 174's synthesised tracks with public-domain compositions in well-known recordings that are CC0 (real orchestras and singers, Musopen's among them), each hit on the piece's own big moment. E.6's rule stands: the file's own licence must be CC0 1.0; a "public domain" mark on an old recording isn't enough.
    - **The intake runs in CI.** The cloud sessions' proxy refuses Commons, upload.wikimedia.org, Musopen, the Internet Archive and Freesound; GitHub's runners don't. `.github/workflows/music-intake.yml` starts on a push that changes `tools/audio/music_intake.json` (the request: which candidates, how many) to an intake branch, or by hand (`workflow_dispatch`, once it's on the default branch); never on a pull request. It installs ffmpeg, builds `dt`, checks the list (`--dry-run`) and the cut path (`--offline-test`), runs the intake, runs `MusicManifestTests`, `FrontEndTests` and `Ballast.Audio.Tests`, uploads the evidence (and the sources as downloaded) as artifacts, and commits `content/audio/music` back to the branch that asked (by hand: to `music-intake`, replaced each time). Its push touches only `content/audio/music`, which the paths filter leaves out.
    - **The candidates** (`tools/audio/fetch_music.py`, `CANDIDATES`): 26 public-domain works over E.6's four moods (E.6's own 14 first, then the Lacrimosa, the Funeral March, the Barcarolle, the Mountain King, the 1812, Mozart's Dies irae, Bald Mountain, Danse macabre, Beethoven's Fifth, the Carmen prelude, the Hallelujah, the Barber overture), each with Commons titles to try first, searches (and Musopen's uploads of the composer) when none of them passes, patterns a found title must contain, and the hit as a range in the file (seconds from the start or end, or fractions of the length: recordings differ) with a note of the moment ("Ridi, Pagliaccio"'s top note, the Dies irae's first bass-drum strikes, the can-can's whip-crack climb, the Valkyries' brass entry, "Vincerò!"). A CI probe found the Musopen overture to *Orpheus in the Underworld* (it ends with the can-can) and Offenbach's Barcarolle CC0; the Ride of the Valkyries and the 1920s *William Tell* on Commons are "Public domain" only, and refused.
    - **The licence test** reads each file page's wikitext through the API: it needs a `{{cc-zero}}` (or `{{CC0}}`) template on the page itself; the extmetadata (`License`, `LicenseShortName`, `LicenseUrl`) is recorded beside it. PD-old, PD-US, PD-USGov or a public-domain mark alone is a refusal. **Reading:** a rights holder's own `{{PD-self}}` release is a dedication but not CC0 1.0, which is all E.6 and the manifest test accept; the report lists such files as near misses, with the template, for a person to decide (a CC0 statement from the performer would let one in). Renderings of MIDI or notation software (title, description or wikitext saying so) are refused: E.6 wants performers. Commons rate-limits (a probe met HTTP 429 after about eight searches), so requests go at least 2.5 s apart with a descriptive User-Agent, 429 and 503 are retried honouring Retry-After, and a search fetches wikitext only for titles that match.
    - **Evidence** (E.6 "Manifest"): per track, in `intake/_sources/music/<id>/` (gitignored, in the artifact): the download, its SHA-256 (and its SHA-1 checked against Commons'), the extmetadata, the wikitext, and the page as rendered (the archived licence page). Committed: `content/audio/music/evidence/<id>.json` (the metadata, the CC0 templates found, the page's licence section, the revision, the source's SHA-256, the window and the analysis), and the manifest's `evidence` gains `sourceSha256`, `page` and `record`; `source` is the Commons file page.
    - **The hit and the window.** Within the hint's range the intake picks the 10 ms block with the biggest rise in the bright (over 3 kHz) level against the 300 ms before it, plus half of how near the next second is to the loudest second in the range (a crash into the biggest bar beats a consonant in a quiet one). It cuts up to 24 s before and 21 s after (at least 4.5 and 19.5: the replay's 3 s lead and the 18 s from the hit to the sequence's end, with a second's slack): more lead than the build uses, for E.5's hit on the final apex, which can be most of the film in. Two-pass `loudnorm` to −16 LUFS (true peak −1.5 dB, linear where it can be), a 50 ms fade in and 1 s out, mono Opus at 48 kHz, 64 kb/s. Then `dt audio music` (`src/DarkTerritory.Cli/MusicCommands.cs`) moves each hit onto the sharpest onset within a second (`AudioBench.SharpestOnset`, the manifest test's own measure) until it stays put, no more than 1 s from the analysis, measures BS.1770 loudness the game's way and sets the gain, takes the SHA-256, and writes the manifest and `CREDITS.md`. A track whose hit won't settle, that's too short, or whose loudness doesn't measure is left out with the reason.
    - **The pool** (`MusicFiles.Pool`, used by both `dt audio music` and `dt audio opera`): with 4 or more CC0 recordings (E.6's demo bar) they replace the synthesised fallback, and a mood none of them covers keeps its synthesised track (the speed weighting wants every mood: `TheDemoPoolHasEveryMood`); with fewer, the fallback stays and the recordings join it. A rerun keeps earlier recordings it didn't retake; files and evidence the pool no longer names are deleted. `dt audio opera` rewrites only the synthesised tracks the pool still has. At most 16 tracks (`maxTracks`).
    - **Opus, not WAV** (`Ballast.Audio.OggOpus`, `AudioClip.Load`): a 45 s window is about 2 MB as 22 kHz 16-bit WAV and about 360 KB as Opus, so 16 tracks are about 6 MB rather than 30. The decoder is the Ogg framing (pages, lacing, OpusHead's pre-skip and output gain, the last granule for the true length; mapping family 0, mono or stereo averaged) around Concentus, the managed Opus already used for voice, so no new dependency. `OggOpusTests` round-trips a stream written with Concentus's encoder and RFC 7845 framing (length to the sample, pitch, level).
    - **Alignment:** unchanged (note 174): `Opera.Cue` starts a track at `StartFor(replayLeadSeconds)`, so the manifest's `hit` lands on the replay's moment of derailment; whatever moment a later cue lines up (E.5's final apex), it lines up the same `hit`.
    - **Credits screen** (E.6: "the credits screen lists every performer anyway"): CREDITS on the title screen; `Screen.Credits` lists every manifest track, two lines each (work, composer and year; performers, licence, and the Commons page or the script that made it), scrolling, Esc back. The app hands the front end the manifest (`FrontEnd.Music`). The pixel font has no accents, so accented capitals draw as their plain letters ("LA DONNA E MOBILE"), curly quotes as straight. `dt screenshot --menu credits` renders it.
    - **Tests:** `MusicManifestTests` as before (CC0-1.0 with the file's hash, fields, every mood, loudness, the hit the sharpest onset where it says, the sequence covered, the credits), with a recording's source a Commons page rather than a repo path, plus `EveryRecordingCarriesItsLicenceEvidence` (source SHA-256, page, the record's CC0 templates and licence section) and `EveryHitLiesInsideItsTrackWithTheLeadTheSequenceNeeds` (the hit in [inPoint, outPoint], at least the replay's lead past the in-point). `FrontEndTests.TheCreditsListEveryTrackWithItsPerformersLicenceAndSource`; `OggOpusTests`.
    - **The first run, and what it changed** (run 37119988938, commit dd89caf): 8 CC0 recordings, all but one Musopen's (the can-can overture, the Anvil Chorus, the Toreador Song, the Carmen prelude, the Funeral March, and a National Library of Sweden phonograph cylinder of "La donna è mobile"), but two mislabelled and the performers wrong:
      - *Mislabels:* the loose title patterns let Åse's Death in as the Mountain King ("peer gynt") and the Egmont overture in as the Fifth ("beethoven"). A candidate now has `must` patterns that all have to be in the title (the work's own words) and `never` patterns that refuse it (another movement's name); `--dry-run` checks them against titles seen in CI (`MATCH_CASES`). Both pieces are candidates in their own right now, labelled honestly: Åse's Death a Lament (the strings' fortissimo peak, 33-62% in), the Egmont overture Doom (the final Allegro con brio's fanfare, 95-25 s from the end).
      - *Performers:* the Commons "Artist" field is often the composer ("Performed by Frédéric Chopin") or a source blurb (the Swedish library's). Now: the page's performer field, then author or artist, then "performed by ..." in the description, never anything with the composer's name, a source or a URL; a Musopen upload naming nobody is "Musopen (performers uncredited)"; else "Unknown performers (uploaded by <user>)".
      - *Finding them:* searches met the rate limit and missed. The intake now scans categories first (`list=categorymembers`, files and subcategories, paced, at most 260 requests): `Category:Musopen` and Musopen's audio categories, the opera audio categories, and each candidate's own (`categories`), then fetches wikitext only for the titles a candidate matches; a search is the last resort. Every matching file that isn't CC0 is reported as a near miss with why (`report.json` `nearMisses`), so what isn't available as CC0 is on record.
      - *Hits:* three of the eight landed where the music falls away or in a quiet bar (the Egmont's was in the Fifth's range). The analysis now also scores how much louder the second after the hit is than the one before, and each cut is checked (`hitCheck`: the 2 s after against the 2 s before, and the loudness rank of the second after; "weak" for a person to listen to first). The manifest's note gives the hit's time in the original (m:ss), the range it was found in and the moment it's meant as. The Anvil Chorus, Carmen prelude and Funeral March ranges were set from the pieces' structure.
      - A full run replaces the recorded set (`dt audio music --replace`), so a mislabel from before goes; `dt audio opera` runs after it to fill the demo's 4 and every mood from the fallback if the recordings don't.
    - **The second run** (run 37122568018, commit 36af685; 104 categories, 1,145 audio files scanned): 7 CC0 recordings (the Anvil Chorus, "La donna è mobile", the Toreador Song, the Funeral March, Åse's Death, the Egmont overture, and the Carmen prelude under the Habanera's name) and the synthesised Infernal Galop and William Tell. The famous arias were mostly "Public domain" only, refused. Fixed for the third:
      - *Pinned sources:* a candidate can pin files (`pinned`: a title and, optionally, a hit range inside it) past its patterns, still licence-checked. The strict patterns had lost the Musopen *Orpheus in the Underworld* overture (its title names neither the galop nor the can-can); it's pinned for the Infernal Galop with the hit on the can-can's final climax at its end. The Carmen prelude is pinned to its own candidate.
      - *Categories don't match:* run 2 handed the Carmen prelude to the Habanera because both listed "Audio files of Carmen" and being in a candidate's category was enough. Only the title patterns (or a pin) match now; categories only say where to scan.
      - *Performer parse:* an empty field ("|author =" then a new line) was read as the next field's name ("|title ="). Values now stop at the line and never start with "|"; `--dry-run` checks the parse on pages laid out like the runs' (`PERFORMER_CASES`) and on every committed evidence record's wikitext, which records now keep (up to 20 KB).
      - *The open pool* (`OPEN`): 39 more well-known dramatic public-domain works across the moods (Bach's Toccata in D minor, the New World finale and Largo, Mozart's Confutatis and Rex tremendae, the Grieg concerto, Tchaikovsky's Fourth and Pathétique, the Moonlight's Presto, Beethoven's Seventh and Fifth finales and the Ode to Joy, Brahms's Fifth Hungarian Dance, Light Cavalry, the Sorcerer's Apprentice, Nimrod, Swan Lake, Pomp and Circumstance, the Radetzky March, the Great Gate of Kiev, Zadok the Priest and more), each with its climax as the hit, matched only against the scanned files (no title guesses or searches). Every candidate's best CC0 file is found first, a file to one candidate; then the picks go a mood at a time in turn, E.6's table first, then the named extras, then the open pool in order of recognisability, to `maxTracks` (16).
      - The job log now has the kept tracks with each hit's time and check, the refusals, and the near misses.
    - **The third run** (run 37123738964) took in the Pathétique sonata and symphony, the Blue Danube, the Tchaikovsky concerto and the Carmen prelude again, then failed `EachTrackIsMeasuredAndNormalisedToMinusSixteenLufs`: the sonata's gain to −16 LUFS put its peaks over the top, so nothing was pushed. loudnorm's dynamic mode had left the piano well under the target. Now the intake measures each encoded cut (ffmpeg `ebur128`), and one more than 0.3 dB short is encoded again with the rest of the gain through the limiter (`limiterGainDb` in the evidence); every cut goes through a limiter last, lowered until the decoded peak is under −1.5 dBFS (Opus overshoots a hard transient: the offline test's noise crash decoded at +2 dBFS from a −2 dBFS limit; `limitDb`, `decodedPeakDb` in the evidence). `dt audio music` caps the gain so the decoded peak stays at or under −1 dBFS (`MusicFiles.Gain`), recording what that leaves it short of −16 LUFS as `shortfallDb`; a track more than 3 dB short is left out. The test holds the same: −16 LUFS, or the recorded shortfall (at most 3 dB) with the peak what stops it, and never a peak over the top. (Run 2's "Habanera" was the Carmen prelude through the shared category, never a Habanera; none has turned up as CC0.) The job log ends with a short table (`--summary`: id, hit, jump, rank, weak, performers; the refusals, near misses and what became of each pinned file) right before the tests, and a failed test prints why.
