210. **The body under a headset (T82, roadmap M4 "VR body IK"; note 51's "not yet").** T47 gave the crew a headset player's arms, reaching from shoulders that never moved: a crewmate crouched for a crate stood bolt upright with their arms down through the roof, and turned their head round with their hips nailed in place.
    - **What's replicated.** The head's yaw and pitch already were (the sim's look follows the headset, note 30), and the hands (note 51). Its height wasn't: the tracking space hangs from the eye point, so a real crouch moved nothing in the sim. Now `PlayerIntent.Head` (the headset's height over the feet, `Eyes.Height` plus where the head is in the LOCAL space) rides with the hands, in the spare bits of the "other hand" byte plus an I16 of centimetres, and `PlayerState.Head` goes out on the player record (one more field, zero for a keyboard or a bot, so delta encoding makes it free). `TakeHand` takes it with the hands, held between kneeling (0.5 m) and `hand.overhead`; nothing in the sim acts on it. Remote players' heads are interpolated with their hands. Protocol 24.
      - **Reading: with the hands, not on its own.** Every free bit of the intent's three flag bytes is taken; the hand block's byte had seven. So a headset whose controllers have both dropped out stands its body straight until they're back.
      - The app now copies the other hand into the intent too: `VrLocomotion.Intent` built it (T43), but the app took only the reaching hand from it, so on the real path the host never had two hands for a heavy crate. `HandTests` drove `PlayerIntent.Reach` directly and never saw it.
    - **The spine** (`Game/VrBody.cs`, pure; `vr.json body`, our numbers, none being in the GDD or spec, note 40). A head under standing height (`Eyes.Height`, where the room hangs from) leans the torso over from the hips for the first `leanDrop` (0.25 m), to `leanDegrees` (35°); past that the knees bend, the hips going down by the rest (to `crouchMost`, 0.5 m). The head's yaw is the sim's; the hips keep theirs until the head is `yawDeadzoneDegrees` (35°) off them, then are dragged round to the deadzone's edge. The torso takes `twistShare` (0.6) of the turn between them, a third on each spine bone, and the neck the rest, nodding the head to the headset's pitch (to `nodDegrees`).
      - **Reading: a lower head is a lean first.** The height alone can't tell a bow from a crouch. Most of what a headset player does near the floor starts as a lean (reaching for a lever, a crate's handle), and a deep drop can only be a crouch, so the first quarter metre leans.
    - **The legs.** Each foot is planted in the frame of the car they stand in (the train moving under them moves nothing; a change of frame plants them afresh). A foot steps when the hips have moved its place `stepDistance` (0.2 m) off it or turned `stepTurnDegrees` (30°) from it: one foot at a time, the furthest out first (level, the one that didn't go last), over `stepSeconds` (0.28 s) in an arc `stepHeight` (9 cm) high, landing `stepOvershoot` past its place so creeping hips aren't chased. The crew model (`CreatureArt.HeadsetBody`) is turned to the hips and let down by the crouch, bent up the spine, and its legs reached to the ankles over the feet by the same two-bone IK as the arms (the knee forward of the hips and a little out), each foot turned back to how the clip had it, at its own yaw. The arms then reach from where the shoulders have gone.
      - **Only stood still.** Walking and running stay the clips' (above 0.4 m/s), and so does any act (the levers, the shovel, a hold). A headset crewmate's pace is now measured in their own car's frame, so riding a moving train isn't walking.
      - **Not yet:** hands that hold what they carry; sideways leans (only the height is sent, not where the head is over the feet, which the sim doesn't follow either). (The greybox box figure leaning: done in note 223.)
    - **Verified:**
      - `VrBodyTests` (8): every number is in vr.json; a lower head leans, then crouches, to the most a crouch goes; the hips wait inside the deadzone and follow past it, the short way round the half-turn, and a look round inside it never steps; a 90° turn steps two to four times and leaves both feet turned with the hips; creeping hips plant and step left, right, left, a planted foot never moving, the stepping one lifting to the step's height, and they settle square once stopped; the same head gives bit-identical bodies; the model-space feet and the hips' lag; and on the crew model, a crouch brings the figure down 0.3 m with its soles still on the floor, and a stepping foot is off it.
      - `HandTests.TheRestOfTheCrewSeeAHeadsetsHeadHeight`: over loopback another client sees the head's height, the headset's own prediction keeps it, and it's held at kneeling and gone without a hand. `VrLocomotionTests.TheHeadsHeightGoesWithTheHands`.
      - `dt screenshot --view crew --vr-body` (or `crewside`) stages three headset crewmates across car 2's roof (`Staging.Headsets`): one leant over reaching down ahead, head bowed; one crouched right down, knees bent and both hands by the roof; one whose head has turned 100°, the hips come round 65° and the torso twisted between, a foot halfway through its step (the stride lived up to that moment).
