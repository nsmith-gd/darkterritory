739. **Pinging the bots (A1, queue #473; the director, 14 Oct, `docs/playtest/2026-10-14-second.md` line 1).**
"Directing bots is kind of difficult. I think I'm going to want to use my middle mouse button to create some sort of context ping for them ... If I point at a task, like a car on fire, they should understand, okay, go deal with that fire. If I point to the cab controls, then it should understand that the cab controls are in need of being taken over ... If I point at loot ... If I point at the crane ... If I point at multiple things, it should understand that the bots should then work as a team to do the thing".

   - **GDD §12 first:** "Ping the bots".
   - **Part one (this): the ping, and two of its orders, the fire and the controls.** Part two: loot, the crane, the abandoned town.
   - **The ping is intent** (`PlayerMoves.Ping`, bit 5 of the moves byte, already on the wire):
     - Sent while the middle mouse is held; the host makes the ping on the press.
     - Not while held by something, nor seated or standing at a gun (there the button's the push, note 703), nor within `pings.cooldown` of the last.
   - **What's pinged** (`Sim/Player/Pings.cs`, the host's):
     - Of what a bot can be sent to, the thing nearest the middle of the view, within `pings.coneDegrees` (12) widened by its size and `pings.range` (60 m).
     - The candidates are a car on fire, the cab's controls (the regulator handle), loot, and a stop's crane.
     - Compared as cosines, ties to the ids, so it's deterministic.
   - **Seen by everyone:**
     - `World.Pings`, kept `pings.seconds` (20) and replicated as their own record kind (`RecordKind.Ping`, protocol 61).
     - On the HUD, a line each, newest first ("PRIYA: FIRE, CAR 2", yours as "YOU").
     - A mark on the world with what it is and how far, or an arrow at the screen's edge the way to it (`PingMarks`). `ScreenPoint.Of` was the creature gym's projection; the gym uses it from there now.
   - **Taken by a bot** (`CrewCalls.Pings`, `CrewCalls.Pings.cs`):
     - Each bot says what it is (walker, gunner, driver). Each untaken ping goes to the nearest free bot that can do it, and a ping taken once is never taken again. So several pings are shared out a bot each: the team the director asked for.
     - An order ends when its job's done, the bot's dead, or after `pings.orderSeconds` (120).
   - **The fire** (a walker's):
     - The pinged car's fire comes first in the walker's trouble.
     - The pinged walker is the fire's first hand (`ClaimFire`). Before, a gunner's legs at their gun on the burning car claimed the first hand and sat on, and the walkers stayed out.
   - **The controls:**
     - Pinged by whoever holds them, they're let go (`World.HoldControls(-1)`).
     - The driver bot, if alive, comes back to them (note 574's hand-over undone). Otherwise the walker who takes it goes forward as the relief (note 741's `DriverGone`).
   - **Checked** (`PingTests`):
     - What's in the middle of the view is pinged: the fire behind from a roof, the cab ahead, the regulator from in the cab, nothing out at the dark.
     - A car pinged on fire: the crew see it on every machine; one walker takes it, is at the car within 30 s, and is its first hand.
     - The controls pinged by their driver: they're let go, and a bot takes them.
     - Shot: `dt screenshot --hud --ping` (the controls marked in the cab, two fires behind as arrows at the edge, the lines).
   - **Found, not this note's:** a crew's walkers didn't put out a car fire in the tests' crew of four on a 4-car train, ping or not. They walked the roofs, and the pinged walker reached the car but didn't get the fire out in two minutes. It's the bots' fire-fighting (note 634's two hands), for its owner to look at; the ping gets the walker there first.
