1040. **A corrupted human's voice on the crew's radio (n1-R2, nathaniel1's Token Budget Manager's worker; queue #729; GDD Part Eleven q4; note 1026 rank 4, its Q1).** Built behind a flag, **off**, until the director answers note 1026's Q1 (the Director's Inbox ask `n1-A4-radio-voice`: wanted? whose voice? how often?).
   - **What it does, on.** Once a night at most, the Brakeman, hidden under the train (out of sight) a while with no living crewmate near him, says on the radio channel only a few words a crewmate said on the radio themselves earlier. Everyone with a set hears it as that crewmate on the radio (the set's click, static and flat voice), except the crewmate whose voice it is. Nothing about how it sounds gives it away. The night's report names it: "The Brakeman on the radio at km 9. In Priya's voice. Not said by them." It's ranked with what the night took, so it's kept when the report's lines are cut (note 416).
   - **Why the Brakeman** (Q1's "whose"; the Grumbler was the other name in note 1026). GDD §21 says the corrupted humans keep "fragments of learned behaviour", and the Brakeman is a railwayman still doing a railwayman's job, on the train itself. His rules already make the moment:
     - **Never with a crew of one** (`brakeman.minCrew` 2). There's always a friend's voice to take and someone else to hear it.
     - **Out of sight half his night** (`BrakemanMode.Hidden`, 30–60 s at a time under the cars). That's "out of sight" without inventing a body that isn't there.
     - **He's aboard**, the one corrupted human who rides the train. The radio is the train's own channel.
     - The Grumbler lives on facility cranes, scuttles like a spider and gnaws crates: an animal, not a man at his job. The Passenger can't do it: its one tell is that it never speaks (GDD §21 "RULE: make everyone speak"), and that stays.
   - **The flag and its numbers** (`content/tuning/enemies.json` `brakeman.radio`, `RadioVoiceTuning`):
     - `on` false.
     - `hiddenFor` 3: seconds hidden before he speaks, so he's gone from view first.
     - `farFrom` 26: no living crewmate this close to him. That's spec A.5's voice cutoff, so nobody near could have heard where it came from.
     - `minSeconds` 0.6 and `maxSeconds` 2.5: a few words, as note 1026 asked for (2–3 s), cut from the start of one utterance.
     - `minAge` 20 and `within` 900: words said at least 20 s ago (never an echo of what was just said) and within 15 minutes (note 1026's "a few words they said ten minutes ago").
     - Once a night is a rule, not a number (`World.RadioVoiceSaid`).
   - **How it works.**
     - **Listening:** `World.RadioVoices` is a second `VoiceMemory` (T40's, note 44), a minute of talk each. The host feeds it in `ForwardVoice` with every frame that actually went out on the radio from a living speaker. It leaves out what a GRAB keys open (App. C.8), because those are cries, not words. `VoiceMemory.Utterances` splits a player's frames into utterances.
     - **The choice:** `Enemies/RadioVoice.Choose`, host only, after the world's step. It needs a Brakeman hidden `hiddenFor` s with every living crewmate beyond `farFrom` of him, and two or more living crewmates. A voice qualifies if the crewmate has a radio utterance at least `minSeconds` long that ended between `minAge` and `within` ago, and someone else has a set to hear it. The pick (whose voice, which utterance) is a `Pcg32` seeded from the route's seed, the Brakeman's id and the tick. The same night, said the same way, says the same thing back.
     - **Once and the report:** `RadioVoice.Step` sets `World.RadioVoiceSaid` and adds the report's line, `IncidentKind.Heard`.
     - **The wire:** `HostSession.SayOnRadio` sends the words at the voice's 50 frames a second as `VoicePath.Mimic | VoicePath.Radio`, with the Brakeman's id as the source. The speaker byte is the crewmate's, so a listener who has muted them (note 724) hears none of it. It goes to every living crewmate with a set whose radio isn't dead where they stand (tunnels, mine spurs: `VoiceRouting.Route`'s own test), except the crewmate whose voice it is. That's T40's `VoicePath.Mimic` reused, with the radio's flag beside it. Protocol 93: a frame may carry both, and the report has the new kind.
     - **The client** (`VoiceChat`): a `Mimic | Radio` frame goes to a stream of its own, keyed by the thing (replayed frames would be stale to the crewmate's own decoder, as the Soot Children's were). It plays as `voice-radio`, with the set's key-down click, static and squelch (`RadioSet`), from nowhere.
     - **Determinism:** nothing here changes a player's or the train's state, so a predicting client never needs it, and the netcode tests are untouched. The choice is the host sim's, from the seed, the tick and what was said. Clients send only their voice, as before.
   - **Reading of the spec (ARCHITECTURE §8):** GDD Part Eleven q4 is still open. This note is the build's reading of it, and the director's answer replaces it. Note 44's "Not yet" (a radio lying on the floor as a way in, GDD §32 q4: the corrupted on the radio) is answered differently: the corrupted human is on the crew's own channel, not on a dropped set.
   - **Not done, on purpose:** more than once a night, another speaker, a radio on the floor, the crewmate hearing their own voice (it would give it away at once: the moment is the others asking them). Each is a number or a line if the director wants it.
