653. **Cars a dropped pin parts off are gone for and coupled back on (D1.4, queue #383; D1's ask after #371: "the crew coupled car 2 back but nobody went back for 4–5").** When a loose pin drops (note 356) and the train parts, the driver goes after the cars it left, ahead of the engine or behind it, and couples them back on.
    - **What happened on seed 7** (note 634's sweep; traced on main at 073745ed, `dt campaign play --bots 4`, with a rake probe, not committed):
        - At a switchyard stop (595–678 s) the train picked cars 4–5 off a siding. They come away ahead of the engine (note 187: every yard track's points face up the line), so the train ran on as 4,5 | engine, 1, 2, 3.
        - At 763.8 s, at 14.5 m/s, the pin behind car 5 dropped. It had been knocked loose coupling up at 5 km/h ("the knock worked the coupling's pin loose"). The pair ran on ahead.
        - A second later a Knotter took car 1's coupling. The driver stood for it; the crew killed it and the train backed onto cars 2–3 (note 365).
        - Nobody went after cars 4–5. They coasted on ahead at 10–14 m/s, past the train's stand at Fort Hensley, and were lost at the night's end with 1.7 car-loads in them.
        - So it wasn't the Knotter's parting. Nothing in the bots went for a rake a dropped pin had parted, ahead or behind: the driver's couple-up was only for a Knotter's cars behind it.
    - **The driver** (`ConductorBot.ForTheParted`, after the six's stands, `ForTheSix`):
        - **Ahead of the engine:** after them at the night's cruise, closing at what stops the engine at them over their own speed (`StopDriver.Toward`), aimed a little into them so they couple.
        - **Behind:** braked till they've stopped rolling after it, then backed onto them at the Knotter's couple-up speed, and the reverser forward again.
        - **Not gone back for:** on another track, rolling back faster than a lost cut (note 261), further back than the driver sets back for anyone (1,500 m), or with dawn too near for the run back and in (note 615's `DawnInHand`).
        - Only on the open line: a stop's moves have the train till they're done, and it's gone for after. Not the express driver (note 376).
        - **The coupler's lock.** A cut's coupler won't take the train till they've been 0.3 m apart (`TrainOnLine`'s lock on a parted rake). Run up on cars ahead still touching, the engine only pushed them along; backing onto cars that had run up against the tail only shoved them away down the line. So the engine draws 2 m clear first, then closes in.
    - **Told from what every client has.** The bots in an app-hosted night drive from a client's world, which doesn't have the host's record of a pin that dropped. The first version used that record: the tests passed and seed 7 didn't change. So the driver tells it from the train as every client sees it (`PinDropped`): the train come apart right behind a car whose pin was loose when last seen. A crew's cut (note 343) or a Knotter's isn't at a loose pin.
