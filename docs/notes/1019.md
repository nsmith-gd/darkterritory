1019. **A crewmate on speakers no longer sends the crew their own voices back: echo cancellation and an echo gate on the capture path (Wren, nathaniel2; queue #722; the director, 24 Oct: "My guest has a half second delay on their audio when they talk, they can hear their own voice and its maddening").**

## The diagnosis

There is no loopback in the code: the host forwards a voice frame to every listener but its speaker (`HostSession.ForwardVoice`
skips `listener == speaker`), a client plays only what the host sends it, and nothing plays the local mic back. (The Soot
Children replay a crewmate's voice to them on purpose, T40, now and then; that's the creature.) So it's **acoustic echo**: the
guest's voice comes out of the host's speakers, the host's open mic hears it, and it's sent back to the guest a round trip late
(the network both ways, the jitter buffer's 60 ms, a frame): half a second on a long link. `Ballast.Voice` had no echo canceller.

## What changed

- **`Ballast.Voice.EchoCanceller`**, managed (it ships wherever the game does; no native AEC3/speex to license and build per
  platform). On each 20 ms capture frame, before the voice activity and the leveller (`VoiceChat.Capture`):
    - A partitioned frequency-domain adaptive filter (MDF): 10 ms blocks, 1024-point FFTs, `tailSeconds` (0.3 s) of the room
      learned after the sound leaves the speakers, normalised per bin, one partition's gradient constrained a block in turn.
    - **Double talk**, so it never learns to cancel the speaker's own words: two filters, a background always learning and the
      foreground the mic is cleaned with, copied from the background only while the background takes out more. Once the room's
      learned, a copy also needs the foreground's error to be mostly echo (it cancels at least half the mic) or the background
      to be far better (the room changed); and each background bin learns by the echo's share of what the foreground leaves in
      it (the larger of the echo estimate's share and the error's coherence with it; a bin with no echo estimate yet learns at
      the full step). Without these the bench caught the background "predicting" the near voice where its harmonics met the far
      voice's, and copying it in: 43 dB of suppression fell to 11 dB after three seconds of both talking.
    - What the filter leaves is suppressed bin by bin by the echo's share of it, times `suppression` (3), down to `floorDb`
      (50% overlap, square-root Hann: the near voice alone comes through bit for bit, 10 ms late).
- **The reference** (`EchoReference`): the mixer's blocks as the audio thread queues them at the device, mixed to mono, and how
  much the device still holds, so the mic is lined up with what's coming out of the speakers now (not what's rendered ahead).
  The two are paired by sample count from the first frame; a slip past `resyncSeconds` (a hitch, a device lost) lines them up
  again and the room's relearned. It's the whole mix, so the train's own sound through the speakers comes out of the mic too.
- **The echo gate**: a frame more than `gateDb` (15) under the echo the filter heard in it is the room's echo of the far crew;
  the voice activity hears silence for it, so an open mic neither opens nor holds open on it (`VoiceChat.FramesEchoGated`).
- **Headphones recommended with OPEN MIC**: SETTINGS' VOICE line says so while it's OPEN MIC. (Not on the yard's first-nights
  card: any longer TALK row breaks in two at TEXT SIZE 1.5 in a 960x540 window and runs the card off the screen, TextSizeTests.)
- Tuning: player.json `voices.echo` (`EchoTuning`); `on: false` is the mic as it came.

## Measured

`dt voice echo [--delay s] [--rt60 s] [--coupling dB] [--latency s] [--seed n]` (`EchoBench`): crewmate A's voice out of B's
speakers into B's open mic, a room's delay and a sparse decaying tail on it; six seconds of A alone, three of B, three of both,
two of A again. Defaults (50 ms, RT60 0.3 s, echo 9 dB under what's played):

| | |
|---|---|
| echo taken out, A alone, 2 to 6 s | 33.7 dB |
| learned (a tenth of a second 20 dB down) | after 0.55 s of A talking |
| echo's room delay / back at A (with 0.1 s each way) | 0.05 s / 0.34 s |
| B alone kept | 0 dB (bit for bit) |
| B kept while both talk | -0.7 dB |
| echo taken out in the second after both talked | 36.9 dB |
| A-alone frames B's open mic sent: before / now | 100% / 0% |
| B-alone frames sent: before / now | 100% / 100% |

A short loud room (20 ms, -6 dB coupling) 44 dB; a long quiet one (100 ms, RT60 0.3 s, -16 dB) 30 dB. `EchoCancellerTests` pins
30 dB (25 in the other rooms), learned within a second, B alone within 0.5 dB, B over A within 2 dB, 30 dB again after double
talk, and the gate shut on the echo (at most 3% of frames) and open on B. Cost: 3.6% of a core in Release (about 0.4 ms of a
90 fps frame).

## Left

- Until the room's learned (about half a second of the far crew talking) the first of the echo can still get through, and an
  extreme room (speakers as loud at the mic as what's played, `--coupling -3`) leaves 25 dB and opens the gate on some frames
  while learning. A room longer than `tailSeconds` (delay plus tail past 0.3 s) is cancelled only in part.
- No comfort noise: a bin suppressed to the floor goes quieter than the room around it.
- The canceller runs only where the app has speakers and a mic (`VoiceChat.Played`); `dt voice bench` doesn't route through it.
