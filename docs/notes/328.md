328. **The threat orchestrator, and the run between stops (D1.2 for D1, queue #67; GDD App. F.3, the director, 7 Oct 2026: "on the train, still relatively boring from point A to point B ... more threats that can board the train at speed ... tower defense style that gives our gunners things to do ... a threat orchestrator that takes into account how many players are on").** The design outline is [docs/design/orchestrator.md](design/orchestrator.md), linked from GDD App. B.1.
    - **Why a fast train is boring, by the numbers:** the train's top speed is 22 m/s, the Cinder Hounds can't board above 19 and the Climbers get a grip only under 14 (note 286); the rest come at stops. Kept hot and never stopped, only the Car Hugger (lying in wait on low ground) can land, which is the director's playtest. App. F.1's boarding-first decision made the hauling train safe on purpose and left "a fast, flying class" for later.
    - **The outline:** an orchestrator over the director (a census each second of who's active, at which post, and how long since each had something to answer; a live crew multiplier, per-player caps, and the post with the most slack favoured); upkeep jobs while the train runs (U1–U6, hot boxes, loose couplings, lamps, powder to the guns); threats that board at speed (S1–S5); waves at the guns (§5.3); pacing targets P1–P8; the director's changes D1–D7; and five questions for the director, the first being §15's "scaling by train length, not enemy count multipliers", which scaling by active players changes.
    - **Built, the first piece: the hound run** (orchestrator.md §5.3, §6.1; enemies.json `director.run`; `Director.Runs`, `CinderHound.Runner`). A train that has run 2.4 km at 19 m/s or more since it last slowed under 2 m/s (×0.75 with the boiler over its working band) draws a stream of Cinder Hounds: round(1.4 + 0.6 × the crew alive), 2 to 6 (2 at crew 1, 3 at 2, 4 at 4, 6 at 8), in pairs 6 s apart on alternating flanks, 200 m behind the rear car. A runner howls (the pack's 4 s telegraph), then closes at the train's speed + 5 m/s whatever that is, and leaps aboard the rear car from 30 m as the director's hounds do, at any speed (the director's packs can't board above 19 m/s; the train makes 22). About 38 s a pair from howl to leap. **Why 19:** it's the director's packs' own top speed: under it they board, and outrunning them stays their counter (App. F.1, Decided: "outrunning them means going fast, which risks derailing"; note 286's table). The run is what answers going faster than that, the top-speed strategy App. F.1 left for later, so running hot is a choice between the bends and the guns.
    - **Answered one at a time:** a ball landing within 5 m of a runner scatters it (break off), and one on it kills it (note 290), so a ball between a pair (4 m apart across, 3 m along) takes both. A round fired near but not at it doesn't: the director's packs keep `suppressRounds` (one round anywhere within 220 m sends a pack off). `EnemyContext.Landed` is the tick's ball landings (not one stopped by the train's own body). Missed, a runner is the pack fight aboard the rear car (App. A.3, staying aboard: note 269).
    - **Where it sits:** outside the budget and the caps, like the left-behind's hunts (note 273): the fast train's own. Nothing in the grace, a fort, the final approach or the terminus' safe stretch, at a stop or with the run not under way; one run at a time (the count starts again once its last runner is dealt with), and a train that slows mid-run sends no more of its pairs. Its own dice (stream 0x4A11), so the night's other draws are untouched. Host only: `Runner` isn't replicated; a client draws a runner as any hound.
    - **Headless:** `dt harness ... --enemies` reports `threats.houndRuns` (each run's time, km, size, crew, hot, and its runners scattered, killed and aboard). Seen: `dt screenshot --run --view run` (three pairs closing on the rear car and its gun, off the line's left; the fog hides a hound past about 40 m, so the staged pairs are closer than a real run's).
    - **Verified:** `HoundRunTests`: a 21 m/s train with four aboard draws a run of four after the grace, both flanks, every runner boarding at 21 m/s when nobody shoots, and no second run while they're aboard; the size at crews 1, 2, 4 and 8; a ball landing between a pair scatters both, one landing 150 m off doesn't; no run at 18 m/s (under the packs' 19), 12 m/s or at a stand; slowing starts the count again. `TheGunnerAtTheGuardGunAnswersTheRun`: the gunner bot at the guard van's gun, a run of two behind a 21 m/s train: 7 rounds, both killed on the line, none aboard. A 4-bot harness night (frontier:7, 900 s) drew no run: the bots haul at cruise (14 m/s) and stop, which is the point; the harness has no top-speed driver yet. The Sim suite, NetcodeTests included.
