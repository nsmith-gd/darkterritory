288. **Driven off by their rules, killed by the crew together (queue #25, A1.10; GDD App. F.1's damage model, "creatures mostly don't take damage; they're driven off by their rules", and the director's clarification of 7 Oct 2026: "They should be driven off by rules but they should also be able to be killed like in Lethal Company if the team coordinates effectively").** Note 272's follow-up listed the creatures still killed by blows. The five whose counter was "club it" (the Grumbler, the Gaunt, the Passenger, Climbers and the Choir's ghosts) now have two ways out. Following the rule drives one off: the normal answer, a break, and it can come back. A coordinated team can kill it, which is riskier and costlier, and rewarded: it's done for the night and the incident report names the gang.
    - **The coordinated kill** (`enemies.json` `coordinatedKill`: `gang` 2, `windowSeconds` 5). Each creature's blows wear it down only while two or more different crewmates have struck it within the window (`Enemy.Strikers`, `Ganged`), plus whatever setup its rule asks for. A lone player swinging never kills one. Killed, `Enemy.Slay` writes a `Slain` incident line ("Killed the Grumbler together … By Ana, Ben.") and, for a kind that comes once (the Grumbler, the Gaunt, the Passenger), adds it to `World.Slain`; the director doesn't draw that kind again tonight. A Climber or a ghost is one of many, so a kill takes only that one. Driven off, the Passenger and the talked-down Gaunt go into `World.DrivenOff`, and `SpawnContext.Once` lets that once-a-run kind come again.

    - **Readings.** The Passenger's "found out" is a blow. The truer "make everyone speak" would be a crewmate calling it out (looking at it and talking), but nothing in the voice layer says who's addressed, so a blow stands in for it. The 2.5 s it stands unmasked is the gang's one chance. Without it, a Passenger in the rearmost car was off the back before a second crewmate could swing. The Gaunt's `talkingCalms` answers half of GDD Part Eleven Q9 ("does talking lower its aggro") yes, as a tuning flag. A ball (note 290: a blow by the gunner) rides each creature's blow rule, so a gunner alone is one striker; a ball and a crewmate's blow together are a gang.
    - **Bots.** `Heed.Rescue` no longer swings at a Choir ghost holding someone: its hush (`Heed.Voice`) is the rescue, and a swing only earns the hit back. Everything else already answers by the rule: they go to a held crewmate together (two of the crew drive a Grumbler or a Climber off, a blow drives the Passenger off), and they talk near the Gaunt, which now talks it down.
    - **Presentation.** The cues follow (`PrototypeSession.Cue`): the Passenger's "hit it and it runs" and "found out: it's running for the back", the Grumbler's "backing off: let it be", the Gaunt's "leaving: if it took something, run it down". A ghost let go by quiet is seen leaving as a dispersing one is (`GreyboxScene.Leaving`).
    - **Director's calls.** (1) A ball alone can no longer kill a Climber on the roofs; it knocks it off instead (§21 says no cannon answers the flank; note 171 let it). (2) The Passenger's unmasking by a blow rather than by a voice. (3) Q9: talking lowers the Gaunt's anger (`talkingCalms`), and a talked-down Gaunt may wake again that night. (4) The window and the gang (2 within 5 s) are one number for all five; at a crew of two, the kill takes both of them. (5) Whether a coordinated kill should earn something in the settlement (scrip, a named commendation) beyond the report line: commendations are player-to-player today (D.12). (6) Found in the harness, before and after alike: the bots never answer the Passenger. It takes walkers who are 84–99 m from anyone, and nothing in `Heed` makes a bot keep company or count heads, so one blow would now save them and none lands. That's bot work for a follow-up.
