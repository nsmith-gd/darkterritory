367. **Hotbox: an axle parasite that seizes a car (G1, queue #104; the director's brief of 8 Oct 2026; design `docs/design/creatures/hotbox.md`; GDD §21, A.7, B.7; spec B.19).**
    - **Its own state, not the hot box's** (D1's note 331): the heat is the creature's (`Extra`), its knock and glow its modes; the seized axle is the car's (`Vehicle.Seized`, replicated; protocol 44), held over `seizedTopSpeed` by a drag of `seizedHold` times the engine's pull (`TrainOnLine.CreatureDrag`), the same on every machine. Never a fire, never a derailment; grease doesn't touch it. A seized axle is freed by a wrench held `repairSeconds` at one of the car's trucks (`Axles.At`), or the car's cut.
    - **Exposed at a stand** (`exposeAfter`): killed by blows, or prised (a crowbar or wrench held `priseSeconds`); it bites `snapDamage` (the damage model's least hit, note 272) every `snapEvery` as the spine's telegraph and commit.
    - **Verified:** `HotboxCreatureTests` (7; `HotBoxTests` are D1's hot box).
    - **The bots answer the six (G1.9 for G1; the director, 8 Oct 2026: "have the bots handle the six new creatures too"; notes 362–367 and their design pages).** Each answer is the counter-play its design page gives, read off what a client has of the creature: its mode, where it is, and a car's `Wound`, `Seized` and `Knot`. The walkers' and the gunner's answers are `Heed` filters (`Bots/Heed.Six.cs`), wired into `BotCrew.Think` after the coupling and before the rescue, so a rescue still beats them. They don't apply to the driver, nor to a walker gone forward to take the controls (note 399). The driver's part is `ConductorBot.ForTheSix` (`Bots/ConductorBot.Six.cs`), placed after the Track Doll and Switchman blocks. Anything that takes a bot off the train does so only with the train standing under it: `StopHand.Afoot` gets it off a car, a roof, a gap or the cab the way a stop's hands get down, then on foot. Once it's done, or the train moves off, the walker's own legs climb back aboard. (Kept down by an errand while the train moved, a bot would have the driver set back for it, and go out to it again.) The numbers are `enemies.json` `crewBots`: the bots' judgement, not the creatures' own numbers.
        - **The Mourners** (`Heed.Mourners`). A bot that can get down, within `mournersWithin` of a body they're about (not one being carried), goes to it. Once it's over the body they drop it and start back. It runs straight at the nearest of them within `mournersLeash` of the body, the haulers first: they back off at `scatter`, under a run. One blow kills one and scatters the rest. With none near, it stands over the body.
        - **Tower Jaw** (`Heed.TowerJaw`). Within `towerJawWithin` of it at its post, a bot gets down and clubs it: four blows inside its window drive it off, and it bites but never kills. Once it's gnawed past `towerJawClearFrom` it's left to fall, and a bot keeps `crushRadius` + `towerJawClearBy` off the line nearest it (where the coaling tower falls, as the wreck heap's groaning has the stop's hands keep clear, note 187). The host's fall point isn't sent, so the gantry's fall is approximated by the line too.
            - **Its wreck** across the main line (`Heed.WreckOnTheLine`, from its sent position and line distance; `Blocks` isn't sent). The driver stops short of it on the Track Doll's braking curve to `wreckStopShort` short of its near edge, on the brake from `wreckHoldWithin` beyond that. The bots within `wreckWithin` get down and hold Use beside it until it's cleared. Then everyone's aboard, and on.
        - **The Brakeman** (`Heed.Brakeman`). Among the bots on the roofs, the two lowest ids are the pincer: the lower takes the front, the other the back. While he's hidden, each keeps its end of the train, `PostIn` in from the roof's edge. He comes up at an end, so he comes up between them. Once he's seen, each closes from its side to `brakemanStandOff` off him: he runs from the nearer into the other, and when both are within his `cornerSpan` he's cornered and they club him. Every other roof bot, and a lone one, unwinds his cars: along the roofs to the car's brake wheel, and Use held there only while the car's wound (an unwound car's wheel turns the whole rake's handbrakes). It keeps holding past the unwind a little, so a late snapshot can't make it press again.
        - **The Knotter** (`Heed.Knotted`, `Heed.Knotter`, `ConductorBot.ForTheSix`).
            - Its gap is never jumped nor stepped down into (`WarmUp.CanJumpGap`; `StopHand.AlongRoofs`). The roof walker turns back at it. The warm-up never drops onto its plate nor leaves a car onto it (`WarmUp.Barred`, `WayOut`). The hot box and the loose pin in its gap are left (`RoofWalkerBot.Grease`).
            - The driver stops once it's forced its gap (Force, Taut, a coil). At the stand it's slack, and the bots get down and club it from the ground beside its gap, on their own side of the train.
            - Killed, it leaves the rear rake standing the knot's metres back. Once everyone's aboard, the driver backs onto it at half `coupleMaxSpeed` and couples (the stop's set-back onto its cut), then puts the reverser forward and goes on.
            - Its coil is broken by a friend at the gap (`Heed.Rescue`'s `KnotRescue`): Use held by the held one, or from the roof of the car in front down its end ladder toward them. The generic rescue's `Strike` is left as it was: from either roof the rope's middle is off the car (its `halfLength + 2`), and walking a bot off a roof's end into a knotted gap would be the slip itself.
        - **The Freight Beetle** (`Heed.Beetle`). A bot on foot within `beetleWithin` clubs it until it's driven off its load. The stop's crate hands leave the crate it has (`StopHand.Pushed`).
        - **Hotbox** (`Heed.Hotbox`, `ConductorBot.ForTheSix`). The driver stops once it glows or has seized its axle. At the stand it unfolds, and the nearest bot nobody's beaten to it gets down with a crowbar or the wrench to hand. It stands off it on the ground, halfway between its bite's `snapReach` and the prise's `priseReach`, and holds Use until it's prised out: prised from the side, it's never bitten. With no tool it's clubbed instead (six blows; it snaps). A car left seized is freed next: the wrench at the nearest of its trucks from beside it (`Axles.At`). The driver stands for that no longer than `standGiveUp`, nor for a Hotbox or a Knotter nobody deals with in that time (nobody to: it's gone on from for good). The express driver (note 376) stops only for the wreck, and a driver mid-way through a stop's moves doesn't stop for these (its stand is the one they need).
        - **The look-out** (note 212) walks to Tower Jaw (`look.towerJawM`) and the Freight Beetle (`look.beetleM`) on an insisted night. The other four come to the train, or only after a death.
        - **Not done (simpler versions):**

            - The Brakeman's pincer is the two lowest ids, not the two nearest. Each holds an end post while he's hidden, so with two roof bots nobody unwinds until he's dead. A bot on his wrong side when he shows has to pass him, and he hides and comes up again between the posts.

    - **The test host's memory.** The Game tests' host held about 12 GB on Linux (lavapipe's device memory beside the managed heap). Main was already at the edge of the CI runner's 16 GB, and the six's six atlases took it over: the runner was shut down mid-run on every push. Three changes fixed it, and no test was dropped:
        - The six's atlases are 1024 squares, the roster's size, so none joins the 2048 tier.
        - The looks loaded in one process share the textures they decode (`Look.Decoded`, keyed by path and checked against the file's time and size). Each test class's static look used to keep its own copy of about 1.2 GB for the whole run.
        - The creature turntables share one renderer, held by the class's fixture (`CreatureArtTests.TurntableRig`) and let go after the class's last test, instead of one renderer per model.
        - In a sandbox where main's Game tests are killed at 11.6 GB after 4.5 minutes, these pass all 977 in 12 minutes.
