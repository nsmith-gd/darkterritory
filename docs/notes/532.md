532. **The host's network off its frame loop (N2, queue #270; the netcode audit's gap 2, note 530).** Every transport was polled once a sim tick inside the window's frame loop (`FixedStepClock`, at most 8 ticks a frame), so a host under 4 fps fell behind for good and the crew rubber-banded on its frame rate, a stall of more than the 8 s timeout dropped everyone (D1's note 531 took the case of a load before the first frame), and the HUD's ping was a frame-time reading (note 450's 3,167 ms).
    - **The pump.** `DatagramTransport.StartPump(ms)` runs a thread of the transport's own that receives, acks, resends, pings and times out between the ticks (`Service`, under the one lock every public member now takes); `Poll` on the sim's tick still reads what arrived. A UDP carrier waits on its socket (`IDatagramCarrier.Wait`), so a datagram wakes it at once; Steam's carrier sleeps the interval (its calls are thread-safe). The app starts it on every transport it makes (`NetPlaySession.Pumped`: the host's UDP and Steam, its own player's, its bots', a joiner's and each redial); the tests and the harness don't, so their timelines stay their ticks. With the pump, note 531's "time nobody listened" never happens.
    - **The link's numbers are tuning:** player.json `link` (`LinkTuning`): `timeoutSeconds` 15 (was the transport's own 8: a wifi blip's worth, and a redial costs a body and a reserve), `keepaliveSeconds` 1, `pumpMilliseconds` 4. A test's transports keep their `DatagramOptions`.
    - **The ping is the link's.** The pump sends and answers the pings, so the round trip the HUD shows is measured between frames, whatever the frame takes.
    - **A host that's behind slows the night (protocol 45; 44 went to D1's vehicle record while this waited).** The snapshot says how many of the client's inputs the host still holds beyond the one it applied (`ClientSession.HostQueued`). Over `paceQueued` (4) for `paceSeconds` (1), the client's `Pace` is 1 + `paceSlow` (1.05) until the host's down to one or none; the app stretches its `FixedStepClock` by it (`Stretch`: a tick takes 5 % longer of real time), so the client sends fewer inputs a second and the host, which used to throw the oldest away past `MaxQueuedInputs` (8), catches up. The sim never reads the pace: prediction is untouched (`NetcodeTests.AClientPacesItselfToAHostThatsBehind`). It also settles two clocks that drift (a client's crystal a fraction fast), which filled the queue the same way.
    - **The host is told.** `FixedStepClock.DroppedSeconds` counts the time the spiral guard threw away; the app hands it to `NetPlaySession.ClockDropped` each frame, and any of it in the last five seconds puts "YOUR MACHINE IS HOLDING THE CREW BACK" on the host's lobby panel and, out on the line, under the corner's ping (`LinkInfo.HeldBack`); a paced joiner reads "THE HOST'S BEHIND: THE NIGHT RUNS AT ITS PACE" (`Paced`). `dt screenshot --hud --held-back`.
    - **Tests:** `UdpTransportTests.APumpedLinkOutlivesAFrozenFrameLoopAndMeasuresItsOwnPing` (nobody polls for three timeouts: both links live, a loopback-sized ping, reliable messages after; a peer whose pump stops is still dropped in time), `FixedStepClockTests` (the guard's count, the stretch), the netcode test above.
    - **Not yet:** a host's own tick isn't moved off the frame loop (the sim runs where it renders); a host whose frames can't hold 30 Hz still drops time, and everyone's night runs at its pace, which is now said rather than suffered as rubber-banding.
