"I think we're gonna need some performance logging. A way that I can send you ... a build report or a performance
report." Before this, a build wrote only frames over 100 ms that are also 4× the median (note 549's `FrameWatch`) to the launch
log, and the director's stutters (lines 1–3: the talk prompt near townsfolk, turning fast, coming back into town) are
shorter than that, so they leave no trace. The report goes where the director's notes already go: the one
`test-reports.txt` in the game's `logs` folder (note 677), so sending it is sending the same file.

   - **What it records** (`FrameWatch`, GDD §34.3). Every frame of a night goes into a 1 ms histogram, for the night and for its minute.
     - A **hitch** is a frame at least 2× the median of the last 120 and at least 25 ms. It's kept with its parts (input, sim, audio/hud, scene, hud/upload, render/present), the part that led it, the garbage collector's work and allocations, how fast the view was turning, and where the night was.
     - It also keeps what the frame counted (`FrameCounts`): scenery cells built on the frame instead of a worker (`cells cooked here`, `cells waited for`, from `WorldArt.Cells`), and meshes uploaded for the first time (`GreyboxRenderer.Uploaded`).
     - Note 549's slow-frame lines in the launch log are unchanged.
   - **The frame's parts with their memory:** each part records what the main thread allocated in it (`GC.GetAllocatedBytesForCurrentThread`). The scene's own laps (`GreyboxScene.Lap`, `dt perf`'s parts) are parts of the frame when `GreyboxScene.Watch` is set, so a hitch reads "scene: town houses 9 (10.1 MB)" rather than "scene 9". The slow-frame lines carry the frame's counts. `WorldArt.HousePiece` counts the house meshes it builds.
   - **The night's report** (`FrameWatch.Report`) is written when a night ends or is left. It has:
     - p50, p90, p99, p99.9 and the longest frame;
     - the hitches, counted by the part that led them, and how many came while turning fast (over 180°/s);
     - memory: MB allocated and the collections per generation;
     - the audio pump's gaps;
     - a histogram in the frame rates a player knows (120 down to under 4 fps);
     - each part of the frame's average ms and MB, past the night's first frames;
     - the night minute by minute (frames, p50, p99, hitches, slow frames, and where at the minute's start);
     - the worst dozen hitches in full.
     It goes in the launch log in every build. In a dev build (`#if DEVTOOLS`) it also goes on the end of `logs/test-reports.txt`, as "the night's performance" (`TestReports.Add(time, what, text)`).
   - **With every note** (F8, F9): `FeedbackNotes.Frames` asks `FrameWatch.Story` for the last 60 s (percentiles and its hitches in full). The note's folder gets it as `frames.txt`, and the note's entry in `test-reports.txt` carries it after the report.
   - **Checked:**

What the frame counted goes with its hitch. The report's percentiles, bands, minutes and worst hitches are right. A second or more reads "≥1000". A note's story is the last minute only.
     - `TestReportsTests`: a note's frames sit beside it and in the file, and the night's report goes on the end under its own rule.
