524. **Live control and shots by subject (W1, queue #261).** An agent could look at the game only through one-shot commands: a night run start to finish (`dt playthrough`, `dt harness`), a recording replayed to a tick (note 515), a camera placed by hand in line coordinates. Now `dt mcp` is a local MCP server an agent's client starts on stdio, and the night stays open between calls: step it, read it, say what comes, look at it, step on. A shot can be framed on what it's of (`subject:gaunt`) instead of where the camera goes.
    - **The server** (`DarkTerritory.Dev/Live/McpServer.cs`): JSON-RPC 2.0, one message a line (MCP's stdio transport), by hand on System.Text.Json, no new packages. It answers `initialize` (protocol 2025-06-18; 2025-03-26 and 2024-11-05 if asked for), `ping`, `tools/list` and `tools/call`, and takes notifications without a word. A line that isn't JSON, an unknown method or tool, or an argument of the wrong type, not taken, or missing is a JSON-RPC error; whatever a tool throws is that call's failed result (`isError`), in words the caller's model can act on, and the server goes on. stdout is the protocol's alone: `dt mcp` sends the console's other output to stderr.
    - **The tools** (`LiveTools`, over one `LiveSession`):
        - `night_start` hosts a night headless as the app does (`NetPlaySession.HostGame`, on loopback, unlisted): a bot crew aboard, the host's own player standing idle. `record: true` records it as a dev build does (note 515), to `out/recordings`.
        - `night_step` steps by seconds or ticks, sooner `until` an enemy kind is about, a new enemy, an incident, a death or a stop at a facility, and at the run's end. It returns the tick, the train, the run and the next stop, the crew (post as the census counts it, car, health), the threats (kind, phase, metres from the train's bodywork), and what happened since the last step (the director's spawns, the incident log's lines).
        - `night_state`: the same in full (each car, the director's pressure, budget and posts, every incident, where everyone is).
        - `night_insist`: the spawns control, only these kinds from now on (`World.Insist`, note 186); an empty list hands the night back to the director; a replay refuses it.
        - `replay_open` opens a recording to a tick or m:ss; the other tools then read the replayed world and `night_step` plays on.
        - `shot` draws the world as it stands (`WorldShot`) and says what it framed: saved whole under `out/live/`, handed back at most 960 wide (a 1280 shot comes back at 640), so a client's context isn't spent on pixels the file has. The crewmate whose eyes an `eye:N` shot is from is left out, as in `dt replay` (note 516).
        - `dt` runs any other dt command as a child of this one (the same executable, edition and mods).
    - **Shots by subject** (`SubjectCamera`, through `WorldShot.Aim`, so `dt replay --shot --view subject:…` has it too):
        - Names: `subject:<kind>` (the nearest active one, the kind as an agent writes it: `gaunt`, `cinderHounds`, `fire flies`), `enemy:<id>`, `crew:<id>`, `car:<n>`, `engine`.
        - The subject is a box: a car's bounds in its frame; a crewmate's height and girth from player.json; an enemy's body spheres from enemies.json, stooped as it stoops. The camera stands off it by what it takes to get every corner of the box into 80 % of the frame at that aspect: three-quarters on from ahead, 15° up, on the side of the line it's on. Of 18 places round it (ahead, behind, square on; its side, then the other; 15°, 35°, 60° up) it takes the first that's clear: out of the ground, inside a tunnel's lining, out of the train's bodywork, no car's box across the sight line, the land not over it (a cutting's bank). Inside a car or the cab, it's down the room at it, at a standing eye.
        - A subject that isn't there is an error that says what is ("no trackDoll in the world; there's engine, car:1..car:6, …; crew:1, crew:2, crew:3, crew:4; no threats about"), never a frame of something else. One no place can see (under the ground) gets a note with its shot.
    - **Recorded nights stay replayable with an insist in them.** An insist is done to the host between its steps, where its transport can't hear it, so a recorded night parted from its replay at the director's next think (found by hand: `divergedAt` 1830, after an insist at 1812). It's now noted in the recording (`insist`) and the replay applies it before the next step, as note 515 does with the host player.
    - **What it costs** (Debug build, lavapipe, a shared 4-core machine): 60 s of frontier:7 with three bots, 10.7 s. The renderer, made once a night (the look and its textures), 22–39 s; each shot after that 1.3–1.6 s. A shot handed back at 640×360 is 0.5–0.7 MB of base64, against 2.3 MB at 1280×720.
    - **Found with it:** the Gaunt's spawn (B.6), put down off a crewmate on foot where the village has no roost, takes the crewmate's height, not the ground's (`SpawnContext.Out` keeps `centre.Y`). On frontier:7 at km 2 it lay asleep 4.9 m inside the hillside, out of sight from anywhere; `shot subject:gaunt` says so in its note. Not fixed here: it's the sim's (a gameplay agent's to take).
    - **Verified:** `LiveControlTests` (6): the protocol end to end over in-memory streams (initialize, the notification unanswered, tools/list, night_start, a 5 s step, night_state with the tick moved by 150 and the crew listed, an insist refused on a night without threats); every error a client can cause answered, the tools' own failures as results, the server still answering after; every subject (the engine, cars, each crewmate, a Gaunt put down off car 2) projected through its camera into the middle of the frame, its whole box in the frame and reaching at least halfway to its edge, the camera out of the bodywork and over the ground; an absent subject an error naming what's there, before anything's drawn; a recorded night with an insist replayed tick for tick; enemy kinds read as written. By hand: scripted stdio sessions with `dt mcp` and `dotnet run … -- mcp` (stdout clean even when it builds first); frontier:7 with three bots and threats on, cinder hounds and ribbits insisted on and stepped `until` them; the shots of the engine, a cinder hound, a ribbit pack, the driver down the cab, a walker jumping down between cars, cars 3 and 6, each in the middle of the frame and whole; its recording replayed tick for tick through `replay_open`, and `dt replay --to 2011 --shot --view subject:ribbit` drew the same frame as the live shot. On #694's branch (W1): car 3 framed with two crewmates on its roof, and an absent Track Doll answered with what's there.
    - **Not yet:** the running windowed app isn't driven: stdio is a process's own, so the app needs its own channel (a local socket under `#if DEVTOOLS`); the night here is the app's headless twin. Nothing moves the crew (the host's player stands idle, the bots do as bots do); a night moves only when it's stepped; lineside props aren't counted as in a shot's way; one night at a time.
    - **Only in the sessions that need it** (the director, 9 Oct, in the inbox: "Not every session for live control, only some"): no project `.mcp.json`. A session that wants it adds it: `claude mcp add dt -- dotnet run --project src/DarkTerritory.Cli -- mcp`.
