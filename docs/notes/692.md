   - **The forms** (`Towns.TownForms`; tuning/towns.json `forms`, world/towns.json `forms`). From the director's renders, named for the Maritimes:
     - **harbour** ("Breakwater Town"; Lunenburg, Louisbourg): the inner harbour inside the sea wall on the platform's side, the wharf along the yard with its piers out over the water (decks), the breakwater round the basin with its light, the slip and the boat yard by the way in, the fish stores (its works) between the platform and the wharf; behind the square the lanes and the head, a terrace with the lighthouse and the seamen's chapel.
     - **dykeland** ("Fenfolk"; the Tantramar, Grand-Pré's aboiteaux): salt marsh inside a palisade-shaped octagon, the line on its dyke, houses on hummocks along boardwalks (decks) out from the dyke, the market hummock by the square, the chapel on the high hummock, the marsh watch, the hay barn and the sluice house. The marsh is waded (0.4 m), not railed.
     - **quarry** ("Pitborn"; Springhill, Shelburne granite): the pit beside the square, three benches 5 m apart down to its floor, houses on every bench facing in, a ramp winding down them, the lift and the derrick, the crane yard (works) and the workers' rows across the line, the founders' row on the rim.
     - **tidewater** ("Drowned Boroughs"; the Fundy tide): the drowned ward under 1.4 m of water on the platform's side, its lost houses standing in it, causeways out to the wharf islands and their staging; above the square's side the blocks climbing 4 m a street to the chapel hill (+14 m) and the high ward (+22.4 m), a stair up to each from the top tier, the ridge behind them past the wall; the salvage crane and yard.
     - **glasshouse** ("Glass Gardeners"; the Annapolis Valley under glass): an eight-sided wall, the long glasshouse ranges across the line, round reservoirs, the beds in plots behind the square, the gardeners' rows, the seed house.
     - The **street town** (note 335) stays, a form among them (`""`).
     Each form's town is drawn from its trade (`forms.byTrade`) on its own stream (`form`), so the town arrived at and the town left from are the same form; `forms.force` makes every town one form (to play or look at one type; `dt screenshot --form`, `dt town map --form`).
   - **How a form is laid** (`TownForms.Planner`): in the town's rail frame from its way out (u metres along the line from the exit gate, d across), the square on the left by the way out and the home platform on the right as every town has them. A 1 m grid says what's taken (the wall's walk margin, the line, the square, ways, works, houses), so nothing laid later stands on anything before. A form gives: its wall's outline (its two gates where it crosses the line); its districts (`TownDistrict`: quarter, water, deck, terrace, plots, yard; outline, level over the yard's, depth for water; each clipped to the wall); its ways (`TownWay`: quays, boardwalks, ramps with a level a point, causeways); the walled town's streets and lanes where it has them (`TownStreet`, `TownLane`, so the art and what's underfoot read them as before); its green and its works (`TownGreen`, `TownWorks`); its landmarks (fixtures: lighthouse, beacon, chapel, watchtower, lift, crane, boatshed, seedhouse, glasshouse, with their words); and its lots (`TownForms.Lot`: middle, side, frontage, turn so its front faces its way, the yard's edge and back where it's square to the line, its level). Every lot is checked against the finished ground: dropped where a later district flooded or raised it, or a way runs through it. The households, open houses (the first lots square to the line), yards, people and words are the walled town's (`TownGenerator.Houses` takes the form's lots); the drowned lots are only ever the lost's.
   - **Two gates** (`Fortresses.Runs`, `Towers`, `Round`): the wall runs round the outline (or the street town's rectangle), open where it crosses the line at both gates; a gatehouse (two towers and the arch) stands at the way in from the last night (`TownBounds.Rear`) as at the way out (`Gate`). `TownPlan.Frame` (`TownFrame`: `Entry`, `Exit`, `Yard0`, `Yard1`) says where they are and where the longest consist stands (towns.json `forms.yardM` 330 m: 20 cars of 14 m, an engine of 20, 1.5 m between; `shortOfGate` 8, run.json's `departShortOfGateM`). Every form is 520 to 600 m between its gates: the yard with room. The street town's rear wall is at the yard's start now (`walled.rear` 0, was −8).
   - **The same town arrived at and left from, the same way round** (`Town.AtTerminus`, `Town.Origin`, `Fort.Origin`, `RailLine.From`): the terminus's town stands in a frame from `GateM − Frame.Entry` along the line, not turned round (note 600 turned it). The train comes in at its way in (its rear wall's gate, on the terminus's `GateM`, where home, the Wakers' stop and the yard-limit board already were), and its way out is a yard's length on, at most the end of the line (`_end`, the terminus's gate plus the departure yard, as note 600 laid it). Its square is on the same side as the departure's. **Seamless:** the frame's origin and tangent are the same in both nights (`Town.Anchor()`, its straight frame through its way in), so the next night's line laid from the arrival's origin would carry the same town in the same place; what a seamless hand-over would still need is listed below.
   - **Its ground** (`Towns.TownGround`, laid by `Town.Lay` over the line's `TerrainField.Lay`): the plan's districts and ways as a 1 m raster in the town's frame: what's stood on (`Level`: terraces, benches, ramps, deck planks, a basin's bed), the land (`Bed`), the water (`Water`). `TerrainField.Ground` asks it inside the town (a bounding-box test first), so the player motor, bodies and everything that asks what's stood on stand on it; the guns' water asks `TerrainField.TownWater` first. The land itself (`Height`, `WaterAt`) stays the plan's: the lineside a route shares between its nights (`LinesideProps.Of`, one per route, dealt from the first line's terrain) is dealt from it, so a town laid on one night's line never moves another's trees (found by `LinesidePropsTests` when `Height` asked the town too). `TownGround.Edges`: a wall along every step past `Stride` (0.7 m: the motor lifts you that far) from the low side to a parapet 1.1 m over the high side, and a rail along every edge of walkable ground on water deeper than `Wade` (0.75 m), merged into runs; `Town.Walls` carries them. `Town.World` and the town's wall boxes stand everything on its level, so the people, doors, fixtures and houses of a terrace or a bench are up on it.
   - **Verticality** (the director, 13 Oct, later: "lacking some of the verticality ... integrating the layout of the town with the layout of the landscape ... always walkable paths for the player ... it doesn't have to be for every single place ... how it fortifies itself"):
     - **The land it stands in** (`TownDistrict.Falloff`, `Outside`; `forms.shapes` `margin`): each form shapes the land past its wall, easing into the plan's land over the margin's outer 40% (`TownGround` `BlendShare`), rolling up and down by `forms.shapes` `roll` (seeded two-octave value noise on 64 m and 23 m lattices, `TownDistrict.Roll`/`RollSeed`; tidewater and glasshouse 6 m, harbour 5, quarry 3, the dykeland's flats none) so a ridge reads as land, not a plateau. The harbour's hill, upland and crag behind the head and the sea under its sea wall; the tidewater's ridge and its tide; the quarry's mesa, its town on the rim and a dry ditch round its wall; the glasshouse in its valley between two ridges; the dykeland on its tidal flats, the flat one. The line comes in on a level embankment through each gate (`Approach`).
     - **Terraced streets** (`forms.shapes` `rise`; `Planner.Blocks`): in the harbour and the tidewater each street band behind the square is a tier `rise` (4 m) over the last, a retaining wall along each back line, the lanes stair streets (`TownWay.Levels`, at most `StairSlope` 0.45 up a metre along); the green stays level with the square.      - **The wall on its ground** (`Fortresses.Footing`, `Fort.Ground`): each bay stands from the lower side of its ground to its height over the higher, on a stone plinth in the art; towers likewise, their guns and lamps raised with them. A town on a rise looks down its wall at the land outside.
     - **Always walked to** (`TownGround.Walk`, `Planner.Walkable`): from the yard, a breadth-first walk over the ground that steps at most a stride (0.7 m), never wades deeper than 0.75 m; every house's front must be reached. Where a lot is cut off the planner lays a stair (3 m wide) up the least step between what's reached and what isn't, and tries again (up to 12 rounds); an island nothing reaches has no houses. `dt town map` says how many stairs it laid and lots it cut off, dropped on a way or a landmark, or off their ground.
     - **The art:** the land past the wall as 96 m chunks on a 6 m grid (`FormLand`, the far ones shadowless), easing into the land's own; the stairs drawn tread by tread (0.3 m risers); rails and parapets along each district's edge and along a stair where it climbs past a stride, not along its level stretches.
   - **The art** (`WorldArt.TownForms`): the town's own ground as one piece a town (`TownGround.Rects`, greedy rectangles of one level: slabs on bodies down to the lowest ground, their sides the terraces' stone or the quarry's rock; decks' planks on piles; the rails and parapets; the ways that aren't streets, a ramp tilted with it), drawn by one transform; the water a sheet over `WaterRects` (a lake's look, tinted for a marsh); the landmarks as the kit's existing pieces (NovaKit's lighthouse, church and fish shed; a wall's tower; the works' headframe and glasshouse; SettingKit's headframe for a derrick).
