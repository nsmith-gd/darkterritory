   - **How a form is laid** (`TownForms.Planner`): in the town's rail frame from its way out (u metres along the line from the exit gate, d across), the square on the left by the way out and the home platform on the right as every town has them. A 1 m grid says what's taken (the wall's walk margin, the line, the square, ways, works, houses), so nothing laid later stands on anything before. A form gives: its wall's outline (its two gates where it crosses the line); its districts (`TownDistrict`: quarter, water, deck, terrace, plots, yard; outline, level over the yard's, depth for water; each clipped to the wall); its ways (`TownWay`: quays, boardwalks, ramps with a level a point, causeways); the walled town's streets and lanes where it has them (`TownStreet`, `TownLane`, so the art and what's underfoot read them as before); its green and its works (`TownGreen`, `TownWorks`); its landmarks (fixtures: lighthouse, beacon, chapel, watchtower, lift, crane, boatshed, seedhouse, glasshouse, with their words); and its lots (`TownForms.Lot`: middle, side, frontage, turn so its front faces its way, the yard's edge and back where it's square to the line, its level). Every lot is checked against the finished ground: dropped where a later district flooded or raised it, or a way runs through it. The households, open houses (the first lots square to the line), yards, people and words are the walled town's (`TownGenerator.Houses` takes the form's lots); the drowned lots are only ever the lost's.
