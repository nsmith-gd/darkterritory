236. **The game at half speed under the opera (GDD v1.4 App. E.6 "Game audio": "Everything else plays at half speed through a 1.2 kHz low-pass. The train bed stops on the derail tick").** Note 174 did the low-pass and left the half speed: the mixer had no time-stretch.
    - **Reading:** half speed as a tape plays it, not a time-stretch: everything on the low-passed tiers (1, 3-6) runs at `mix.json music.gameRate` (0.5) of its speed, so its pitch drops an octave and its envelopes, tremolo, decays and takes last twice as long. It's the cheap, exact version (no new DSP: a voice's clock, oscillators, filters and take reads are scaled by its `SoundInstance.Rate`) and the one that sounds like the world winding down under the punchline. Voices (tier 2) keep their speed with their clarity (note 174's reading), and a stream or a clip (the radio, the music itself) keeps its own.
    - The rate glides with the low-pass's fade (`Mixer.GameRate`: 1 dry, `gameRate` fully muffled), so it slurs down over the 0.25 s fade-in and back up after, instead of jumping an octave.
    - Only the sound slows: the game's clock doesn't. A held loop follows its state as before; a one-shot played under the opera lasts twice as long (a crash's tail rings on under the music).
    - "The train bed stops on the derail tick": the bed already does (note 193: chuff, wheels, brakes, airflow and labour all end on `World.Derailed`; the wreck's own grind and crashes take over, note 233).
    - Tests: `MusicBusTests.WhileMusicPlaysTheGameRunsAtHalfSpeedAndComesBackButVoicesKeepTheirs` (a 440 Hz bed at 220 Hz under the music and 440 after; a voice at 440 throughout; a 0.2 s one-shot lasting 0.4 s).
