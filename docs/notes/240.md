240. **The yard on the radio has a voice (note 178's "no speech in the stack"; the audio checklist's voice-clerk, the last line at L0).** The manifest and the tally were a buzz through a vowel's formants under the typed card. Now they're said.
    - **A word bank, not a script.** The lines are made from the night's own numbers and the crew's names (`Sim.Run.Radio`), so nothing could be recorded whole. `tools/audio/clerk.py` reads the clerk's vocabulary, each phrase the lines hold ("body not recovered", "powder and shot") and each word (numbers as words, the cargoes, the bots' names), as a take of its own into `content/audio/samples/voice-clerk/bank`, with each one's length in `bank.json`. `ClerkVoice` strings a line together as it comes on: numbers said as words ("two thousand four hundred fifty", "minus three hundred fifty"), the longest phrase the bank has taken first, a beat at a full stop, a shorter one at a colon. The result is one clip, played as `radio-clerk-voice` (tier 2, through the set as `voice-radio` is), over `radio-clerk`, now the open channel's static alone.
    - **The voice:** a stand-in until one's recorded, Piper's LibriTTS model (CC BY 4.0, "LibriTTS, Zen et al. 2019"), the same as the prisoners' (note 179). Speaker 385 was picked from a survey of the model's men for the least pitch movement over a clerk's line: low and even, read with the expression taken out (low noise scales). It's none of the prisoners' voices. Two more candidates are on the checklist for the director, and `clerk.py --speaker N` rebuilds the bank in the one kept. Names the phonemizer gets wrong are respelled (`SAY`: "Achebe" had come out "Aitch-b").
    - **Reading:** a name a player typed isn't in the bank and can't be. The set breaks up over it, a burst of hiss and crackle at about a syllable's pace, as loud as the voice, so the shape of a name comes through and the name doesn't. The card still shows it. The alternative, speech made in the game (a TTS engine and model shipped, tens of MB), is the director's call.
    - **At the voice's pace.** A line said takes longer than `radio.lineSeconds` (1.6 s), so with a voice each line's turn is its length said and `radio.pauseSeconds` (0.5) after, never less than lineSeconds (`Radio.Times`; `Reading` and `Length` take the times). The card is typed in step with the voice, and the end screen waits for the last word: a tally runs about half a minute. The voice is presentation, so each client times its own (`NetPlaySession.RadioPace`, set by the app from `GameAudio.Clerk`). With no bank (a mod without it) it's the card at the old pace, over static.
    - `dt audio clerk [--line "..."]` renders a manifest and a tally (or one line) at the voice's pace to a WAV and spectrogram, with each line's turn and what broke up. Tests: `ClerkVoiceTests` (numbers as words; a bot crew's manifest and tally said with nothing breaking up, so a new word in `Radio` fails until the bank has it; a typed name breaks up; each line said once as it comes on, well over the static), `RadioReadingTests.SpokenEachLineTakesAsLongAsItsSaidThenThePause`.
    - Also: the prisoners' and the Soot Children's provenance in `samples/index.json` said CC0, though they're LibriTTS (CC BY 4.0). It now says so, and `install.py` writes it that way. A full install leaves the clerk's bank (`KEEP_SAMPLES`).
