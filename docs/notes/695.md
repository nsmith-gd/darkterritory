695. **A top-to-bottom performance audit (A1, queue #427; the director, 13 Oct evening, `docs/playtest/2026-10-13-evening.md` lines 1–3).**
"We're definitely running into some sort of performance problem here. So I think we're going to need a performance audit
of everything"; "now that we're getting back to the town, things are starting to stutter again"; "I'm getting a frame
drop when the UI pops up" (the talk prompt near townsfolk); "I'm also getting frame drops when I'm rapidly turning". The
director, 13 Oct, on main: "things were pretty small once we left the towns, but the towns feel like they're just
crawling ... really bad performance drops in there." The director asked A1 for the whole audit, top to bottom.

   - **How it's measured.** The app runs headless in the town (`xvfb-run ... DarkTerritory.App -- --route frontier:7 --internal 320x180 --window 320x180 --quit-after 45 --mute`). Note 691's report gives each part of the frame its ms and MB, the scene's own parts among them, with the counts on its slow-frame lines. On this machine the render runs in software (lavapipe) and its time means nothing. The CPU's parts are the game's own code, and on any machine they cost the same proportion of the frame.
   - **Found 1, the town crawl (lines 1 and 3):**
     - Standing in frontier:7's first town, every frame allocated 10.8 MB. 10.1 MB of it was `scene: town houses`, which took 8 ms. The slow-frame lines said "house meshes built 27, meshes uploaded 27", every frame.
     - Why: `WorldArt.Houses` kept one cache of house meshes and let it go whenever a different town's houses were asked for. The terminus's town (note 600) is asked for its houses every frame, kilometres off or not, so the two towns emptied each other's cache every frame. The near houses (built to their designs, each its own mesh) were built again and uploaded on every frame in a town. On the line no houses are near, so nothing was rebuilt: "things were pretty small once we left the towns".
     - At 60 fps that's 600 MB a second of garbage, and the collector's pauses on top of it are the stutters.
     - **Mended:** each town keeps its own houses (`WorldArt.TownHouses`, the last `TownsKept` = 3 towns), and its blocks with them.
     - **After:** 0.6 MB a frame (0.4 MB of it this machine's 8 sim ticks a frame), the houses' part under 0.5 ms, hud/upload 8 ms down to 3.5 ms.
     - **Held by** `FogLoadingTests.ATownsHousesAreBuiltOnceNotEveryFrame`: frontier:7's first town with its terminus town, from the square and the houses, builds its houses once. It fails on the old cache.

   - **Found 2, code compiled on the spot (line 1's "a frame drop when the UI pops up", line 3's loading drops):**
     - A build wasn't ReadyToRun, so the first time any code ran in a night (the first prompt or card, the first creature, the first menu), .NET compiled it right then, in that frame. Each frame now counts the main thread's compiling (`JitInfo`, "jit N ms" with the methods), on its hitch and its slow-frame line.
     - frontier:7's start, from a release build, compiled 819 ms on the first frame (3,948 methods) and 204 ms on the second.
     - The prompt and card themselves cost little: next to a townsperson the HUD, the prompt and the talk take 0.5–0.7 ms and 19 KB, the same as anywhere. A first one compiled its code, and in a town the frame also rebuilt the houses (found 1).
     - **Mended:** `tools/package.sh` publishes ReadyToRun. The same start compiles 154 ms (1,489 methods) on the first frame and 1 ms on the second. That's 16 MB more on a 500 MB build. A precompiled dev build passes `dt build check --dev`.
   - **The collector's mode:** the app plays in `GCLatencyMode.SustainedLowLatency`, so no blocking full collection unless memory runs short. The background collector works alongside the frames.

   - **Found 3, built again every frame what never changes (the director, 13 Oct: "it doesn't really make sense that we're ... rebuilding things in real time, frame by frame ... I would imagine more that we are loading in assets and unloading assets"):**
     - **Measured** (frontier:7, `GreyboxScene.Build`, this machine's CPU). About 97% of a frame's triangles were already models cooked once and drawn where they are: the line's cells (loaded on workers as they come into the fog, let go behind), the towns' houses (near, far and block forms), the cars' bodies, the figures (posed on the GPU). Standing 60 frames, no new model.
     - The rest was rebuilt each frame from scratch and uploaded again: 14,000–17,000 triangles (3.5 MB a frame), 7.6–10.7 ms of the scene's build. Of that, things that never change:
       - the cab's run map, its every dot and letter a box (4,400 triangles, 1.9 ms);
       - the art pass's branch track (a facility's spurs, a dead line), each sleeper asked where it was every frame (5,000 triangles, 3.2 ms);
       - what the townsfolk wear, copied onto each near figure (5,000–7,000 triangles);
       - the cab's levers and tools, cooked pieces copied in (1,500 triangles).
     - **Mended:**
       - The run map's sheet is cooked once for the line and the engine, in the cab's frame, and drawn by the cab's transform. Only the red mark of where the train is is drawn each frame.
       - A branch's bed and rails, and its sleepers, are cooked in 100 m pieces (`WorldArt.BranchPiece`) and drawn where they are, the sleepers within 150 m as before. They're let go with the line.
       - `CreatureArt.Wear` draws a worn piece where it is, always (it was only for far figures, note 479).
       - The cab's controls are drawn where they are.
     - **After:** standing in the town square, by the spurs on a roof, or in the cab, 5,400–6,100 triangles are built again a frame. 2,900 of those are a Stoker's soot, there only while one's in the fire. The rest is what moves by the frame: the folk's hoses, the flames, the crew and creatures' bits.
     - **Held by** `RetainedSceneTests`: those four places build no model frame after frame (`MeshAsset.MadeOnThisThread`, counted per thread), and at most `perf.json`'s new `maxRebuiltTriangles` (8,000), tight enough to catch any of these coming back. The run map, the spurs, the town and a townsperson were drawn and compared with main's: the same to the eye (at most 14 of 255 in a channel, from where the float rounding falls).
     - The report counts what's built on a frame: "house meshes built", "cab maps built", "branch pieces built", and "meshes uploaded".
     - **Not yet:** a Stoker's soot (`Effects.SootFall`, 2,900 triangles while it's there), and 60-odd other `mesh.Append` copies of cooked pieces into the frame in the art (the depots' set pieces, some creature parts). They're under the budget in these places. Each can go the cab controls' way when a report shows it.

   - **Found 4, the town form's water scanned every frame (queue #427 step 3, n1-w2, 18 Oct):**
     - **Measured** (`dt perf --route local:5 --town square,houses --frames 12 --only pc`, Release, this machine's CPU): the scene's build took 49–78 ms in the town, 42–54 ms of it `town form`. The report's one lap "town square, green, works" is now five (town square, green, works, rail yard, form), which named it.
     - Why: `WorldArt.Forms` asked `TownGround.WaterRects()` every frame, and it allocated and scanned the whole ground grid each time. The ground never changes.
     - **Mended:** `TownGround.WaterRects` is worked out once per ground. **After:** `town form` 1.5 ms, the build 12 ms (square) and 11.8 ms (houses).
     - **Held by** `TownFormTests.ATownsWaterRectanglesAreWorkedOutOnceNotEveryFrame`.
     - **Found 5, the chimneys' places found again every frame:** `WorldArt.Chimneys` took a line sample and rebuilt every lived-in house's chimney positions each frame. They're found once a town now (`_chimneys`; a town's plan and houses never change). The "town smoke" lap: 1.6 ms → 0.21 ms (square), 2.4 ms → 0.22 ms (houses). (That run's whole build was also lighter on this machine, so only the lap's own change counts.) `TownSmokeTests` holds the smoke itself.
     - **Still open:** the next parts by cost in a town are the cars (1–3 ms), the creatures (1–2 ms), the townsfolk (2 ms) and the effects (1.8 ms); line 2 (turning fast) waits on the director's next report.
