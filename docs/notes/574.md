574. **The controls belong to whoever's working them (queue #304, D1; the director, 9 Oct 2026, on the short-nights test build: "if a player goes to the cab to drive the bot in there should find another task to do or position to take and shouldnt interfere with the player driving", then "yes the bot was still fighting me for the controls"; and note 16 of their in-game notes: they jumped off at speed, and no bot went back to the cab).**
   - **Why the bot fought.** A bot only sees the controls, not anyone's hands on them. At a stand the host clears the brake and re-applies whoever's holding it in the same tick: someone playing let the brake off (a notch up) while the bot driver held it, and the brake simply stayed on. Nothing the bot could see changed. The first try (a player stood at the driver's stand, by the brake valve, hands it over) missed the director, who drove from elsewhere in the cab.
   - **Now the host decides** (`HostSession.HoldTheControls`). Someone alive in the cab who works a control (a notch of the regulator, the brake, the reverser: `CabControls.Works`) takes the controls; while they hold them, nobody else's hand on them counts. Someone playing takes them off a bot; a bot takes them up only when nobody alive in the cab holds them. Out of the cab or dead, the holder lets them go. Someone in the cab who touches nothing (warming up) takes nothing.
   - **Who's a bot**: a bot's Hello says so (a flags byte after the outfit, bit 0; `ClientSession.Bot`, set by the harness and the app's bot crew). **Who holds them** rides on the controls record (`World.ControlsHolder`, `ControlsHolderBot`), and a client predicts with the same rule: working the controls it takes them if nobody holds them or (playing) off a bot; held by someone else, its own hand doesn't count. Protocol 49.
   - **The bot driver** (`ConductorBot.HandedOver`): while someone playing holds the controls it still calls itself the crew's driver (so no walker comes forward to relieve it, note 399), sends nothing on the regulator, brake or reverser, and goes out the back of the engine to work the train on a walker's legs (the roofs, the fires, the cold, the six's heeds); with steam driving the fire is the speed (T97), so it leaves the shovel too. The moment nobody playing holds them (they've left the cab, or died), it comes back over the roofs and down the hatch, as the relief driver does, and drives.
