260. **The line's own kills are telegraphed: a roof warning before a tunnel's mouth and a bend taken too fast (T115 playtest, "random death walking outside"; GDD App. A.1's fairness contract).** `Lineside.Hazards` struck anyone stood on a roof at a tunnel's mouth (`Struck`, 200) and threw roof riders off a posted stretch taken `throwOver` too fast (`Thrown`). The only warning was a board the headlamp had to find (lamps down, the mouth itself 10 m short), and the HUD said what happened only on the death screen. App. A.1: "TELEGRAPH always precedes COMMIT", and the line's kills aren't whole-train events.
    - **The warning** (`Lineside.Warns`, `Warning`; tuned in `sight.json` `roofWarning`). A board's stretch is warned of once it's within `leadSeconds` (10) of the leading car at the speed now, plus `leadMargin` (30 m, a train gathering speed), with speeds under `minSpeed` (3 m/s) counted as that, until the last car's through. Every tunnel is warned of. A posted stretch (a sharp bend or a weak bridge) is warned of only with the train within `curveHeadroom` (1.5 m/s) of throwing riders off it (`ThrowsAbove`: `throwOver` over the board, more with handrails). It comes from the route and the train alone, **lamp or no lamp**: running dark costs the driver the boards (note 71), not the roof riders their warning. Every machine works it out the same, so nothing new is sent. **No wire change; protocol stays 28.** Prediction is untouched: the hazards are still host-side, and the warning's counter only gates them.
    - **No commit without it.** `See` counts each board's warning ticks on every machine. A hazard takes nobody until its warning has been up a full `leadSeconds` (`WarnedInTime`; counted before the step, so one tick more than the HUD shows). Otherwise the rider is spared (`Lineside.Spared`, which should be zero). The fairness contract is held by construction, not just by tuning: a train surging over a board, or reversing into a mouth, can't kill without the warning.
    - **Why 10 s:** App. A.1's reaction window at its longest (4 s), plus about 6 s to walk from mid-roof to an end ladder (14 m car, `walk` 2.4 m/s) and climb down it (`ladderClimb` 1.6 m/s).
    - **Who gets it** (`Lineside.For`, `roofOnly`, on): whoever is up top, on a roof or on an end ladder, and not a gun's crew behind its shield (clear of both hazards). Off: everyone aboard, the cab included.
    - **HUD** (`Hud.RoofWarningLines`, in `Alerts`): "LOW CLEARANCE / TUNNEL MOUTH IN 6S: GET OFF THE ROOF" ("IN THE TUNNEL: STAY OFF THE ROOF" once in it), and "TOO FAST FOR THE BEND" (or "BRIDGE", `Sign.Bridge`) / "50 KM/H BOARD IN 6S: GET OFF THE ROOF", flashing red and amber.
    - **By ear** (`GameAudio.Lineside.cs`): two new tier-1 sounds, each a synth definition as every tell started. They play as the warning goes up and every `repeatSeconds` (4) while it's up, to whoever it's for. **`warn-low-clearance`**, the telltales: a run of cord slaps over the roof just ahead, 4–7 kHz (the railways hung cords before low bridges and tunnels so a brakeman felt them before the portal). **`warn-curve`**, the roof irons chattering, rocking in and out, at your feet, 2.5–4.5 kHz. Both are in spec A.4's table, told from the tells sharing their bands by rhythm, and in `AudioBench.TellBands` (every roof must hear them, the cab needn't). `dt audio render --listener all` (chaos): warn-low-clearance is 28.3 dB over the bed at its worst listener and warn-curve 22.0 dB (the floor is 6). `AudioTests` are green.
    - **Not the whistle, not a shout.** No whistle code exists in the GDD or spec (App. C has the whistle only as a source of loudness). A whistle nobody pulled is the Whistler's tell (App. A.4) and feeds the Choir's meter (C.7). A fireman's shout needs a voice, and a fireman. So the cue is the line's own, needing nobody in the cab.
