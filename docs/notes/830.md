830. **The coupling gap's step up: walked up onto the deck at an angle, not held at the plate's lip (C1, queue #558; the director's Look Review note of 15 Oct).**
The director, on the Look Review (15 Oct, 19:57 UTC), on the coupling gap seen from the engine's rear ladder (`dt screenshot --view rearstep --lantern`): "I dont like that the player doesnt automatically step up over this ledge, it forces me to jump every time which feels bad."

**What's wrong** (found by walking it in the sim)
- The coupler plate is 1.1 m over the rail. The engine's rear deck and the footplate beside the plate (T90) are 1.4 m. The rise is 0.3 m, inside `player.json stepUp` (0.35 m), so the motor steps you up it.
- But on a plate or a roof, T128's edge hold (`PlayerMotor.HoldAtEdge`, note 273) stops a step that would take your feet within `edge.lip` of where the footing ends. It lets you on only if you walk within `edge.stepOffDegrees` (30°) of straight at the edge (note 717's `walkOff`).
- Its test for footing (`Footing`) only counts what's at or below the feet (`TopAt(..., feet + 0.05)`). A deck one step up reads as no footing at all, a drop off the train.
- So walking off the plate onto the engine's deck at 40° off straight, you're held 0.3 m short of it, for good. Only a straight walk at it, or a jump, gets you up.

**What changes**
- `Footing` counts footing up to a step above the feet (`stepUp`), as the motor does, so the lip lets you on to anything you'd step up onto, at any angle. Drops are judged as before.

**Tests** (`FallTests`)
- `OffThePlateAtAnAngleYouStepUpOntoTheEnginesDeck`: off the plate behind a moving engine, walked at it 40° off straight either way, and straight, you're on its deck at 1.4 m (the ±40° cases fail without the change).
- `BesideThePlateOverTheBallastItStillHoldsYou`: walked at the plate's side at a slant, mid-gap, you're held at its lip.
