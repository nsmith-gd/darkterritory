196. **The last of F.3's upgrades: lamp armour, gun cooling, repair kit charges, radio range, the powered switch thrower (spec F.3, A.5; GDD §17, §23).** Five upgrades were bought and saved with an empty `effect` and did nothing: a purchase that does nothing is a bug. Each now goes through `Campaign.Apply` as note 184's did, a tuning value in campaign.json (each citing F.3), so every machine gets the same night from the upgrade list the Welcome already carries.
    - **Lamp armour** (`lampOut` 0.5): the Climbers still smash the forward lamp coming over the tender into the cab (§23 "lights fail", note 183, the only thing that smashes it), but it can be lit again after 22.5 s, not 45. The time was a code default; it's now `enemies.json climbers.lampOutSeconds`.
    - **Gun cooling** (`foul` 0.5): `combat.json guns.foulChance` halved, 2% a shot (5% in the wet). The foul is a hash of the tick and the gun under the chance, so a cooled gun fouls on a subset of the shots a hot one would.
    - **Repair kit charges** (`repair` 0.6): **Reading:** a kit is never used up. Mending the boiler (`CrewActions`, `repairSeconds`) and opening a Holdout's lock (D.7) both leave it in hand; nothing in the sim counts uses, so a charge has nothing to count, and adding charges would mean making the kit consumable, which F.3 doesn't ask and which would make losing kits (Stranded, GDD v1.4) come sooner. Read as a better-stocked kit that works faster: a ruptured boiler is mended in 15 s, not 25. The lock's slower quiet opening is left as D.7 has it (that trade is the point of it).
    - **Radio range** (`radioReach` +150 m, the one additive effect): spec A.5's radio already covers the whole run and "dies in tunnels and mine spurs". **Reading:** the upgrade carries it a distance into them before it dies. `train.json kit.radioReach` (0) is how far in from a tunnel's mouth (`Route.DeepInTunnel`) or from a mine spur's points (`Run.Underground`, along the spur's path) a radio still works; the host's voice routing (`HostSession.ForwardVoice`) passes it to both tests, so it stays host-side and nothing changes on the wire. At 0 both are exactly what they were.
    - **Powered switch thrower** (`switchThrower`, fitted to the cab like the handrails: `train.json composition.switchThrower` and `thrower`): F.3 "removes the ground excursion at junctions". A lever in the cab (`InteractableKind.Points`, the driver's side behind the tool rack, drawn as a red-handled lever when fitted; `dt screenshot --upgrades poweredSwitchThrower --view points`). Held there with Use for route.json's `throwSeconds`, it throws the nearest points within `thrower.reach` (200 m) ahead of the engine's front on the main line (`SwitchStands.PointsAhead`), through the same host path as a stand (`SwitchStands.CrewAct`, `World.SwitchThrows`), so it's intent (Use) and bots could use it the same way. Only with the train at or under `thrower.maxSpeed` (5 m/s, 18 km/h), and the points still won't move with a wheel on them. The HUD says what it'll do: the points' distance and which way, or to slow down, or that none are in reach. Unfitted, the lever's there in the shape but throws nothing and has no prompt.
      - **Reading:** "removes the ground excursion" is nobody stepping down, not taking junctions at speed: a crew still slows for one, as it would to put someone on the ground. The speed limit is a tuning value, not a safety need (the points' own lock already refuses a throw under a wheel).
    - **Not yet:** the driver bot doesn't use the thrower: the stop crew's switch legs (`StopCrew` SetBack/Held/Clear) still send the shunter out. The HUD's thrower prompt has no Game test (`PrototypeSession` takes no upgrades); the art pass has no modelled lever (the greybox draws it). The radio's reach has no HUD cue for where it dies.
    - Protocol unchanged (21): the upgrades travel in the Welcome as before, the new tunings are content (hash-checked), and the lever is a new interactable kind in the engine's shape, which isn't sent. Tuning: `enemies.json climbers.lampOutSeconds` (45, the old code default), `train.json kit.radioReach` (0), `composition.switchThrower` (false) and `thrower`; no spec-pinned number changed. Tests: `UpgradeTests` (each upgrade's tuning, and every upgrade sold does something; the lamp lit again sooner; a cooled gun fouls about half as often; the kit mends faster and stays in hand; the radio heard 60 m inside a tunnel with it and not without, and dead past its reach; down a mine spur the same; the thrower throws the points ahead from the cab fitted, and not unfitted, out of reach, too fast, or over the points). `CampaignTests` no longer expects lamp armour to do nothing.
