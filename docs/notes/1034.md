     - A GRAB's, a PUNISH's or a dead player's bookmark is a clip. Not a derailment's (the film is its clip) or the Stranded outro's.
     - After its still, each machine takes `frames - 1` more frames (10 in all) at `fps` (5 a second).
     - Each frame is from the still's own eye, held where it was and turned on the chest of whom it's of while they're about. A bookmark of nobody keeps its look.
     - The frames are small (`width` × `height`, 192 × 108). The still itself stays 320 × 180 and is what the album keeps.
     - At most `most` (16) clips a night; the rest stay stills.
     - The incident report's thumbnails play each clip round from the still, holding the last frame `hold` (0.8 s). `Still.At(now)` picks the frame by this machine's clock; the HUD's `now` is passed down.
     - Each frame is a render off the screen, as a still is. A grab costs nine more of them over two seconds, and that stall is the price. Off (`on: false`) it's a still, as before.
     - On by default: it's the item. It's a flag for the director to turn off if the stalls show in a playtest.
   - **`dt moments`** (`Dev/Moments/MomentScorer.cs`, `Cli/MomentsCommands.cs`, `tools/moments/scores.json`):
     - It scores a night as the host played it, a tick at a time, from a recording (`--replay night.dtrec`). Without one, it hosts a bot night headless and records it first (`--route frontier:7 --bots 4 --seconds 900`).
     - What it scores:
       - The incident report's facts worth a clip: a death, a rescue, a derailment, a stranding, a car lost, a punish.
       - A grab broken, worth more the longer it held ("a grab broken late").
       - Someone left behind: on foot 60 m from every car with the train going.
     - It prints every moment and the reel's best (`--top 6`, kept 20 s apart) to out/moments/moments.json.
