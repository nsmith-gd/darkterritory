348. **COLOURS: a colourblind-safe palette (F1, UI/UX 3; queue #85; GDD §32 "Accessibility"; the director, 8 Oct 2026).** The HUD's colours that mean something were green (heard, a good ping, a rescue), amber (a heading, a ping going bad, a bend to slow for) and red (dead, a grab, an alarm). To a protanope green and amber are 15 apart in CIELAB, where under 20 reads as one colour: SPEAKING beside a heading, a good ping beside a bad one.
    - **Two palettes in hud.json** (`standard`, `colourblind`; `HudTuning.Standard`, `.Colourblind`, `HudPalette`), chosen by the settings' **COLOURS** (`Settings.Colours`); `Hud`'s Green, Amber and Red are the chosen palette's good, warning and danger. Colourblind is sky blue, yellow and a deeper red: every pair 43 or more apart under protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia (Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes 2009, full severity), and no closer than the standard's with full colour vision.
    - **What isn't in it:** the menus' amber (a selection, not a meaning), the hotbar's lit slot, and what's drawn in the world (the cab map's red limits, the callouts' amber glow). Those are shapes and places as much as colours; they're the polish pass's (#88) to look at.
    - **Verified:** `HudPaletteTests`: the simulation holds the standard palette under 20 for a protanope and the colourblind one at 40 or more for all three; COLOURS chooses what the roster's SPEAKING is drawn in. `dt screenshot --hud --colours colourblind` (`--roster`, `--spectating`, `--bend-warning`), looked at.
