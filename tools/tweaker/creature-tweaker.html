How the numbers work together

  1. Pressure builds through the night: faster on harder tiers, toward the end, in a long quiet, when the crew is loud, with cargo aboard, and with a bigger crew or a longer train (set under "The whole night").
  2. When it's high enough, the director picks one creature. Each that could come right now (some need a stop, a fast train, a bridge, a dark car) is weighted by its How often × its tier weight.
  3. Cost is how long the quiet after it lasts. The pick takes 3 × its cost off the pressure, so after a cost-1 Freight Beetle the next thing comes soon, and after a cost-5 Passenger the night goes quiet for a while. The night also has a budget of cost, but on today's 7 km nights the director spends about 45 of its 360, so the budget never runs out.
  4. Most in a night and Nights it comes take a creature out once it's reached its cap or on the nights it's not about. Its picks go to the others.
  5. Five come their own way, not the director's pick: the Pickers when the train stands at a yard with crates about, and the Lodger, the Householder, the Hollow House and the Hanger in the stops' open houses. They take nothing off the pressure, so they don't move the rest. Their "How often" is their share of the houses: more Lodgers means fewer of the other house creatures.
  6. So every number moves the others. More of one means fewer of the rest. A costlier creature means fewer things all night, for everyone. The grid at the foot shows each creature's effect on every other.

The whole night: dawn, the crew and the train

The grid is what those three make. In each square, the large number is how fast the night builds toward its next creature against a crew of four on eight cars (the outlined square, where the estimates above are measured): ×1.5 is half again as often. Below it is how many can be under a threat at once, and the most on one crewmate. Squares your values change from the game's have an amber edge. More comes on a bigger crew or a longer train, but how many can be about at once also limits it, so the creatures a night rise less than the large number says: on 12 Oct, four bots on 18 cars went from 17 to 26 a night.

Estimates and measurements

The estimate comes from measured nights.

Creatures that can't be about at once are named on their rows and ringed in the grid. The estimate doesn't count how long one keeps the other out, and it can be all night: on 12 Oct's main, five Knotters a night kept the Hotbox out of every night.

Not in the estimate: creatures sent to answer each other (the director's pairs), a creature sent lately coming on less, one threat on each crewmate at a time, and how the creatures change a night's speed and stops. A measurement plays real nights.

With "Nights it comes" set on many creatures, the estimate samples 256 nights of who's about, the same nights for your values and the game's. It shares each night's creatures among those about, so it shows the pile-up, but less than the game does: the creatures others crowded out when everything was about (the Cinder Hounds, the Tippy Toesie) come far more often in the game on a night when few are about than the estimate says. The Measured column shows what really happens.
